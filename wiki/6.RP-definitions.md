# Grade 6 Ratios: the text

The definitions a student can be sent back to. Plain language, each one carried by a
single example that hits it. When a move in [6.RP.md](6.RP.md) says "go back to the
text," this is the text. Voice: [VOICE.md](VOICE.md).

## Ratio

A ratio compares two quantities: how much of one for how much of the other. We write it
2 to 3, or 2:3, and we read it "for every 2 of the first there are 3 of the second." The
standard's own case: for every 2 wings there was 1 beak, a ratio of 2 to 1.

## Part-to-part and part-to-whole

A ratio can compare two parts to each other, or one part to the whole. In a group of 2
cats and 3 dogs:

- cats to dogs is 2 to 3. That is part to part.
- cats to all the pets is 2 to 5. That is part to whole.

Same animals, two different comparisons. Which one a problem is asking for is the thing
to check. (Backs the A.1 move.)

## Rate

A rate is a ratio that compares two quantities measured in different units, like miles
and hours, or dollars and pounds. 150 miles in 3 hours is a rate.

## Unit rate

The unit rate is the amount for one. 150 miles in 3 hours is 50 miles for one hour, so
50 miles per hour. $6 for 3 pounds is $2 for one pound. The standard's case: 3 cups of
flour to 4 cups of sugar is 3/4 cup of flour for each cup of sugar. The units of the
unit rate tell you which way to divide, which is the tell when a rate comes out flipped.
(Backs both A.2 moves.)

## Equivalent ratios and the ratio table

Two ratios are equivalent when you scale both quantities by the same number. 2 to 6
scales to 4 to 12 by multiplying each by 2. Scaling is multiplying, not adding: 4 to 8
came from adding 2 to each, and that changes the ratio. A ratio table sets the
equivalent ratios in a row so the scaling is easy to see. (Backs the A.3 move.)

## Double number line and tape diagram

Two ways to draw a ratio so a student can see it.

- A double number line puts the two quantities on parallel lines that scale together:
  pounds on top, dollars underneath, lined up.
- A tape diagram draws each quantity as equal-sized boxes: 2 boxes of one thing next to
  3 boxes of the other. Good for part-to-part and part-to-whole at once, because you can
  see the parts and the whole in the same picture.

These are the pictures the A.3 move reaches for.
