# Voice

How every entry in this wiki talks to a teacher, and how the teacher talks to a
student. Every misconception page is written to this. If an entry reads like a
textbook or a correction, it is wrong no matter how accurate it is.

## Foundation

Mistakes are expected, respected, and inspected. (Jo Boaler, youcubed.)

- **Expected.** A wrong answer is a normal part of learning. Nothing we say treats
  it as a lapse.
- **Respected.** We hand the reasoning back and trust the student. Their thinking is
  worth respecting.
- **Inspected.** We look into the mistake together. "What is the 6 minus 3
  measuring?" is an inspection, not a correction.

Everything below is downstream of this one idea.

## The moves

- **Diagnose the kind of error first.** A careless slip, a wrong step run correctly,
  and a real gap in a concept each want a different response. Name which one before
  you say anything.
- **Facilitate, do not lecture.** Hand over one precise, concrete question and let the
  student's reasoning carry it. We are a high-trust social facilitator, not a
  mathematician performing the proof.
- **Precise wording, intuitive idea, one concrete case.** Say the exact thing, but say
  it in a way a sixth grader can hold. Anchor it to a single number the student
  already wrote.
- **Reach for an organizing, visual, or refer-to-the-text move before another
  calculation.** Set the problem up with its units and the answer we want. Draw the
  diagram. Go back to the definition. Redoing the arithmetic is the last resort, not
  the first.
- **Hold back and let them find it.** Ask the question, then stop. Do not tell them
  what to notice. Let them notice it.

## What we never do

We do not tell a student their answer is "obvious," that it "doesn't measure
anything," or anything that puts the work of feeling wrong on them. If a result does
not make sense, we ask what it measures, or ask what moves are available, and let them
decide. More reasoning on them, less pronouncement from us. Less intimidating, and it
holds.

## Sources and the curate-and-cite model

Entries are original writing in a human voice. We do not copy tasks, assessments, or
prose from sources that restrict it. We cite and link the real source so a teacher (or
the local search agent) can fetch the current material themselves. Citing is not
redistributing, which keeps us clear even of non-commercial and no-derivatives
sources. Every misconception names the source that grounds it, with its license.
