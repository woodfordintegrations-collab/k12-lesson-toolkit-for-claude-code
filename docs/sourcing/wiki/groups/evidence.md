# evidence

Five pages, and each one appraises a specific claim rather than describing a thing. The page states
the claim, states what was measured, and says which half of the claim survives. Three of the five
appraise a claim **this project made in writing and got wrong**, which is why they are worth the
space: the belief is already in the record and will be cited again unless something contradicts it
at the point of use.

You want this family in one situation above all others: **you are about to write a sentence about
what does not exist.** "There are no documented misconceptions for this standard." "This layer is
not in the public export." "C.7 is the weakest standard in the corpus." "The store has never been
validated." Every one of those is available from an artifact you can honestly read, and every one
of them is either false or unsupported. The census that shows why lives here.

The distinction that makes the family work: a `trap` page tells you an empty result cannot be
trusted; an `evidence` page tells you what the actual population is, so the empty result can be
diagnosed instead of believed. They are two halves of the same defence and neither works alone.

Two rows carry an unresolved disagreement rather than a resolution.
[[evidence-k12-lesson-toolkit-acceptance-record]] holds three artifacts about the same day that do not
agree, and records the disagreement rather than repairing it. This wiki documents systems; it does
not fix them.
