# What misconception research exists for similarity and right-triangle trigonometry

## Summary

The authored misconception set has to be able to prove two things: which reading informed it, and
that the reading was lawful. This page is the per-paper record that lets it. Every row names the
document, the notice **inside that document**, and what the notice permits.

The error it exists to prevent is one inference, and this project's own extract calls it the most
dangerous available on its host: reading *full text available on ERIC* as *available to reuse*.
ERIC's own Copyright Policy says the opposite, verbatim:

> ERIC does not retain copyright to the works indexed in the database and cannot grant permission
> to use indexed works under copyright protection.

The grant is per paper and lives inside the PDF. It is not in ERIC's metadata, not on the record
page, and not implied by a 200. Of **7 ERIC-hosted PDFs opened individually**, 1 carried a CC BY
notice, 2 carried expressly restrictive notices, and **4 carried no notice at all**.

The second error is subtler and this project made it in writing. The twelve-host adjudication
calls Arhin and Hokor 2021 "the single fully reusable research source found across all twelve
hosts". The paper is not on any of the twelve hosts. It is at mathsciteacher.com, reached only
because the ERIC search for it returned zero. The precise claim is **the one reusable on-topic
paper this seven-PDF sweep located**, and the sample size belongs in the same sentence.

## When to reach for it

Reach for it before quoting or paraphrasing any research paper into a deliverable, and before
writing a *sources consulted* line that implies more permission than was granted.

Reach for it when a source is described as "open access", which is an access statement and often
not a licence at all. Two of the four papers this project could not clear are freely downloadable.

Reach for it before citing mathmistakes.org, because the live host serves nothing and every
citation has to point at an archive snapshot: [[source-math-mistakes]].

Do **not** reach for it as a catalogue of student errors. The errors themselves are pedagogical
content and this wiki does not restate them. What is in scope is the provenance: which documents
carry which grant, and in what form.

Do not reach for it as a statement about ERIC as a whole. Seven PDFs measure the sample, not the
population, and the underlying report calls them samples in terms.

## The claim

**C1. Silence is the modal case in the sample.** Of the 7 ERIC-hosted PDFs opened individually on
2026-08-08: 1 carries a Creative Commons Attribution notice, 2 carry expressly restrictive
notices, 4 carry no licence, copyright or rights statement anywhere in the extracted text.
Falsifier: open the same seven and find a different split.

**C2. One on-topic paper in this sweep is cleanly reusable, and it is off ERIC.** Arhin and Hokor
2021 carries an in-PDF Creative Commons Attribution notice, and the version is pinned at the
publisher's own policy page to CC BY 4.0. Falsifier: a version other than 4.0 at the journal, or
an NC, ND or SA term anywhere in its terms.

**C3. The paper the build most wanted grants nothing.** Simsek, Hoyles and Clark-Wilson, on the
additive-strategies similarity misconception, contains no copyright notice and no licence
statement across 13,348 extracted characters. Falsifier: any rights sentence in the PDF, or a
grant on the publisher's record.

**C4. mathmistakes.org carries CC BY 3.0 Unported and the licence is not the binding constraint.**
The grant is real, site-wide and stable across root captures from 2014 to 2026. What blocks reuse
of its images is chain of title over teacher-submitted photographs and student privacy, both of
which a valid CC BY grant leaves untouched. Falsifier: a submission agreement or contributor terms
anywhere on the site.

**What these claims do not say.** They do not say the 4 silent PDFs are restrictive at source;
"silent" means silent in the ERIC copy, and several of those journals may well be CC BY on their
own sites. They do not say this is all the misconception evidence that exists; a later, wider
sweep located a great deal more at a **lower evidence grade**, recorded below. They do not clear
any specific figure, photograph or student work sample.

### The two restrictive notices, verbatim

EJ1184973, HTTP 200, fetched 2026-08-08:

> This article may be used for research, teaching, and private study purposes. Any
> substantial or systematic reproduction, redistribution, reselling, loan, sub-licensing,
> systematic supply, or distribution in any form to anyone is expressly forbidden. Authors
> alone are responsible for the contents of their articles. The journal owns the copyright
> of the articles.

EJ1064122, HTTP 200, fetched 2026-08-08, with the report's own elision marker preserved:

> Copyright © 2015 by Academic Publishing House Researcher All rights reserved.
> [...] WARNING! Article copyright. Copying, reproduction, distribution, republication (in
> whole or in part), or otherwise commercial use of the violation of the author(s) rights
> will be pursued on the basis of Russian and international legislation. Using the
> hyperlinks to the article is not considered a violation of copyright.

That last sentence is the corpus's clearest textual support for a cite-and-link model: linking is
expressly not infringement, said by a rights-holder that forbids everything else. See
[[practice-cite-without-redistributing]].

### The one reusable paper, and where its version actually lives

In-PDF notice, `https://www.mathsciteacher.com/download/analysis-of-high-school-students-errors-in-solving-trigonometry-problems-11076.pdf`,
HTTP 200, 626,803 bytes, verified 2026-08-08:

> Copyright © 2021 by Author/s and Licensed by Modestum. This is an open access article
> distributed under the Creative Commons Attribution License which permits unrestricted
> use, distribution, and reproduction in any medium, provided the original work is properly
> cited.

That sentence names no version and links no deed. The version is pinned only at
`https://www.mathsciteacher.com/home/open-access-policy`, HTTP 200, verbatim:

> Articles are published under the terms of the Creative Commons Attribution License
> (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Everyone can download and read the
> article, as well as share and adapt the articles, even for commercial purposes, without
> requesting consent of the author or the publisher beforehand, if appropriate credit is
> given to the original publication.

Two documents were needed to make one claim. The PDF alone supports "a Creative Commons
Attribution licence of unstated version", which is not a citable grant. See [[license-cc-by]].

### The paper that grants nothing

`https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10115606/3/Clark-Wilson_simsekali_ICME14%20paper.pdf`,
HTTP 200, 476,009 bytes, 4 pages, verified 2026-08-08. 13,348 characters extracted and searched
for "Creative Commons", "licen" and "Copyright": all NOT FOUND. The UCL Discovery record page is
HTTP 403 live with the body "Enable JavaScript and cookies to continue", retried with two browser
user agents, which is a bot challenge and not a dead page. Recovered via a Wayback snapshot whose
only rights sentence is, verbatim:

> Additional information: This version is the author accepted manuscript. For information
> on re-use, please refer to the publisher's terms and conditions.

The report's verdict: no licence granted, Berne default is all rights reserved, cite only, do not
paraphrase and republish. This matters more than its one row suggests, because this is the paper
covering the additive-versus-multiplicative similarity misconception, which the twelve-host
adjudication calls the pedagogically central one for B.4 and B.5. See [[license-unmarked-silence]].

### What a wider sweep added, and at what evidence grade

A later adjudication located misconception evidence well outside the twelve-host frame and lists
several sources as CC BY 4.0, including MalruleLib (arXiv 2601.03217), the Australian Mathematics
Hub planning tools, and the papers it groups as "Arhin & Hokor 2021; Msi et al. 2026; Namlı 2024;
Ngu & Phan 2020". It also names Ohio's per-distractor rationales as a state-validated taxonomy of
error, and CK-12's Trigonometry Teacher's Edition *Common Errors* as CC BY-SA 3.0 US.

That document's own note to page-writers is the reason none of it is promoted into the table
above, and it is reproduced because the caution is the finding:

> **NOTE FOR PAGE-WRITERS.** Not one licence in this table carries a pasted footer with a URL and
> an HTTP status *in this document*. Three carry a stated verification method (IM's footer read by
> four agents; OpenStax via the CMS API; CK-12's front matter). The rest are report-of-a-report and
> do not meet this wiki's evidence floor on their own. Cite them as this project's sweep record,
> not as verified licence findings.

So: the conclusion that misconception prose must be written original **survives**. The implication
that no evidence exists does not. See [[source-ohio-released-items]] and [[concept-curate-and-cite]].

## Gotchas & constraints

**1. Access is not a rights fact, and this host is where the confusion is manufactured.** ERIC
hosts full text under permission it cannot pass on. There is no licence or rights field anywhere
in ERIC metadata: a full field dump of one record returns author, description, id, issn, language,
peerreviewed, publicationdateyear, publicationtype, publisher, subject, title, and nothing else.
Five record pages were fetched and the only occurrence of "copyright" on any of them is the global
footer nav link. See [[trap-access-is-not-a-rights-fact]].

**2. "Silent" means silent in the copy that was opened.** Verification covered the extracted text
layer only. A notice rendered as an image, or living on the journal's own site rather than in the
PDF, would have been missed. Several of those journals may well be CC BY at source, which would
raise the count. What would close it: open each journal's policy page. Until then the four silent
papers are cite-only under the Berne default, not proven restrictive.
See [[license-unmarked-silence]].

**3. The over-claim this page retires.** The twelve-host adjudication's phrase, "the single fully
reusable research source found across all twelve hosts", is imprecise twice: the paper is on none
of the twelve hosts, and a 1-CC-BY / 2-restrictive / 4-silent split is a seven-PDF sample of a
database whose size nobody here measured. Write "the one reusable on-topic paper this seven-PDF sweep
located". Anything stronger is a population claim nobody measured.

**4. Never upgrade a version silently.** mathmistakes.org is CC BY **3.0 Unported**, not 4.0. The
report records that 3.0 lacks 4.0's 30-day cure period for inadvertent violations and handles
attribution and moral rights differently. Arhin and Hokor's PDF names no version at all. A version
is part of the claim.

**5. A valid grant does not clear chain of title.** The mathmistakes corpus is teacher-submitted
photographs of student work. The About page states the model verbatim: *"That student work will be
posted by me, but sent in by you."* No submission agreement, rights transfer or contributor terms
exist anywhere on the site, and no `/terms` page was ever created. Whether the owner had authority
to sublicense submitters' photographs under CC BY is not established by anything on the site. See
[[concept-chain-of-title]].

**6. Student privacy is a separate axis from copyright.** Every post is a photograph of a minor's
handwritten work, some possibly carrying names or identifying marks. No agent opened the images. A
valid CC BY grant does not resolve this. The recorded recommendation is not to reproduce the
photographs at all.

**7. No attribution string is specified for mathmistakes.org.** The site gives a bare CC BY widget
with no requested credit form. The author handle is `mpershan`, Michael Pershan. A citer has to
construct title, author, source URL and licence. See [[practice-assemble-an-attribution-block]].

**8. Cite mathmistakes to Wayback or the reader lands on a stack trace.** A bare
`https://mathmistakes.org/...` link in a published repository resolves to the PHP fatal.

**9. A date discrepancy on the ICME paper, recorded not resolved.** The PDF header reads
"Shanghai, 12th–19th July, 2020"; UCL catalogues it as 2021 with event dates in July 2021. ICME-14
was postponed. Cite the record's year and say which you took.

**10. The central gap survives.** One reusable paper covers trigonometry errors, in the C.6 to C.8
band. Nothing reusable covers the additive-versus-multiplicative similarity misconception, which
is the one that sits under B.4 and B.5. That prose has to be authored.
