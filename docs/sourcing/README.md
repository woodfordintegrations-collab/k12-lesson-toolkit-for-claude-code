# Instructional resource sourcing

A reference on what you may actually do with an open educational resource: cite it, quote it,
or adapt it. Those are three different operations with three different licence requirements,
and most licence trouble in open education comes from treating them as degrees of one thing.

**Start at [`wiki/index.md`](wiki/index.md).** [`wiki/when-to-use.md`](wiki/when-to-use.md)
routes you by situation.

## The two things worth knowing before you use any of it

**A licence is a fact about a moment, not a property of a source.** Every verdict here carries
the date it was checked. Two grants went away inside six months during 2026: Open Middle's
CC BY-NC-SA became all-rights-reserved, and Achieve the Core's blanket CC0 dedication was
withdrawn. In both cases third-party OER indexes kept publishing the old answer afterwards, so
a second-hand licence claim is worth roughly nothing. Re-check the host.

**Licences attach per resource, not per organisation.** The same publisher routinely ships one
document CC BY and the next all-rights-reserved. MARS carries four different regimes across
one site. ERIC grants nothing at all and says so, while "full text available" reads to most
people like permission. Read the artifact, not the homepage.
