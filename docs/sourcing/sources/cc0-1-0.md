## 1 · The deed's structure is different from every licence deed

The six licence deeds in this cluster share a template: `You are free to` / `Under the following
terms` / `Notices`. **This deed has none of those headings.** Its headings are `No Copyright` and
`Other Information`. There is no conditions list at all, because there are no conditions.

## 2 · No Copyright, verbatim

Heading: **No Copyright**

> The person who associated a work with this deed has **dedicated** the work to the public domain by waiving all of his or her rights to the work worldwide under copyright law, including all related and neighboring rights, to the extent allowed by law.

> You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the work, even for commercial purposes, all without asking permission. See **Other Information** below.

The words `dedicated` and `Other Information` are bold in the served page.

Note `to the extent allowed by law`. The waiver is bounded by jurisdiction, which is why the
legal code carries a fallback licence. See section 6.

## 3 · Other Information, verbatim

Heading: **Other Information**

> In no way are the patent or trademark rights of any person affected by CC0, nor are the rights that other persons may have in the work or in how the work is used, such as publicity or privacy rights.

> Unless expressly stated otherwise, the person who associated a work with this deed makes no warranties about the work, and disclaims liability for all uses of the work, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law.

> When using or citing the work, you should not imply endorsement by the author or the affirmer.

Two footnotes attach. Verbatim:

> **publicity or privacy** — The use of a work free of known copyright restrictions may be otherwise regulated or limited. The work or its use may be subject to personal data protection laws, publicity, image, or privacy rights that allow a person to control how their voice, image or likeness is used, or other restrictions or limitations under applicable law.

> **endorsement** — In some jurisdictions, wrongfully implying that an author, publisher or anyone else endorses your use of a work may be unlawful.

The third bullet is the closest thing CC0 has to an obligation, and it is **not** one. It says
`you should not`, not `you must not`, and its footnote grounds it in general law rather than in
CC0. A page must not present it as an attribution requirement.

## 4 · ShareAlike and NoDerivatives

**Neither exists, and neither could.** Measured: the strings `ShareAlike` and `NoDerivatives`
appear 0 times in the deed body and 0 times in the legal code. There is no copyleft and no
restriction on distributing modified versions. `You can copy, modify, distribute and perform the
work` is unconditional.

## 5 · The three answers a page will need

**Commercial use: permitted, explicitly.** Deed, verbatim: `even for commercial purposes, all
without asking permission`. Legal code Section 2, verbatim, waives rights `(iv) for any purpose
whatsoever, including without limitation commercial, advertising or promotional purposes`.

**Indication of changes: not required.** Measured: `changes were made`, `modified the material`
and `previous modifications` each appear 0 times in the CC0 deed and legal code. The legal code's
front matter does address the topic, but as a request rather than a term, verbatim from the
`Considerations for the public` block:

> A licensor may make special requests, such as asking that all changes be marked or described. Although not required by our licenses, you are encouraged to respect those requests where reasonable.

That block also states its own status, verbatim: `The following considerations are for
informational purposes only, are not exhaustive, and do not form part of our licenses.`

**Attribution must contain: nothing. There is no attribution requirement.** This is the single
most important fact on this page and the one most often got wrong. The deed states no attribution
condition; the legal code states none. What remains is the non-endorsement caution quoted in
section 3, which is a caution about implying endorsement, not a duty to credit.

Citing remains good practice and is unconstrained. This wiki's own verdict vocabulary already
holds that citation needs no licence at all, so a CC0 work is cited for scholarly reasons, never
for licence-compliance reasons. Do not write that CC0 "requires attribution".

## 6 · Where the deed differs from the legal code

**The deed says it is not the instrument, and says it more strongly than the licence deeds do.**
Verbatim, under the heading `Notice`:

> The Commons Deed is not a legal instrument. It is simply a handy reference for understanding the CC0 Legal Code, a human-readable expression of some of its key terms. Think of it as the user-friendly interface to the CC0 Legal Code beneath. This Deed itself has no legal value, and its contents do not appear in CC0.

> Creative Commons is not a law firm and does not provide legal services. Distributing, displaying, or linking to this Commons Deed does not create an attorney-client relationship.

> Creative Commons has not verified the copyright status of any work to which CC0 has been applied. CC makes no warranties about any work or its copyright status in any jurisdiction, and disclaims all liability for all uses of any work.

`its contents do not appear in CC0` is stronger than anything on the licence deeds, and the third
paragraph is unique to this page: **CC has not verified that any CC0-marked work was the
affirmer's to dedicate.**

Differences measured in this fetch:

1. **The deed omits the waiver-then-fallback structure entirely.** The deed presents a single
   dedication. The legal code has two operative mechanisms. Section 2, the Waiver, verbatim:

   > To the greatest extent permitted by, but not in contravention of, applicable law, Affirmer hereby overtly, fully, permanently, irrevocably and unconditionally waives, abandons, and surrenders all of Affirmer's Copyright and Related Rights and associated claims and causes of action, whether now known or unknown (including existing as well as future claims and causes of action), in the Work (i) in all territories worldwide, (ii) for the maximum duration provided by applicable law or treaty (including future time extensions), (iii) in any current or future medium and for any number of copies, and (iv) for any purpose whatsoever, including without limitation commercial, advertising or promotional purposes (the "Waiver").

   Section 3, the Public License Fallback, verbatim in its operative part:

   > Should any part of the Waiver for any reason be judged legally invalid or ineffective under applicable law, then the Waiver shall be preserved to the maximum extent permitted taking into account Affirmer's express Statement of Purpose. In addition, to the extent the Waiver is so judged Affirmer hereby grants to each affected person a royalty-free, non transferable, non sublicensable, non exclusive, irrevocable and unconditional license to exercise Affirmer's Copyright and Related Rights in the Work (i) in all territories worldwide, (ii) for the maximum duration provided by applicable law or treaty (including future time extensions), (iii) in any current or future medium and for any number of copies, and (iv) for any purpose whatsoever, including without limitation commercial, advertising or promotional purposes (the "License").

   So where a waiver of copyright is impossible, an unconditional licence applies instead. The
   practical effect is the same for a reuser and the deed's flat "public domain" is a fair
   summary, but the mechanism is not what the deed describes. Note `non transferable`, `non
   sublicensable` and `non exclusive` are unhyphenated in the served bytes.

2. **Moral rights.** The deed does not name them. The legal code Section 1 lists `moral rights
   retained by the original author(s) and/or performer(s)` among the Copyright and Related Rights
   the Waiver reaches, subject to `to the greatest extent permitted by ... applicable law`. In
   jurisdictions where moral rights are inalienable, they survive. The deed gives no hint of this.

3. **Clearing third-party rights.** The legal code Section 4(c) is blunter than the deed,
   verbatim:

   > Affirmer disclaims responsibility for clearing rights of other persons that may apply to the Work or any use thereof, including without limitation any person's Copyright and Related Rights in the Work. Further, Affirmer disclaims responsibility for obtaining any necessary consents, permissions or other rights required for any use of the Work.

   This is the CC0 counterpart of a third-party carve-out. A CC0 mark on a page says nothing
   about embedded images, quoted passages or contributed material inside it. See
   `[[concept-third-party-carve-out]]` and `[[concept-chain-of-title]]`.

4. **Trademark and patent.** Legal code Section 4(a), verbatim: `No trademark or patent rights
   held by Affirmer are waived, abandoned, surrendered, licensed or otherwise affected by this
   document.` The deed says this too, so this one is not a divergence, but it is easy to lose.

5. **CC is not a party.** Legal code Section 4(d), verbatim: `Affirmer understands and
   acknowledges that Creative Commons is not a party to this document and has no duty or
   obligation with respect to this CC0 or use of the Work.`

## 7 · Rider next door to the grant

Deed page site footer, verbatim, with its carve-out in the same sentence and a third-party
attribution immediately after:

> Except where otherwise noted, content on this site is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license. Icons by Font Awesome.

The page documenting CC0 is itself published under **CC BY 4.0**, not CC0. A reuser of this page
owes attribution. Do not read the footer as evidence about the instrument the page documents.

The legal code page separately dedicates the licence texts, verbatim:

> The text of the Creative Commons public licenses is dedicated to the public domain under the CC0 Public Domain Dedication.

followed immediately by the trademark reservation, verbatim:

> Except for the limited purpose of indicating that material is shared under a Creative Commons public license or as otherwise permitted by the Creative Commons policies published at creativecommons.org/policies, Creative Commons does not authorize the use of the trademark "Creative Commons" or any other trademark or logo of Creative Commons without its prior written consent

The licence text may be reproduced freely; the CC name and logo may not. Two facts, not one.
