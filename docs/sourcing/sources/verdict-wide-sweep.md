### The honest savings estimate

- **Direct item reuse: ~10-15% of the bank.** The document's stated arithmetic basis: a 10-lesson
  unit with 3 quizzes, a practice exam and a final exam needs roughly **150-200 items**. IM's
  CC BY 4.0 practice sets, cool-down statements and activity problems across Units 3 and 4, plus
  OpenStax 1e examples, plausibly supply **20-30 items outright** with attribution.
- **Item *design*: essentially solved, ~40% of the authoring cost eliminated.** The document's
  reason: item taxonomies, difficulty ladders and distractor-to-misconception mappings are *facts
  about assessment*, not copyrightable expression, so this does not depend on licence at all.
- **Net:** original expression is still forced for **~85-90%** of items. Total saving on the item
  phase is put at **35-45%**, and the residual work is called the cheap half (writing stems for a
  specified archetype with a specified distractor set) rather than the expensive half (deciding
  what the archetypes and distractors should be).

### The design artefacts obtained without needing permission from anyone

- **A complete C.7 difficulty ladder, 14 rungs**, from bare identity through algebraic
  two-expression through set-up-only meta items through monotonicity coupling to the SAT-level use
  of the identity as an unprompted simplification tool.
- **State-validated distractor sets.** Ohio publishes a rationale for *every* multiple-choice
  distractor naming the specific reasoning error. MCAS 2025 independently arrives at the same
  three-way distractor design for C.7. Two states converging is called strong evidence those are
  the real error modes.
- **A rubric grammar for the two exams**, from the NY Regents rating guide: computational, graphing
  and rounding errors cost 1 credit (capped), **conceptual** errors are penalised harder, and the
  guide's own named example of a conceptual error is, VERBATIM (host): *"choosing the incorrect
  trigonometric function."* Recurring shape: [2] answer+work / [1] work with one computational
  error / [1] work with one conceptual error / [1] answer with no work / [0] insufficient.
- **A second rubric grammar** from AQA: M / A / B / M1dep / A1ft / "oe", with accepted answers given
  as **ranges** (for example `[125.99, 126.572]`), which is how to mark a trig answer without
  punishing legitimate rounding.
- **A five-dimension process rubric with real scored student papers**, from Oregon DOE's "Flagpole
  Spotlight" work sample (Making Sense / Representing and Solving / Communicating Reasoning /
  Accuracy / Reflecting and Evaluating), each score point attached to an actual student response
  with scorer commentary.
- **A coverage-guarantee format** from Dr Frost: every item tagged with a `Categorisation:` line
  naming its *type* plus exact provenance. Their stated aim is "one question of each type seen in
  past papers".
- **A diagnostic-item design rule with a warning** from Eedi: assign each distractor to a named
  misconception, **but reserve one slot per item for a plain arithmetic slip**, because in their
  worked example the most popular wrong answer was a computational slip, not a misconception.
  Assigning all three to concepts systematically over-diagnoses.
- **MalruleLib (CC BY 4.0)** gives *executable* wrong procedures with templates that fire inside
  these standards: `distribute_square_root_over_addition` with a `pythagorean_context` template,
  `additive_instead_of_multiplicative` with a `similar_figures` template, and
  `ratio_as_division_only`.
- **A documented authoring trap**: JMAP Cofunctions 2 #1 records a *defective real Regents item*.
  For sin 6A = cos 9A, VERBATIM (host answer key): "as originally written, distractor (3) was
  A = 54, also a correct response." Cofunction equations with two coefficients admit solutions
  outside the intended acute branch unless the domain is constrained.

### What the item phase gains from JMAP's formats

**JMAP publishes every worksheet in .doc and .tst (ExamView) form alongside the PDF**, and its
per-standard index pages give question counts per archetype. Recorded weights: **B.4's largest
single archetype is the side-splitter theorem (43 items across three graduated worksheets)**, B.5
is proof-dominated, **C.8 is by far the largest bank**, C.7 is compact and highly patterned. The
document calls that a blueprint for how to *weight* the five standards.

### Cross-cutting, and the one the document says should shape scoring

**Errors concentrate at TRANSFORMATION and PROCESS SKILLS, not at reading.** Three independent
studies converge:
- Arhin & Hokor 2021 (Ghana, 90 students): on item 1, **81.1%** reached comprehension, only
  **38.9%** succeeded; of the **64.4%** who reached transformation, **47.8%** erred there.
- Msi, Matsepe & Sehowa 2026 (South Africa): reading errors **0%**, comprehension **30%**, process
  skill **50%**.
- Usman & Hussaini 2017 (Nigeria).

Students understand the situation and still cannot write the mathematical statement. **Implication
as stated: applied items should score setup separately from arithmetic**, which is what Oregon's
five-dimension rubric does, and should not spend items on reading comprehension.

### Which standards still lack documented error evidence

**B.4's proof-construction is the only genuine hole.** There is no released assessment item, no
research study, and no per-distractor rationale anywhere in this sweep documenting how students
fail *when asked to prove Pythagoras via similarity*. What exists is:

- **Teacher testimony, not error data**: Picciotto ("almost no student can follow this proof
  presented passively"), CK-12 Geometry TE (students cannot mentally rotate the sub-triangles).
- **Errors about the theorem's *statement***, not its proof: Kaplinsky's Scarecrow list.
- **Errors about the *configuration***: #41, #43.

Nothing about the proof itself. The document's own instruction: since B.4 is the standard that
explicitly names "prove the Pythagorean Theorem using triangle similarity", the B.4 misconception
entries will be *inferred* from #41 and from teacher testimony, and **should be labelled as such
rather than dressed as documented.**

Second-order gaps: **B.5's formal proof-writing** has evidence only indirectly, via the NY Regents
rating-guide error taxonomy. The model response sets (real scored student work at every credit
level) were confirmed to exist at **HTTP 200** but were never downloaded, because they are scanned
handwriting that pdftotext would fail on. **C.6's "explain that by similarity" demand** is covered
well by CK-12's Teacher's Edition and New Mexico PED, but by no released assessment item.

## 4. THE C.6 TO C.7 GEOMETRIC BRIDGE

**ADJUDICATION.** VERBATIM: "**a distinct minority derive it; the majority assert it. But the
derivations that exist are excellent, plural, and at least three of them are in a geometry (not
precalculus) register**".

**Roughly 12 sources derive; roughly 15 assert or omit.** The split is not random: **assertion
correlates with a trig/precalculus audience and with commercial reference sites; derivation
correlates with geometry courses and with open curricula.**

### The best treatments, ranked by usefulness, with their licence status

**1. IM/Kendall Hunt Geo U4 L8 Activity 8.2, "Twin Triangles", CC BY 4.0.** Called the best
instructional move in the entire sweep, and flagged as such by four agents independently. The class
is split into Column A and Column B and neither knows the other's problems. Column A solves
cos(41°)=7/x, sin(65°)=y/6, sin(50°)=8/z. Column B solves sin(49°)=7/x, cos(25°)=y/6, cos(40°)=8/z.
**The same three triangles, set up from the other acute angle.** Partners compare, find identical
answers row for row, and have to explain why. The conjecture, that the sine of an acute angle
equals the cosine of its complement, is *produced by the class*, not announced. Only then does θ
enter and sin θ = cos(90−θ) get written down. Activity 8.3 "Explain the Co-nnection" then forces
the geometric justification as a group visual display, with a published stall-scaffold: *draw a
right triangle, label one acute angle θ, ask what the other one is, then label the sides with
variables.*

**2. EngageNY/Eureka Geo M2 L27 Example 1, CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 (cite only).** The algebraic route, and
called the most watertight. One right triangle, sides a, b, c. Tabulate sin α, sin β, cos α, cos β
**written as opp/hyp and adj/hyp over those letters**. sin α and cos β are literally the same
fraction on the page. It then names the payoff explicitly, VERBATIM (host): *"the 'co-' prefix in
cosine is a reference to the fact that the cosine of an angle is the sine of its complement."*

**3. The one-sentence mechanism: OpenStax / LibreTexts Precalculus 1e §5.4, CC BY 4.0.**
VERBATIM (host): *"The side opposite the angle of π/3 is also the side adjacent to π/6"*,
generalised on the LibreTexts page as, VERBATIM (host): *"when two angles are complementary, one
angle's opposite side becomes the other angle's adjacent side."* The document calls that the whole
bridge in one clause, under a licence that can be reused, and worth carrying verbatim as the formal
restatement after the discovery arc.

**4. The best *figure* design: GeoGebra "Sine vs Cosine Exploration in A Right Triangle"
(Mulert/Graber, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, cite only).** An agent downloaded the `.ggb` and read
`geogebra.xml`. One text object prints four lines simultaneously:

```
sine A   = c/d
cosine A = e/d
sine C   = e/d
cosine C = c/d
```

**As fractions of named segments, not as decimals.** Rows 1 and 4 are literally the same fraction;
rows 2 and 3 are literally the same fraction. The student sees identity of *expression*, not
numerical coincidence. It has been forked by at least five different teachers.

**5. The complementary second figure: Wolfram Demonstrations, "Cofunction Identities for Sine and
Cosine" (Chris Boucher, 2008, CC BY-NC-SA).** Normalise the hypotenuse to 1. Sine *is* the length
of the opposite side; cosine *is* the length of the adjacent side; the complementary swap becomes a
claim about two segments in one picture rather than an algebraic identity.

**6. Discovery from measured data: NZ Maths *Introducing Trigonometry* Session 2 (CC BY 3.0 NZ,
licence needs confirming).** Students construct right triangles with a **one-metre hypotenuse**
(following Hipparchus), measure the opposite and adjacent sides at 30°, 45°, 60°, 75°, and
tabulate. The stated observation is, VERBATIM (host): *"the values are swapped for 30 and 60
degrees."*

**7. Discovery from the class's own ratio table: IM Geo U4 Lesson 4 activity 4.3, CC BY 4.0.** A
*second, independent* route inside the same unit. Students build a class table of adj/hyp, opp/hyp,
opp/adj for 10° to 80°, then are asked to fill the 35° row using the **55°** row and to notice that
the 20° and 70° rows contain the same numbers rearranged. **The table is the figure.**

**8. Concrete-then-general: MathBitsNotebook (cite only, hostile licence).** A 22°/68° triangle
worked fully numerically from *both* angle perspectives, tabulated, the equality noticed, then
m∠B = 90 − m∠A substituted, then generalised to θ. Also the only source that states the guardrail
explicitly.

**9. Complementarity from the construction: Wikibooks (CC BY-SA 3.0, cite only).** Build the right
triangle via **Thales' theorem** (a point on a circle sees the diameter at a right angle), then the
second acute angle falls out as π − π/2 − θ = π/2 − θ, VERBATIM (host): *"the complement of angle
θ."*

**10. The algebraic alternative, for extension: Cuemath.** sin(π/2 − θ) = sin(π/2)cos θ −
cos(π/2)sin θ = cos θ.

Also: **Corral, Theorem 1.2** gives the cleanest statement (GFDL); **IM Task 1443**'s teacher
commentary insists, VERBATIM (host), *"the picture is vital"*; and **Story of Mathematics** runs a
complete side-ratio derivation with 4 worked examples and 6 practice questions.

### Who asserts instead

GeeksforGeeks (states the identity, two token examples, **no derivation**); Pearson Channels
(states the theorem, then drills); Calcworkshop (mnemonics only); Third Space Learning and
MathsGenie (the anti-derivation: the cosine row taught as "the same pattern backwards" from the
sine row, a memory trick about a *table*, never a statement about *angles*); Siyavula (names
similarity without any investigation to back it); the current NCERT (section deleted); and
Purplemath and Math is Fun, which **omit cofunctions from their identity round-ups entirely**,
using "co-" only in the *reciprocal* sense (secant with cosine).

Also named: **Math Open Reference's sine.html displays sin A and sin C computed side by side in one
figure and never cashes it in.** The scaffolding is on the page; the observation is never made.

### Two documented orderings, both defensible

- **NESA (NSW) MA5-TRG-P-02** puts **exact values first**, VERBATIM (host): "Derive and apply the
  exact sine, cosine and tangent ratios for 30°, 45° and 60°" from the half-equilateral (1, √3, 2)
  and the 45° isosceles (1, 1, √2), *then*, VERBATIM (host): "Verify and use the relationships
  between the sine and cosine ratios of complementary angles: sin A = cos(90° − A) and
  cos A = sin(90° − A)." Rationale: the 30/60 pair makes the swap concretely visible in numbers the
  student just derived.
- **EngageNY M2 L27 runs it the other way inside one lesson**: derive C.7 first, then use it as the
  *reason* the special-angle table is memorable, because the cosine row is the sine row reversed
  precisely as (0,90), (30,60) and (45,45) are complementary pairs. It supplies the memory device
  √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2.

Either works. EngageNY's is called the tighter lesson; NESA's the gentler ramp.

### The guardrail every derivation must carry

**Tangent does not follow the rule.** tan θ and tan(90−θ) are **reciprocals**, not equal. Students
who internalise C.7 as "swap the ratio" over-generalise. Three sources handle it deliberately:
MathBitsNotebook flags it explicitly; K12 LibreTexts 2.1.5 runs the tangent case in Examples 3-4
*immediately after* the sine/cosine case as contrast-by-design; IM U4 L8's "Are You Ready for More?"
makes students **conjecture and prove** tan θ · tan(90−θ) = 1.

### The synthesis question named as worth stealing outright

IM U4 L8's lesson close, VERBATIM (host): *find θ for which sin θ < cos θ, sin θ = cos θ,
sin θ > cos θ.* Answers: θ < 45, θ = 45, θ > 45. Justifiable **two ways**: algebraically
(sin θ = cos(90−θ), so sin = cos forces θ = 90 − θ, hence θ = 45, "the angle complementary to
itself") and visually (a small angle has a short opposite leg). IM Task 1443 part 2 asks the same
question and ships two independent solutions: the isosceles argument, and the monotonicity argument
(sine increasing, cosine decreasing across 0 to 90, so they cross exactly once).

## 6. WHAT THE WIDE SWEEP CHANGES

**ADJUDICATION.** This is the section a page most often needs, and it is where the retirements are
stated. Items 1, 2 and 3 below retire claims carried in `verdict-twelve-host-table.md` (§5 there,
and the framing of its row 6). Item 4 retires a folk claim that document does not make. A page
citing either extract must carry the correction from the other.

### Flatly wrong

**1. "C.7 has near-zero coverage."** 37 sources, 24 usable treatments, 6 complete lessons, 45
answer-keyed items in two files, one CC BY 4.0 lesson found independently by four agents. **Retire
the claim and rewrite the page** (§1).

**2. "No host supplies a clean adaptable bank of assessment items."** False twice.
(a) JMAP supplies a standard-by-standard, answer-keyed, **provenance-tagged** bank across all five
standards, in `.doc` and `.tst` as well as PDF; cite-only, but a complete blueprint including
relative weights. (b) IM/Kendall Hunt supplies **openly licensed** practice sets, cool-down
statements and activity problems across Units 3 and 4.

**3. IM is not NC-SA.** The task bank is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; the **curriculum is CC BY 4.0**. These
are two properties of one organisation under two licences. VERBATIM: "I think this is the precise
error that caused the earlier sweep to write IM off, and it cost us the single most useful source
in the field."

**4. "OpenStax is CC BY 4.0."** Only first editions. Every 2e maths title checked (Alg&Trig 2e,
Precalculus 2e, College Algebra 2e, Prealgebra 2e, Elementary Algebra 2e, Intermediate Algebra 2e)
plus Contemporary Mathematics is **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**. The newer edition is the *more* restricted
one, which is counter-intuitive. Verify per-book at

```
openstax.org/apps/cms/api/v2/pages/?type=books.Book&slug=<slug>&fields=license_name,license_version
```

never from memory, and never from the Open Textbook Library, whose metadata is stale and *wrong* on
exactly this book.

**5. "Twelve curated OER hosts" is a sampling frame, not the internet.** Every C.7 source that
matters sits outside it: state assessment archives, a national syllabus (NSW/NESA), a federal
curriculum module (EngageNY), a peer-reviewed literature, and one commercial-publisher-hosted open
curriculum (Kendall Hunt). None is an "OER host".

### Overstated

**6. Licensing as a blocker.** Reading is unconstrained. Item taxonomies, difficulty ladders,
distractor-to-misconception mappings, rubric grammars and misconception catalogues are **facts about
assessment and about students**, not copyrightable expression. The constraint bites on the *prose*
of about five sources. It does not bite on the design work, which is the expensive half.

**7. The C.7 authoring burden.** C.7 is not written from nothing. IM Geo U4 L8 alone gives a
complete derive-level lesson under CC BY 4.0, and it can be paired with IM L4.3 as an independent
second route, both reusable with attribution.

### Understated

**8. C.7 is genuinely hard, with numbers.** Florida BEST 2025 publishes percent-correct: **40%** on
the cofunction item, **36%** and **31%** on the other two trig items, against a whole-test range of
**~21-81%**. Right-triangle trig sits well below the test median. **No other source in the sweep
publishes difficulty data.**

**9. The real content gap is B.4, not C.7.** *No current national textbook opened anywhere in this
sweep does Pythagoras-by-similarity in the lesson that introduces the theorem.* NCERT removed it in
the 2023 rationalisation, and the chapter intro still *promises* "a simple proof of Pythagoras
Theorem". CK-12's own §4.27 "The Pythagorean Theorem" states the theorem and its converse, gives
five examples, and **never proves it**; the similarity proof lives three chapters later at §7.14.
Mathigon's three proofs are dissection/algebraic. Wikibooks offers rearrangement and an outline of
Euclid. **A unit that follows any of these books' organisation would satisfy the computational
demand and silently miss B.4 entirely.** The clean skeletons that do exist: K12 LibreTexts 7.14
(drop the altitude, three similar triangles, a/c = (c−y)/a and b/c = y/b, cross-multiply and add);
IM Geo U3 L13 (tracing the triangles apart, then hunting the pattern inductively); Picciotto's
*The Three Triangles* (copy, redraw separately, AA, numerical case a=4 b=5, then generalise).

**10. There is no AIM-approved high-school geometry textbook at all.** The entire vetted open
universe for these topics is Yoshiwara, Stitz-Zeager, Collingwood et al., and, for geometry, only
Hitchman's *Geometry with an Introduction to Cosmic Topology*, which is undergraduate and
irrelevant. **That, not C.7, is called the structural gap in the OER landscape.**

**11. C.6's similarity argument is skipped even by good textbooks.** OpenStax Alg&Trig 7.2 uses the
word "similar" **twice in the entire section** and never argues from similarity that the ratio
depends only on the angle. It states the ratios directly. The only textbook that does C.6 the way
the standard demands is **Corral**: an American student measures a right triangle in inches, a
German student measures a similar one in centimetres, the triangles are similar so
BC/AB = B′C′/A′B′, therefore sin A = sin A′, VERBATIM (host): *"you may use ANY right triangle which
has A as one of the angles."* **C.6 must be authored deliberately, not adapted.**

**12. NAEP is public domain and was never reached.** IES policy, VERBATIM (host),
nces.ed.gov/help/webuse: *"all information on the U.S. Department of Education's IES website … is
in the public domain and may be reproduced, published, linked to, or otherwise used without IES'
permission."* NAEP released items are the **only** genuinely CC BY 4.0-compatible released-assessment
source found. The NAEP Questions Tool serves an SPA shell to curl and to WebFetch; **zero items were
opened.** It reportedly carries scorer comments on real student constructed responses plus
percent-correct data. Called **the single highest-value remaining action in the whole sweep**, and a
browser-rendering problem rather than a licensing one.

**13. Assessment-design artifacts nobody was hunting for turned up.** The NY Regents rubric grammar
with its conceptual/computational distinction; AQA's M/A/B/M1dep/A1ft/"oe" marking grammar with
answer *ranges*; Oregon's five-dimension process rubric with scored student papers; Ohio's
per-distractor rationales; Dr Frost's per-item `Categorisation:` coverage guarantee.

**14. Van Dooren's overcorrection result is a design constraint.** Their teaching experiment
successfully broke the illusion of linearity (unwarranted linear answers on non-proportional items
fell to **~18%**), **and students overcorrected**, applying non-linear strategies to genuinely
linear items, rising from **13%** at pretest to **36%** at post *and* retention.

**15. The picture-first sequencing has an experiment behind it.** Usman & Hussaini 2017: 80
students, the same seven problems, two methods. Drawing the right triangle produced **238**
process-skill errors; the formula route produced **303**. Namlı 2024 adds that students who redrew
their own diagram were more likely to reach for angle-side relationships than for Pythagoras.

**16. Delaying the calculator is a convergent design choice, not a quirk.** Math Medic (calculator
on day 4 of 4), Jon Orr (predict-the-ratio theatre before naming tangent), Jo Morgan (printed tables
so "sin 30 = ½" reads as "opposite : hypotenuse is 1 : 2"), and Weber (2008, *Mathematics Teacher*
102(2)), who supplies the reason: trigonometric functions are **among the first functions a student
meets that cannot be evaluated by performing arithmetic.**

**17. Dead ends now recorded, so nobody re-walks them.**
- **Texas:** spring 2013 was the final STAAR Geometry EOC, and TEA no longer publishes PDF released
  tests at all. Any Texas geometry item in the wild is pre-2014 TEKS.
- **Virginia:** the pre-2012 SOL archive predates the CCSS right-triangle-trig standards entirely
  (zero sin/cos/tan items in the 2010 release).
- **EQAO:** a dead end. Ontario's similarity and trig sit in Grade 10 MPM2D, which has no provincial
  assessment.
- **Kate Nowak's f(t) origin post** is 404 and **web.archive.org is refused from this environment.**
- **mathematicsvisionproject.org fails TLS handshake from this machine (reproduced three ways)**;
  the document's advice is to reach MVP through the Open Up HS Math NC port instead.

> **NOTE, two tensions worth carrying.** (a) That last recommendation points at Open Up HS Math NC,
> which this same document's open-questions list marks **"Do not assume CC BY 4.0"** because its
> licensing FAQ returned 403. The recommendation and the caveat are both reproduced as written and
> are not reconciled here. (b) "web.archive.org is refused from this environment" is stated of the
> wide-sweep agents' environment; the twelve-host sweep used Wayback successfully and repeatedly.
> Both are preserved. This is a per-environment reachability fact, not a claim about the archive.

**18. The UK banks are silent on C.7 by *specification*.** AQA GCSE 8300 rows G19-G22 read verbatim:
G20 is Pythagoras plus the three ratios, G21 is bare recall of exact values, and there is **no
complementary-angle content anywhere in the spec**. Same absence at national level in Australia
(ACARA v9), Ontario, and Singapore. **Only NSW names it.** So Corbettmaths, MathsGenie, Dr Frost,
Maths4Everyone, PMT, Dr Austin and Third Space are silent for a reason, and that reason is useful:
their treatment of the exact-value table as two separate memorised patterns is precisely the
blindness a C.7 lesson exists to fix.

**19. One source is affirmatively hostile and must be flagged in the build docs.**
PhysicsAndMathsTutor is stamped **CC BY-NC-ND 4.0** on every page. **ND bars adaptation even in
part.** It is the only source in the sweep that is worse than "cite only".
