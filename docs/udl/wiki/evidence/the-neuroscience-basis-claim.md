# Is UDL Grounded in Neuroscience?

## Summary

UDL's three principles are presented by CAST as built on three brain networks:
affective, recognition, and strategic. This page appraises that warrant. The short
version: the networks are real enough as coarse functional neuroanatomy, but the
inference from "three networks" to "three principles of instructional design" is not
carried by the neuroscience, and an audit of CAST's own citations found the
neuroscience literature barely present in its evidence base. None of that makes the
design advice wrong, which is the distinction this page exists to hold open.

## When to reach for it

Reach for this page when someone is treating the brain-network story as the reason to
adopt UDL, or as the reason to reject it. Both moves overload a warrant that cannot
bear the weight in either direction.

The distinctions matter because these are four separate claims that share a
vocabulary. A weak neuroscience warrant, a thin outcome literature, a learning-styles
resemblance, and a statutory label are independent problems. Refuting one does not
refute the others, and the common practitioner move of bundling them into "UDL is
debunked" is not supported by any of them individually.

## The claim

CAST states it directly:

> The three basic principles are built upon the knowledge that our learning brains are
> composed of three different networks, recognition, strategic, and affective.

With the mapping spelled out: affective networks "evaluate environments and drive
motivation and engagement" and align with [[multiple-means-of-engagement]] (the WHY);
recognition networks "sense and perceive information, transforming it into usable
knowledge" and align with [[multiple-means-of-representation]] (the WHAT); strategic
networks "plan, organize, and initiate purposeful actions" and align with
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]] (the HOW).

**Two claims are bundled inside that sentence, and they are not the same claim.**

**(a) The descriptive claim.** Affective, recognition, and strategic systems exist,
are dissociable at some level of description, and are implicated in learning. This is
close to a restatement of coarse functional neuroanatomy. It is not seriously
contested, and it is also not doing much work: it says that motivation, perception,
and action are distinguishable, which was known long before anyone imaged a brain.

**(b) The licensing claim.** That the three-way neural division *authorises* a
three-part design prescription: that instruction should be organised as three
principles, each offering multiple means along its axis. This is the claim the word
"built upon" is making, and it is a much stronger one. For it to hold you need at
least three things the anatomy does not supply:

1. **The partition is the right one at the level where teaching happens.** Three
   networks would have to carve instruction at its joints, not merely carve cortex.
2. **Each network's properties entail specific design moves.** "Recognition networks
   perceive information" does not, by itself, say to offer captions rather than to
   offer a second worked example. Something else has to bridge from the network to
   the option.
3. **Instruction organised this way outperforms instruction not organised this way.**
   That is an outcome question, and it is not answered by neuroanatomy at all.

**(a) does not establish (b).** A reader who accepts every word of the descriptive
claim has been given no reason yet to accept the framework's structure, and this is
where the argument actually has to be won or lost.

## What the evidence shows

**The critique is published and specific.** Murphy's is the strongest statement in
this wiki's critique source:

> [CAST's] claims about brain function remain an untested hypothesis rather than an
> established fact. The framers of UDL claim that its principles relate to brain
> function, but this is a hypothesis, not an established fact.

Murphy, M. P. A. (2021), *Policy Futures in Education* 19(1), 12–27. Peer-reviewed,
and the critique source rates it WELL-SOURCED.

Boysen presses the level-of-analysis point directly:

> CAST's brain model is overgeneralized and implausible given current understanding of
> neuroscience. A major flaw in the neuroscience justification of UDL is its
> oversimplified summary of brain function, using this broad generality to justify one
> third of their educational framework by pointing to three fourths of the cerebral
> cortex.

Attribution caveat, stated by the source rather than inferred here: the critique
source dates this to Boysen (2024) but records only that it "appears in subsequent
scholarship on UDL neuroscience critiques", and its own assessment says the claim
"requires direct paper access to confirm exact Boysen attribution." Treat the wording
as reliably reported and the citation as not fully pinned.

**The citation audit.** This is the most checkable part of the case, because it is a
claim about CAST's own reference list rather than about brains. Reported verbatim from
this wiki's critique source:

> Only 1% of the 1,442 unique sources cited by CAST represented neuroscience, and 75%
> of UDL guidelines had no neuroscientific sources. The neuroscientific sources CAST
> cited mostly demonstrated activity across multiple brain regions, which contradicts
> their proposed alignment of guidelines with three distinct brain areas.

A second pass over the same evidence base is reported alongside it:

> A critical analysis of CAST's UDL guidelines found that the cited studies provided
> little evidence for claims about UDL, most studies did not offer a choice to learners
> or measure learning, and none were related to brain function.

**Attribution, precisely.** These figures are the audit's, not this wiki's. The
critique source attributes the first to "multi-institutional critiques of CAST's
evidence base (2023-2024)" and the second to "Analysis of CAST citations by Ritchey and
collaborators", and its own assessment states that "the original source document has
not been fully verified via primary access." So: reported by the audit, corroborated
across several critiques, not verified here at the primary document.

**Three imprecisions in those figures, disclosed.** "1%" is given rounded, with no
count of the neuroscience sources behind it. The denominator for "75% of UDL
guidelines" is not stated, and it cannot be the nine guidelines of 3.0, since 75% of
nine is 6.75. The audit was therefore counting a finer unit, most plausibly the 2.2 checkpoints
that preceded the current considerations, but the source does not say which version was
audited. Nor does it define what counts as a guideline "having" a neuroscientific
source. The direction of the finding is clear; the precision is not what the round
numbers imply.

**What CAST currently says.** Version 3.0 did not retreat from the framing. CAST
"continues to lead with the three brain networks as foundational" while stating that
3.0

> builds upon, updates, and expands the previous research base to include a greater
> diversity of perspectives and research from the areas of sociocultural perspectives on
> learning, disability studies, and asset-based pedagogies.

The foundations source reads this as "a softening of the neuroscience-first framing,
not its removal": neuroscience remains central but is no longer the sole foundation,
where materials from 2011 and earlier led more exclusively with brain networks.

**The proponents' response is a reframe, not a rebuttal.** The defence on record runs
that the three networks are "presented as grounded in cognitive science, not
neuroscience dogma" and represent valid cognitive distinctions "without claiming
one-to-one mapping to brain regions", captured in the widely circulated formula
"Aligned with neuroscience, UDL principles target affective, recognition, and strategic
brain networks." The critique source attributes that formula to CAST-aligned
publications rather than to CAST directly, and rates the defence WEAK: the shift from
"proven by" to "aligned with" is "a rhetorical concession rather than a substantive
rebuttal", and it leaves the licensing claim (b) exactly where it was.

**There is no formal CAST rebuttal.** The critique source states plainly: "No published
peer-reviewed response from CAST to Murphy or Boysen's neuroscience critiques has been
located", and its summary table records the neuroscience line as "WELL-EVIDENCED but
CAST has not formally responded." That is an absence found by this wiki's source, not a
proof that none exists. But it has now stood for several years across a critique
literature that is not obscure.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **A weak warrant does not make the advice wrong, and this is the whole point of the
  page.** "Offer captions as well as audio" stands or falls on whether it helps a
  learner get the content, not on the recognition network. Every design option in this
  wiki is testable on its own terms, and most of them were arrived at by people
  watching learners rather than by reading imaging studies. Separate the *warrant* from
  the *practice*: the neuroscience critique lands on the first and leaves the second
  untouched. Read this page as a debunking of UDL and you have made exactly the
  inference the page is arguing against.
- **What the weak warrant does cost is real, though.** It supplies unearned confidence,
  it fixes the framework's shape at three columns when nothing empirical requires three,
  and it feeds the "scientifically valid" language that travels into policy. See
  [[scientifically-valid-the-statutory-claim]] for what that statutory phrase does and
  does not certify.
- **This is not the outcome question.** Nothing here says UDL-designed instruction does
  or does not improve learning. That literature is appraised separately in
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]], and it has its own problems, which are not
  these problems.
- **This is not the learning-styles question either.** The neuroscience critique and
  the learning-styles parallel are argued by overlapping authors and are frequently
  fused into one objection. They are distinct: one is about whether the brain story
  licenses the framework's structure, the other is about whether "multiple means of
  representation" repeats a discredited matching hypothesis. See
  [[udl-and-the-learning-styles-problem]].
- **The audit is second-hand in this wiki.** The 1% and 75% figures reach these pages
  through a critique review, not through the audit document itself. Anyone building an
  argument on them should retrieve the primary analysis before quoting them in print.
- **Deliberately not reported here.** A claim sometimes attached to this debate (that
  neuroscience framing raises the perceived credibility of an educational claim
  independent of its merit) is not present in this wiki's staged sources, so it is not
  asserted on this page. It would be relevant if sourced; it is not sourced.
- **The framework does not need the brains.** [[learner-variability]] and
  [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] are the load-bearing ideas in practice, and neither one
  requires the three-network story to be true. A version of UDL that dropped the
  neuroscience entirely would lose a marketing frame and very little else.
