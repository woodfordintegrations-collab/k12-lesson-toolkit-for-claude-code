# 9.2 Develop Awareness of Self and Others

## Summary

Consideration 9.2 sits under [[emotional-capacity]] (Guideline 9) in the Engagement
principle, [[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. It has two halves: building the capacity
to recognize and manage one's own emotional responses in the moment, and building the
capacity to understand the perspectives of others, particularly people from different
backgrounds and cultures.

Like the rest of Guideline 9, this is an environmental-design consideration, not a
diagnosis of a learner's regulatory ability. The design options below are scaffolds
you build into the classroom (models, prompts, structured activities), not a
description of what a well-regulated learner looks like.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 9.2 when a learner needs a modeled, practiced strategy for managing an
emotional response as it happens (frustration, anxiety, distraction, rumination), or
when the gap is in reading and understanding other people's perspectives, especially
across difference.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The need is a structured occasion to look *back* on progress, not a live coping strategy | [[individual-and-collective-reflection]] (9.3) |
| Trust has already broken between learners and needs repair, not general awareness-building | [[empathy-and-restorative-practices]] (9.4) |
| The learner doesn't believe their goals are reachable in the first place | [[expectations-beliefs-and-motivations]] (9.1) |
| The barrier is bias built into the materials themselves, not a gap in learner awareness | [[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3) |
| The distress is persistent or severe enough to need clinical support | outside UDL's scope: a counseling or mental-health referral, not a design fix |

**9.2 vs. 9.3, precisely.** These two are easy to conflate because both are about
processing emotion in relation to learning. 9.2 is developing the underlying
capacity to *notice*: recognizing what you or someone else is feeling and having a
strategy on hand for it. 9.3 is the structured *occasion*: an actual scheduled
moment, individual or collective, to look back on what happened. 9.2 without 9.3 is a
toolkit nobody is ever asked to use; 9.3 without 9.2 asks learners to reflect using
skills they were never given.

## How it works

CAST's own definition:

> Providing a model of regulatory skills and practices is not sufficient for most
> learners. Opportunities for learners to process their emotions and reflect on their
> strengths and challenges are essential. Reminders, models, and checklists can assist
> learners in choosing and trying an adaptive strategy for managing and directing
> their emotional responses to external events (e.g., strategies for coping with
> anxiety-producing social settings or for reducing task-irrelevant distractors) or
> internal events (e.g., strategies for decreasing rumination on depressive or
> anxiety-producing ideation). In addition, scaffolds may be provided to develop and
> support social awareness, or the ability to understand the perspectives of others,
> including people from different backgrounds and cultures. Such scaffolds should
> provide sufficient options to honor the variability and cultivate identity-safe
> spaces for every learner.

The opening line is the bound: **modeling alone is explicitly named as insufficient.**
CAST asks for practiced, chosen strategies, plural and differentiated, not a single
demonstrated technique the whole class is expected to adopt.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 9.2, verbatim:

**Use differentiated models, scaffolds, and feedback for:**
- Managing frustration
- Seeking external emotional support
- Developing internal controls and coping skills
- Appropriately handling subject-specific phobias and judgments of "natural" aptitude
  (e.g., "How can I improve on the areas I am struggling in?" rather than "I am not
  good at math.")

**Additional strategies:**
- Use real life situations or simulations to demonstrate coping skills
- Create opportunities for learners to reflect on social interactions
- Create opportunities for learners to appreciate their personal, cultural, and
  linguistic assets and the assets of others (e.g., displaying learner-created
  self-portraits, creating spaces for affinity groups, sharing notes of appreciation
  with peers and colleagues)

**A worked pass.** A learner shuts down after every math error, framing it as "I'm
bad at math." This looks like 9.1 territory, and partly is, but 9.2 is the piece
that hands the learner an actual in-the-moment tool: a differentiated script for
naming what specifically went wrong ("what tripped me up here" rather than a global
verdict), practiced ahead of time through a real or simulated example, so it's
available at the moment frustration hits rather than something the learner is asked
to invent under stress.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **This brushes directly against clinical territory, and CAST's design options stay
  on the design side of that line.** "Strategies for decreasing rumination on
  depressive or anxiety-producing ideation" and "subject-specific phobias" are named
  in the source, but the actual design options are classroom scaffolds (differentiated
  models, feedback, reflection prompts), not therapeutic technique. A learner showing
  sustained clinical-level distress needs counseling or mental-health support, a
  system UDL does not replace and should not be read as replacing.
- **"Sufficient options to honor the variability" is not quantified.** CAST does not
  say how many strategies, or how a designer would know coverage is adequate. That is
  a disclosed gap in the framework, not an omission on this page.
- **Social awareness here means reading others' perspectives, not agreeing with them
  or resolving conflict.** Conflict repair specifically is 9.4's territory, not this
  one.
