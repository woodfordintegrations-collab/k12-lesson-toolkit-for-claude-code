# 7.1 Optimize Choice and Autonomy

## Summary

Consideration 7.1 sits under [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] in the Engagement
principle. Its one-line brief from CAST is "Develop agency in the learning process."
It asks you to embed **authentic choices that align with learning goals**, and it is
explicit that the amount and kind of choice must be tuned to learners rather than
maximized.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 7.1 when a learner is capable of the work but not investing in it, and you
have reason to think **control is the missing ingredient**: the task is being done to
them rather than by them.

It is the wrong tool for a surprising number of nearby problems:

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| They cannot access the material at all | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |
| They do not see why this matters | [[relevance-value-and-authenticity]] (7.2) |
| They can start but cannot sustain effort | [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) |
| They cannot show what they know in this format | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) |
| The environment feels unsafe | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |

The diagnostic question is not "could I offer a choice here?" You almost always
could. It is "**is the absence of control what is actually costing me engagement?**"
Choice offered against a different problem adds workload for you and noise for the
learner without moving the thing that was stuck.

## How it works

CAST's claim is bounded, and the bound is the substance of the consideration:

> Embedding authentic choices that align with learning goals can be a meaningful way
> to enhance learner engagement... individuals differ in how much and what kind of
> choices they prefer to have. **It is therefore not enough to simply provide choice.**

Two conditions travel with the word *optimize* in the title:

1. **Alignment.** The choice varies the *means*, never the *goal*. This is
   [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] applied at the level of a single task. A choice that
   changes what is being learned is not this consideration; it is construct-shift, and
   [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]] covers why that fails.
2. **Calibration.** Learners differ in how much choice they want. Open choice is a
   support for some learners and a cost for others, and CAST treats the right amount
   as a design variable rather than a constant to be maximized.

The output is [[learner-agency]]: the framework's stated goal in version 3.0, which
replaced "expert learners". Agency is what choice is *for*; that is why the guideline
brief says "develop agency" rather than "offer options".

## In practice

CAST names six dimensions you can vary. They are worth reading as a menu to pick
**one or two** from, not a checklist:

- the content to explore
- the tools used for exploration or production
- the type of rewards or recognition available
- the opportunities for practicing and assessing learning
- the design or graphics of layouts
- the sequence or timing for completion of tasks

Note how modest several of these are. **Sequence and timing**, letting a learner
decide what order to work in, or when a piece is due within a window, is a real
instance of 7.1 that costs almost nothing to design and does not touch your
assessment. The dimensions people reach for first (content, tools) are the most
expensive and are not privileged in CAST's list.

Beyond the menu, CAST names one move that is different in kind:

> Use a collaborative approach between learners and educators to co-design learning
> goals, activities, and tasks.

Co-design is not a choice among options you built. It is a choice about what the
options are, and it is the strongest form of this consideration.

**A worked pass.** A unit ends in a five-paragraph essay and half the class is
disengaged. Applying 7.1 does not mean replacing the essay with a choice board. It
means asking which part of "five-paragraph essay" is the construct. If the goal is
*constructing an evidence-based argument*, then the paragraph count, the medium, and
the order of drafting are all means, and any of them can be opened. If the goal is
*writing an essay to a genre convention*, then the genre is the construct and the
openable dimensions are timing, sequence, topic, and tools, a narrower but still real
set. The goal decides what is available; you do not decide first and check the goal
after. See [[apply-udl-to-a-standard]] for doing this against standards language.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"More choice is better" is a misreading of this page.** CAST's own text says
  plainly that it is "not enough to simply provide choice", and that learners differ
  in how much they want. A choice board is not evidence of UDL. The failure mode has
  its own page: [[choice-as-a-substitute-for-design]].
- **CAST does not quantify the optimum.** The Guidelines say to optimize choice
  without specifying how much, for whom, or how to tell when you have overshot. This
  is a real gap in the framework rather than an omission in this page, and it is a
  specific instance of the operationalization problem that
  [[measuring-udl-implementation-fidelity]] takes up.
- **Choice can be offered and simultaneously withheld.** If every option requires
  reading dense text, a learner who cannot decode has no choice at all. Options must
  themselves clear the access bar: see [[access-to-assistive-technologies]] (4.2) and
  [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]].
- **Autonomy is not the absence of structure.** Withdrawing scaffolding is not the
  same design move as offering control, and the two are frequently confused.
  [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] (5.3) handles fading support; this page
  does not.
- **Evidence caveat.** Engagement effects for UDL as a whole rest on a literature with
  weak designs and no consensus operationalization; nothing on this page should be
  read as a demonstrated effect size for this consideration specifically. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
