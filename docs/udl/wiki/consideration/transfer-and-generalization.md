# 3.4 Maximize Transfer and Generalization

## Summary

Consideration 3.4 sits under [[building-knowledge]] in [[multiple-means-of-representation]],
and it is the last of the guideline's four considerations, building on the other
three: prior knowledge connected ([[connect-prior-knowledge]]), critical features
identified ([[patterns-critical-features-and-big-ideas]]), and multiple ways of
knowing honored ([[multiple-ways-of-knowing]]). The design move here is to apply
learning to new contexts, and this is the hardest thing this guideline asks for.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 3.4 when a learner has genuinely built the knowledge or skill inside the
context where it was taught, and the barrier shows up specifically at the boundary:
moving it to a new problem, format, or setting, not when the original learning
hasn't actually happened yet.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The knowledge was never actually built in the first place | [[connect-prior-knowledge]] (3.1) |
| They can't identify what was critical about the original learning to carry forward | [[patterns-critical-features-and-big-ideas]] (3.2) |
| The way the original understanding was built is itself the mismatch | [[multiple-ways-of-knowing]] (3.3) |
| They need repeated, scaffolded practice to get fluent within the same context, not across contexts | [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] (5.3) |
| You want to know whether the design options below actually produce transfer before you build a lesson around them | [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] |

## How it works

CAST states the claim directly:

> "All learners need to be able to generalize and transfer their learning to new
> contexts. Learners vary in the amount of scaffolding they need for memory and
> transfer in order to improve their ability to access their prior learning."

And names the failure mode without proper support produces:

> "information might be learned, but is inaccessible in new situations."

The staged source's own annotation (its wording, not CAST's) names **transfer** and
**generalization** as the core concepts here, and adds **memory** and **schema**
accessibility across contexts, noting that mnemonic support is what keeps learned
material reachable outside the context it was learned in.

**This is the point where naming the goal and achieving it come apart.** The
wider cognitive-science literature treats transfer (especially to contexts that
don't superficially resemble the original one) as one of the hardest effects to
reliably produce in education. The staged source does not distinguish near transfer
(same domain, similar surface features) from far transfer (a different domain or
setting), even though that distinction is exactly what determines how hard transfer
is to achieve. That is a real gap in what is grounded here, not a page limitation to
paper over: reporting it is more honest than importing a transfer-distance framework
from memory that CAST's own page doesn't use. CAST's design options below are
plausible supports for transfer; nothing in the staged source demonstrates that they
produce it. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for what the broader UDL
evidence base does and doesn't show, and treat naming this consideration as the start
of a design problem, not the solution to one.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 3.4:

- Use organizational tools like checklists, sticky notes, and electronic reminders
- Prompt mnemonic strategies including "visual imagery, paraphrasing strategies,
  method of loci"
- Incorporate explicit review and practice of concepts
- Apply templates, graphic organizers, and concept maps for note-taking
- Create scaffolds connecting new information to prior knowledge
- Embed concepts within "familiar ideas and contexts (e.g., use of analogy, metaphor,
  drama, music, film)"
- Provide "explicit, supported opportunities to generalize learning to new
  situations"
- Revisit key ideas and their interconnections over extended periods

**A worked pass.** A student learns to calculate proportional relationships in a
recipe-scaling math unit. Transfer isn't achieved by restating the rule at the end of
the unit: it's built by presenting the same underlying construct, proportional
reasoning, in a genuinely different surface form later in the term: a map-scale
problem, a paint-mixing-ratio problem, spaced out rather than back-to-back, with
explicit prompting to notice the underlying structure ("this is the same kind of
relationship as..."). That combination (spacing, surface variation, and explicit
structure-naming) is what separates CAST's design options (revisit ideas over
extended periods, embed in familiar contexts, prompt generalization explicitly) from
simply re-teaching the same problem type twice. See [[apply-udl-to-a-standard]] for
identifying which part of a standard is the transferable construct in the first
place.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **Do not let naming this consideration read as accomplishing it.** "Maximize
  transfer and generalization" is a design goal, not a design outcome. CAST's options
  list (mnemonics, review, templates, revisiting ideas) is a set of plausible
  supports, not a demonstrated mechanism, and the staged source gives no number or
  effect size for whether they work. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
- **Near versus far transfer is not distinguished in the staged source**, though it's
  the standard way cognitive science talks about why transfer succeeds or fails. This
  is a disclosed gap, not a claim this page is making on the framework's behalf.
- **"Revisit key ideas... over extended periods" gestures at spaced or distributed
  review** without naming that research base or specifying a schedule. Treat it as
  the design option CAST states, not an evidenced protocol.
- **Confusion with 5.3.** [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] is about becoming
  smooth and accurate on repeated instances of the *same* kind of task. 3.4 is
  specifically about crossing to a *different* context. A learner can be fully fluent
  in one context and still fail to transfer to another: fluency and transfer are not
  the same achievement.
