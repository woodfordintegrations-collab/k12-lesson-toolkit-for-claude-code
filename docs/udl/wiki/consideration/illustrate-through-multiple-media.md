# 2.5 Illustrate Through Multiple Media

## Summary

Consideration 2.5 sits under [[language-and-symbols]] in the Representation
principle. It targets a common default failure: materials that explain a concept only
in expository text or a single equation. CAST's fix is to illustrate the *same*
concept through additional formats (diagrams, video, animation, manipulatives) so
text isn't the sole route in.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 2.5 when a concept is currently presented in only one format, usually text,
and you want to give learners a second way to *receive and understand* that same
concept.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| You want the learner to *produce* their understanding in a non-text format | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1): this is expression, not this page |
| The learner cannot perceive one modality at all (blind, deaf) | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |
| The illustration itself uses notation the learner can't decode | [[decoding-text-notation-and-symbols]] (2.2) |
| The vocabulary inside the illustration's labels or captions is the barrier | [[clarify-vocabulary-and-language-structures]] (2.1) |

**The single test that resolves most misapplications here: is a format being opened as
an additional input the learner receives, or as an additional output the learner
produces?** 2.5 is always input-side: a second (or third) way a concept is presented
to the learner. [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) is output-side: the
formats a learner is allowed to use to show what they know. Getting this backwards is
a common and specifically flagged error: adding video explanations for the class is
2.5; letting students turn in a video instead of an essay is 5.1. They are frequently
paired in the same lesson and easy to file under the same instinct, but they solve
different problems and sit under different principles: 2.5 under Representation,
5.1 under Action & Expression.

## How it works

CAST's definition of the barrier:

> Relying on text as a single mode of representation can impose barriers when
> presenting concepts and processes, particularly for learners who have text- or
> language-related disabilities or learners who communicate in languages other than
> the dominant language.

And the benefit:

> Offering multiple representations—especially illustrations, simulations, images,
> videos, or interactive graphics—can make the information in text more relatable and
> comprehensible for any learner and accessible for some who would find it completely
> inaccessible in text.

For contrast, 5.1's own framing makes the input/output split explicit from the other
side: it directs educators to welcome a variety of media for expression so learners can
select the medium that best fits their content and audience. That staged source is
itself a summarized paraphrase rather than a verbatim capture, so it is described here
rather than quoted, but the point stands: that is about what the *learner* produces,
which is exactly what 2.5 is not about.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 2.5, notably thinner than its sibling considerations, just
two items in the staged source, and this page will not pad that out with invented
strategies:

- Present key concepts beyond text (e.g., expository text or a math equation) with
  another or multiple formats including: illustration, dance/movement, diagram, table,
  model, video, comic strip, storyboard, photograph, animation, or physical/virtual
  manipulative
- Clarify links between information provided in texts and any accompanying
  representation in illustrations, equations, charts, or diagrams

**A worked pass.** A photosynthesis unit currently relies on a single paragraph of
expository text. Under 2.5, the same concept gets a labeled diagram of the chloroplast
and an animation of the light-dependent and light-independent reactions: two more
illustrations of the same content, still input side. Whether students then explain
photosynthesis back in writing, a recorded explanation, or a diagram of their own is a
separate, 5.1-governed decision, made against the assessment's own construct: see
[[apply-udl-to-a-standard]].

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The 2.5 / 5.1 confusion is the main risk on this page**, and the source gives no
  extra help disambiguating them beyond the principle each sits under, hence the
  explicit swap-test above.
- **Illustrating a concept in multiple media does not by itself satisfy any
  Action & Expression consideration.** A beautifully illustrated unit can still ask
  every student to demonstrate mastery in exactly one format.
- **CAST does not say how many additional formats are enough**, or when adding more
  becomes clutter rather than clarity: a gap in the framework similar to
  [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]]'s unspecified optimum, not an omission on this
  page.
- **A second illustration can introduce its own barrier.** A diagram with unfamiliar
  notation, or an animation narrated in only the dominant language, reintroduces a
  2.1/2.2/2.3-level problem nested inside what was meant to be the fix. Illustrating
  through multiple media does not exempt the illustration itself from the rest of this
  guideline.
- **Evidence caveat.** See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]]; no effect sizes are
  available in this source for multi-format illustration specifically.
