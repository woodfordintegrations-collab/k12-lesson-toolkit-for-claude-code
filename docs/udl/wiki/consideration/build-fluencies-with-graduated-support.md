# 5.3 Build Fluencies with Graduated Support for Practice and Performance

## Summary

Consideration 5.3 sits under [[expression-and-communication]] in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. CAST's overview page condenses it to apply
and gradually release scaffolds to support learner agency. It is the framework's
scaffolding node: support that exists to help a learner build a fluency, and is
**deliberately withdrawn** as that fluency develops. Support that is never withdrawn is
not this consideration. It is something else, and this page draws that line explicitly.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 5.3 when a learner needs help right now to practice or perform, and the
design intends that help to shrink over time as skill grows. The question this
consideration answers is "how much support, fading on what schedule," not "how much
choice" and not "which format."

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The learner needs more say over what they do, not more help doing it | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| The learner needs a different medium to express in | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) |
| The learner needs better tools mid-task, not more scaffolding | [[tools-for-construction-and-composition]] (5.2) |
| The support in question is a documented individual accommodation | [[assuming-udl-replaces-accommodations]] |

The first row is the one worth pausing on. **Fading support and offering control are
different design moves, and they get conflated constantly.** Choice is about who
decides what to do. Scaffolding is about how much help is available while doing it. A
learner can have full autonomy over a task and receive heavy scaffolding within it, or
have no choice in the task at all and work entirely unaided. The two axes move
independently. See [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]]'s own gotcha on this same
confusion, stated from the other direction.

## How it works

CAST distinguishes process from product, and treats both as legitimate sites for
building fluency:

> Instead of emphasizing the end product, the most valuable learning can take place
> during the process itself.

and, on the other side of the same page:

> While engaging in the process of learning itself is critical, the end product or
> performance can also be a meaningful way to build fluencies, be it in the form of an
> essay or a dramatic production or some other form of expression.

The mechanism that does the work is release: "scaffolds that can be gradually released
over time" as independence and skill increase. This is where the word *graduated* in
the title earns its place: the design commitment is not just to provide support, it is
to plan for less of it.

That commitment has a direct consequence CAST's own language implies but does not spell
out at length: a scaffold described as "gradually released with increasing
independence" is, by definition, temporary. If it is never withdrawn, what is being
practiced and reported quietly stops being "the learner can do this" and becomes "the
learner can do this with the scaffold present," a different, narrower claim than the
one the assessment or the classroom is usually treated as making. CAST does not use
those words, but it follows from what CAST does say about release.

## In practice

CAST's design options, listed under this consideration:

- Use differentiated models to emulate (e.g., models that demonstrate the same
  outcomes but use differing approaches, strategies, skills, etc.).
- Use differentiated mentors (e.g., teachers/tutors who use different approaches to
  motivate, guide, feedback, or inform).
- Use scaffolds that can be gradually released with increasing independence and
  skills (e.g., embedded into digital reading and writing software).
- Use differentiated feedback (e.g., feedback that is accessible because it can be
  customized to individual learners).
- Use multiple examples of novel solutions to authentic problems.

**A worked pass.** Teaching persuasive writing: early on, a learner drafts with a
graphic organizer, sentence starters, and a fully worked model in front of them. Mid-way,
the organizer is partial and the model is gone. By the summative task, the learner
writes from a blank page under the same conditions the assessment will use. The fade
schedule is planned at design time, not improvised in the moment, and its end state is
chosen to match whatever the assessment actually measures: if the summative task still
provides the organizer that scaffolded early practice, the practice period never
actually ended.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **This is not 7.1.** Fading scaffolds and offering choice sit on independent axes.
  Do not read a heavily scaffolded task as low-autonomy, or a high-choice task as
  unscaffolded. Check each separately.
- **A scaffold that never fades is not this consideration as CAST states it.** "Gradually
  released" is a claim about direction, not a permanent feature. A crutch that stays in
  place indefinitely has become a standing accommodation or a lowered bar, not graduated
  support, and it quietly changes what "the learner can do this" is actually reporting.
- **The fade's end state has to match the construct being assessed.** If the summative
  task still carries the scaffold the practice period was supposed to remove, the design
  has retrofit the assessment to the support rather than building toward independence.
  See [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] and [[construct-irrelevant-variance]].
- **CAST does not specify a fade rate.** How fast to release, for whom, and how to tell
  a stalled fade from a working one are not addressed in the source. This is a
  disclosed gap in the framework, not a detail this page is withholding.
- **This is a design-level move, not an eligibility decision.** Fading scaffolds for
  instructional design generally is not the same thing as removing an individual
  learner's documented accommodation. See [[assuming-udl-replaces-accommodations]].
