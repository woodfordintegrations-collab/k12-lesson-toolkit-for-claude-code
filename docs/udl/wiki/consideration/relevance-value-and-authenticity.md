# 7.2 Optimize Relevance, Value, and Authenticity

## Summary

Consideration 7.2 sits under [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] in the Engagement
principle. Its one-line brief from CAST is "Connect learning to experiences that are
meaningful and valuable." The load-bearing claim on this page is a correction to how
most people read "relevance": CAST states outright that **authenticity does not
require real-life equivalence**: fiction can be just as engaging to learners as
non-fiction.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 7.2 when a learner can do the work, is not lacking control over how they do
it, and is still checked out, because the task reads as arbitrary schoolwork with no
connection to anything they value.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| They cannot access the material at all | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |
| The task is being done to them, not by them | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| The work is technically relevant but flat, joyless | [[nurture-joy-and-play]] (7.3) |
| The environment itself feels unsafe | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |
| It's the *goal* that feels pointless, not the activity | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |
| The material doesn't depict people like them | [[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3) |

The last two rows are genuinely adjacent and worth separating deliberately. 8.1 is
about whether a learner understands why a *goal* matters enough to sustain effort
toward it; 7.2 is about whether the *activity itself* connects to something the
learner already cares about. 1.3 is about who and what is represented inside the
material's content; 7.2 is about whether that content connects to the learner's own
interests, goals, or community, regardless of what it represents.

## How it works

CAST's own definition sets up the correction directly:

> Individuals are engaged by content and activities that are relevant and valuable to
> their interests, goals, and communities. Authenticity does not require real-life
> equivalence—fiction can be just as engaging to learners as non-fiction—but content
> must connect to learners' individual and/or community goals and the instructional
> goals.

Two things are doing work in that sentence. First, "real-life equivalence" is
explicitly not the bar: a common misreading of "relevance" as "make it applied" or
"make it real-world" is not what this consideration says. Second, the actual bar is a
connection to the learner's own goals and community *and* the instructional goal
simultaneously: a task can be maximally engaging and still miss 7.2 if it serves only
one of those.

CAST names a specific tradition here rather than a generic call for variety: it says
activities and sources should be **"culturally relevant and sustaining"** alongside
personalized, socially relevant, age- and ability-appropriate, and appropriate across
racial, cultural, ethnic, and gender groups. That phrase names a scholarly tradition in
education, not a synonym for "include diverse materials." This page uses it as CAST
states it and does not elaborate further, since the staged source does not go beyond
naming it.

CAST's own account of why 3.0 revised this guideline names an **asset-based framing**
as one of three core shifts in the revision: putting UDL "in conversation with other
asset-based approaches and theoretical frameworks, including pedagogies that center,
value, and sustain learners' cultural practices." That is the frame this consideration
sits inside: relevance is not a motivational trick layered onto instruction, it is
treated as connected to which cultural practices the design honors.

Finally: "different learners find different activities equally relevant." Like
[[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]], this is a calibration problem, not a single correct
hook that works for everyone.

## In practice

Design options, verbatim from the source:

- Vary activities and sources of information so they can be personalized and
  contextualized to learners' lives, culturally relevant and sustaining, socially
  relevant, age and ability appropriate, and appropriate for different racial,
  cultural, ethnic, and gender groups
- Design activities so learning outcomes are authentic, communicate to real audiences,
  and reflect a purpose that is clear to the participants
- Provide tasks that allow for active participation, exploration, and experimentation
- Invite personal response, evaluation, and self-reflection to content and activities
- Include activities that foster imagination to solve novel problems or make sense of
  complex ideas creatively

**A worked pass.** A math unit is stuck at "make the word problems real-world"
(grocery budgets, sports statistics) and half the class still isn't engaged, because
those specific real-world hooks aren't relevant to them. Applying 7.2 correctly does
not mean searching harder for the right real-world scenario. The instructional goal is
solving multi-step ratio problems; that goal does not require the scenario to be real
at all. A learner who cares about a fictional world (a game, a book series, a fandom)
can solve the identical ratio structure inside a scenario drawn from that world, and by
CAST's own text this is not a lesser version of "authentic": fiction is stated to be
just as engaging as non-fiction. The construct stays firm (ratio reasoning); the
scenario is the means, and it opens onto whatever the learner actually values. See
[[firm-goals-flexible-means]] for the general move.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Relevant" does not mean "real-world."** This is the single most common misreading
  of this consideration, and CAST's own text rejects the equivalence directly. A
  fictional or fantastical scenario that connects to a learner's actual interests
  clears the bar that a "realistic" scenario with no personal connection does not.
- **"Culturally relevant and sustaining" is named, not defined, in the staged source.**
  This page uses the term precisely as CAST states it and does not expand it with
  attribution or history the source does not provide: that would be a memory-grounded
  claim, not a sourced one.
- **No single hook works for the whole class.** The source states plainly that
  different learners find different activities equally relevant; treating one
  "relevant" redesign as done for everyone repeats the calibration mistake covered on
  [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]].
- **Relevance is not a legal or accessibility requirement.** It is a design
  consideration inside UDL, not an enforceable standard. See
  [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]].
- **Evidence caveat.** Engagement effects for UDL as a whole rest on a literature with
  weak designs and no consensus operationalization; nothing on this page should be read
  as a demonstrated effect size for this consideration specifically. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
