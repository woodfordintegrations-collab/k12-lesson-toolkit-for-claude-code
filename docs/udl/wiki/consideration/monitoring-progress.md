# 6.4 Enhance Capacity for Monitoring Progress

## Summary

Consideration 6.4 sits under [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. It is about building the learner's own
capacity to see where they stand relative to a goal (self-monitoring) through
feedback that is varied, tied to goals, and usable by the learner directly rather
than only delivered as an external evaluation.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 6.4 when a learner has a goal and is working toward it but cannot tell,
without waiting for a teacher, whether they are on track.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| There is no goal yet to measure progress against | [[set-meaningful-goals]] (6.1) |
| The learner can see their status but the feedback doesn't sustain their motivation to keep going | [[action-oriented-feedback]] (8.5) |
| The learner doesn't see why the goal is worth tracking in the first place | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |
| The materials being tracked aren't organized enough to monitor against | [[organize-information-and-resources]] (6.3) |
| The problem is identifying what's critical in the material itself, not tracking progress through it | [[patterns-critical-features-and-big-ideas]] (3.2) |

6.4 and 8.5 are the pair most likely to be conflated in this guideline, for the same
reason 6.1 and 8.1 are: both are "feedback" pages, in different principles. 6.4
(Action & Expression) builds the learner's own capacity to self-monitor: the skill
of tracking status. 8.5 (Engagement) is about feedback that emphasizes effort and
process to sustain persistence: the motivational function. A learner can accurately
see they are behind (6.4 working) and still lose the will to close the gap (8.5
unaddressed), or stay motivated (8.5 working) without ever being given a way to check
their own status (6.4 unaddressed).

## How it works

CAST's framing, drawn directly from the staged extract:

> Learning requires feedback, and learners need clarity on progress toward learning
> goals.

CAST calls for "multiple and varied forms of feedback throughout the learning
process" that are connected to goals and characterized as "explicit, timely,
informative, accessible, and customizable." The stated priority is formative feedback
that lets learners "self-monitor and guide their own effort," the emphasis sits on
building the learner's own capacity to check status, not solely on the teacher
delivering an evaluation.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 6.4, the longest list in this guideline:

- Use prompts to guide self-monitoring and reflection.
- Use representations of progress (e.g., before-and-after photos, graphs and charts
  showing progress over time, process portfolios).
- Explore the different types of feedback that are most useful according to specific
  preferences, goals, and contexts.
- Use templates that guide self-reflection on quality and completeness.
- Use differentiated models of self-assessment strategies (e.g., role-playing, video
  reviews, peer feedback).
- Use checklists, rubrics, models, and examples.

**A worked pass.** A writing portfolio kept across a semester, with early and late
drafts kept side by side (design option two: before-and-after representations), a
rubric-guided self-reflection template completed at each review point (design option
four), and occasional peer-feedback rounds (design option five): a learner can see
their own trajectory between formal grading events, rather than learning their status
only when a teacher hands back a mark.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Explicit, timely, informative, accessible, and customizable" is a five-way
  balancing act CAST does not resolve.** Timely feedback (fast) and customizable
  feedback (tailored) can trade against each other in practice; the source names the
  five qualities without saying how to weigh them against each other. Disclosed gap,
  same pattern as 6.1 and 7.1's unquantified optimum.
- **Do not conflate this with 8.5.** See the differential above; both pages are
  about feedback, in different principles, doing different jobs.
- **The source's research link was not retrieved.** No findings are reported.
- **Evidence caveat.** No effect size specific to self-monitoring scaffolds was
  retrieved; nothing here should be read as a demonstrated outcome for this
  consideration alone. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] and
  [[measuring-udl-implementation-fidelity]] for the general measurement problem this
  runs into.
