# 6.2 Anticipate and Plan for Challenges

## Summary

Consideration 6.2 sits under [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. Once a goal exists (6.1), 6.2 is about
building the space and the habits to plan a strategy toward it and foresee the
obstacles that will show up along the way: proactive planning at the level of one
learner's own work, rather than at the level of curriculum design.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 6.2 when the goal is clear but the learner has no strategy for getting
there, or hits foreseeable obstacles they had no chance to plan around.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| There is no clear, workable goal yet | [[set-meaningful-goals]] (6.1) |
| The plan exists but the learner can't hold onto the information it depends on | [[organize-information-and-resources]] (6.3) |
| The plan is being executed but the learner can't tell if it's working | [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4) |
| The task itself needs to be harder or easier, not better planned | [[optimize-challenge-and-support]] (8.2) |
| The obstacle is the environment or community, not the individual's plan | [[challenge-exclusionary-practices]] (6.5) |

The diagnostic question: is this a foreseeable obstacle in executing a plan toward an
already-clear goal, as opposed to the goal itself being unformed, the task's
difficulty being miscalibrated, or the barrier being systemic rather than personal?

## How it works

CAST's definition:

> "Once a goal is set, it is important to create time and space to plan a strategy,
> identify useful tools, and anticipate the challenges that may surface along the
> way. This proactive planning can reduce barriers and ensure more individuals have
> the opportunity to reach their goals."

This is the same proactive-over-reactive move that [[proactive-design-vs-retrofit]]
names at the curriculum level, applied here at the grain of a single learner's
strategy: anticipating a specific obstacle before it derails the work, rather than
responding to it after it has already cost time or confidence.

Guideline 6's overview frames its scaffolding as two-part, and 6.2 is where both parts
show up directly: CAST scaffolds executive skills "(1) by scaffolding lower level
skills so they require less executive processing; and (2) by scaffolding higher level
executive skills and strategies so they are more effective and developed." A planning
template is the first kind: it takes the "hold the whole plan in mind" load off the
learner. A mentor modeling how they think through obstacles is the second: it
develops the learner's own planning strategy rather than replacing it.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 6.2:

- Use reflection prompts to anticipate challenges and encourage strategic planning.
- Use prompts to "show and explain your work" (e.g., portfolio review, art critiques).
- Use checklists and project planning templates for understanding the goal and
  setting up prioritization, sequences, and schedules of steps.
- Use coaches or mentors that model think-alouds of the process.
- Use guides for breaking long-term goals into reachable short-term objectives.

**A worked pass.** A learner is assigned a research paper due in six weeks. Applying
6.2 does not mean handing over a fixed timeline. It means using a planning template
(design option three) to break "write a research paper" into short-term objectives:
find and read five sources, draft a thesis, outline, draft, revise, and pairing that
with a reflection prompt (design option one) asking the learner to name where they
expect to get stuck: finding enough sources, narrowing the topic, or finishing on
time. The plan is built once and referenced against as work proceeds, which is what
distinguishes this from [[monitoring-progress]]: 6.2 builds the plan, 6.4 tracks
progress against it.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The source's own research link was not retrieved.** No findings are reported
  because none were staged, not because none exist.
- **CAST does not say how much planning scaffolding is enough, or when to fade it.**
  The two-part framing above (reduce load vs. build the learner's own skill) implies
  scaffolding should shift from the first kind toward the second over time, but the
  source does not state a fading schedule or a signal for when to withdraw support.
  Disclosed gap.
- **Don't mistake a hostile environment for a planning deficit.** If an obstacle
  recurs because of bias or exclusion rather than a gap in the individual's strategy,
  6.2's design options will not fix it. See [[challenge-exclusionary-practices]].
- **Evidence caveat.** No effect size for planning scaffolds specifically was
  retrieved; the general caution about UDL's evidence base applies here too. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
