# 6.5 Challenge Exclusionary Practices

## Summary

Consideration 6.5 sits under [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. It is the odd one out among its four
siblings, and the wiki says so plainly: 6.1 through 6.4 give an individual learner
tools for goal-setting, planning, organizing, and monitoring. 6.5 asks something
structurally different: that educators and communities surface and address their
own exclusionary practices. It is an institutional-practice consideration living
inside a guideline otherwise built from individual-learner strategy skills.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 6.5 when the question is not "what scaffold does this learner need" but
"what pattern in how we teach, assess, or run this community is itself producing
exclusion." It is a different unit of analysis from the rest of Guideline 6: a
community or program-level examination, not a per-lesson design decision.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| One learner needs a strategy scaffold: goal-setting, planning, organizing, monitoring | [[set-meaningful-goals]], [[anticipate-and-plan-for-challenges]], [[organize-information-and-resources]], [[monitoring-progress]] (6.1–6.4) |
| The bias is embedded specifically in vocabulary or symbols used | [[biases-in-language-and-symbols]] (2.4) |
| The bias is in which modes of expression are valued or devalued | [[biases-in-modes-of-expression]] (5.4) |
| The barrier is threats or distractions in the immediate learning space | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |
| The work is about an individual's own capacity for empathy and repair, not a systemic pattern | [[empathy-and-restorative-practices]] (9.4) |

6.5 and 9.4 both name restorative practice as part of the answer and will often
compose together in a real design: 6.5 is the Strategy Development consideration
that names community-based restorative justice as the remedy once an exclusionary
practice is uncovered; 9.4 is the Emotional Capacity consideration for cultivating
the empathy and relational skills restorative work draws on. They are not the same
node.

## How it works

CAST's own framing of what it means by exclusionary practices:

> "Deep-seated biases and systems of discrimination have led to the exclusion of
> historically marginalized learners across societies and around the globe. It is
> imperative to create structures to learn from learners' lived experiences in order
> to surface exclusionary practices–as well as to address them."

CAST's four design options, read together, are the actual ask, and they run in a
specific sequence: surface, name, act, repair.

1. **Surface, through lived experience.** "Create time for regular community
   sessions and individual reflection sessions": structured, recurring inquiry into
   learners' actual experience, not a one-off audit.
2. **Name and address collectively.** "Work as individuals and as communities to
   name, explore, and address exclusionary practices": the practice has to be
   identified out loud, by the community, not just diagnosed by an individual
   educator.
3. **Develop concrete action.** "Work as individuals and as communities to develop
   concrete and specific actions for addressing exclusionary practices and building
   inclusive communities": CAST specifies "concrete and specific," ruling out a
   general commitment to do better as sufficient.
4. **Repair through restorative approaches.** "When an exclusionary practice is
   uncovered, provide opportunities for healing using community-based practices such
   as restorative justice."

That four-step structure (surface, name, act, repair) is reported here because it
is what the source actually specifies, not a framework imported from elsewhere.
Nothing in the staged extract is inflated beyond it, and nothing is hedged away: this
is what CAST asks for.

3.0's broader rationale for the version, quoted from the version-history source,
places 6.5 in context: the revision "emphasizes addressing barriers rooted in biases
and systems of exclusion" as one of its central aims, not an isolated addition.

## In practice

**A worked pass.** A project-rubric review surfaces, through a regular reflection
session with students (step 1), that the rubric's language consistently penalizes
non-standard English syntax regardless of argument quality. The teaching team names
this explicitly: "our rubric language treats one dialect as the unmarked default"
(step 2), rather than treating individual low scores as isolated grading calls. The
concrete action (step 3) is rewriting the rubric language and recalibrating how
graders read syntax against argument quality, not a general resolution to "be more
inclusive." Where the practice caused real harm to specific students' grades over
time, the team uses a restorative conversation process to repair trust with those
students before the new rubric takes effect (step 4).

## Gotchas & constraints

- **In this wiki's assessment, 6.5 is the consideration most likely to be skipped in
  adoption.** This is this wiki's own inference from the shape of the five
  considerations, not a claim CAST makes anywhere in the staged source. 6.1–6.4 can
  each be adopted by a single educator redesigning a single lesson. 6.5 asks for
  recurring community sessions, collective naming, and restorative-justice capacity
  (things that need institutional time and often institutional authority an
  individual classroom-level UDL redesign does not require). A district or school
  that implements 6.1 through 6.4 well can still have done nothing that touches 6.5.
- **CAST names the remedy category without a protocol.** "Community-based restorative
  justice practices" is named as an example of the kind of repair called for; the
  staged source does not specify how to run one, who facilitates it, or what counts
  as done. A team that wants an implementable playbook, not a design principle, will
  not find it on this page.
- **This is a UDL design commitment, not a statement about legal nondiscrimination
  obligations.** UDL is a design framework without enforceable success criteria;
  separate legal duties around discrimination exist independently of anything on this
  page. Do not read adopting 6.5 as satisfying, or as a substitute for, compliance
  obligations under other law.
- **No effect-size or outcome evidence for 6.5 was retrieved.** The general caution
  about UDL's evidence base applies with the same force here as anywhere else in the
  wiki. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
- **The source's research link was not retrieved**, consistent with the rest of
  Guideline 6.
