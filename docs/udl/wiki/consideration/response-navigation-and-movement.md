# 4.1 Vary and Honor the Methods for Response, Navigation, and Movement

## Summary

Consideration 4.1 sits under [[interaction]] (Guideline 4) in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. Guideline 4 was called "Physical Action"
in UDL 2.2; 3.0 renamed it **Interaction**, and "Physical Action" is not current
vocabulary. CAST's one-line brief for 4.1 is "Interact with tools and environments
that make learning physically accessible." It is about the physical mechanics of
doing a task: how a learner moves, selects, and responds, not what the task asks
them to know or produce.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 4.1 when a learner understands the material and knows what they want to do,
but the physical act of doing it (clicking, dragging, writing by hand, turning a
page, moving through a room) is the barrier.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| They cannot perceive the material at all | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |
| The interaction methods are varied, but their own assistive technology can't reach the material, isn't available, or they haven't been taught to use it | [[access-to-assistive-technologies]] (4.2) |
| They can physically respond, but the medium of the response is the barrier (writing vs. speaking vs. drawing) | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) |
| The tools for building or composing the response are too limited | [[tools-for-construction-and-composition]] (5.2) |
| The room or environment itself feels unsafe or distracting | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |

The diagnostic question is "**is the barrier in the motor act of navigating or
responding, separate from perceiving, understanding, or choosing what to
express?**" CAST draws this boundary itself: Guideline 4's own description names
"page turning, mouse/keyboard use" as the kind of thing that restricts learners with
physical disabilities, blindness, dysgraphia, or executive-functioning support
needs. That is a different problem from Guideline 5's, which is about which
*medium* of expression a learner uses, not whether they can physically operate the
interface at all.

## How it works

CAST's own framing:

> "Learners differ widely in the ways they navigate and move through the physical
> environment, information, and activities. To reduce barriers to learning, it is
> essential to embed flexibility in the means for response, navigation, and
> movement."

The guideline also asks designers to "reflect[ing] on biases and assumptions
regarding these different means; it is important to honor the multiplicity of ways
to engage and embody the learning process." The "honor" in the consideration's
title is not decorative. It is a direct instruction not to treat one interaction
method (typically mouse-and-keyboard, or pen-and-paper) as the default that other
methods merely accommodate.

CAST's design options for 4.1, quoted directly from the source:

1. "Embed flexibility in the requirements for rate, timing, speed, and range of
   motor action required to interact with instructional materials, physical
   manipulatives, and technologies."
2. "Offer options for physically responding or indicating selections (e.g.,
   alternatives to marking with pen and pencil, alternatives to mouse control)."
3. "Offer options for physically interacting with materials by hand, voice, single
   switch, joystick, keyboard, or adapted keyboard."
4. "Embed flexibility into the design of the physical space (e.g., flexible seating
   and positioning, lighting, etc.)"

Note that only the first three concern digital or material interaction. The fourth is
about the physical classroom itself. 4.1 is not exclusively a screen-and-software
consideration.

## In practice

**Digital task, worked pass.** A matching activity asks learners to drag terms onto
definitions. A mouse-drag-only implementation fails 4.1 outright: there is exactly
one motor pathway. Applying the design options means adding a keyboard path (tab to
an item, press enter to select, tab to a target, press enter to place), a
single-switch-compatible scanning path, and a voice-command path ("select
photosynthesis, select definition two"). None of these change what is being
assessed (matching a term to its definition); they change how the selection gets
made physically. That is the 4.1 move: vary the motor act, hold the task constant.

**Timing and range of motion.** Option 1 covers something easy to miss: a task with a
fixed time limit or a required range of motion (drag across the full screen, hold a
key down) embeds a physical-action requirement that has nothing to do with the
construct being taught. Widening the timing window or shortening the required range
of motion is a 4.1 move even when no alternative input device is involved.

**Physical space.** A classroom with fixed desks, single-height counters, and
overhead fluorescent lighting only offers one way to be positioned in the room.
Flexible seating, adjustable-height work surfaces, and controllable lighting are the
same consideration applied to a room instead of a screen.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **4.1 is about the design of the interaction, not whether the tool exists.**
  Offering several input methods in the design does not guarantee a learner has
  access to the device, software, or training needed to use any of them: that
  question belongs to [[access-to-assistive-technologies]] (4.2), the companion
  consideration under the same guideline. The two are frequently run together; they
  are not the same move.
- **Accessibility law and this consideration are not the same thing.** Following 4.1
  is a design choice with no success criteria and no enforcement mechanism.
  Accessibility obligations under Section 504 and ADA Title II are legally required
  and enforceable, and the WCAG technical standard they point to (the specific
  version depends on the regulation) is testable and carries specific success
  criteria. Varying interaction
  methods under 4.1 does not by itself establish legal accessibility compliance, and
  a legally compliant interface is not automatically a good 4.1 design. See
  [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]].
- **Do not confuse this with Guideline 5.** 4.1 varies the physical means of
  responding or navigating; Guideline 5 (Expression & Communication) varies the
  medium of what gets produced. A learner who can physically operate every input
  device but is still forced to write an essay when speech would show the same
  knowledge has a Guideline 5 problem, not a 4.1 one.
- **Disclosed gap.** The staged CAST extract for this consideration notes that
  research evidence is referenced only by a link to a separate research page; no
  specific findings, designs, or effect sizes were retrieved for 4.1 itself. Nothing
  on this page should be read as reporting an evidence base for this consideration:
  see [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for what the broader UDL evidence
  literature does and does not support.
