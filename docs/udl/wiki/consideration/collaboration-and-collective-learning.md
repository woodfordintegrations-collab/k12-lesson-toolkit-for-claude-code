# 8.3 Foster Collaboration, Interdependence, and Collective Learning

## Summary

Consideration 8.3 sits under [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) in
[[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. Its brief from CAST is "Learn from and with one
another." It treats collaborative structure (teams, defined roles, protocols for
surfacing disagreement) as a design lever for sustaining effort, not as an
enrichment activity layered on top of the real work.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 8.3 when the design question is *how* learners work relative to each
other (together, in structured roles, drawing out one another's thinking) as the
mechanism for keeping effort up over time.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| Whether a learner feels wanted in the room, independent of any task structure | [[belonging-and-community]] (8.4) |
| A lack of varied perspectives represented in the content itself | [[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3) |
| Low engagement generally, where joy or play is the missing lever | [[nurture-joy-and-play]] (7.3) |
| The goal's meaning is unclear, not the working structure around it | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |

**8.3 is not [[belonging-and-community]] (8.4), and the two are the easiest pair in
this guideline to blur.** 8.3 is a *method*: a way of organizing the work so that
learners build knowledge from and with each other. 8.4 is a *state*: whether a
learner experiences the room as one they belong in. A class can run flawless
collaborative structures while a learner still doesn't feel they belong there, and a
learner can feel entirely welcome in a class that never asks them to learn with
anyone. Neither consideration substitutes for the other.

## How it works

CAST's description:

> "Collectively generating knowledge and learning from one another's diversity of
> ideas, perspectives, and lived experiences is central to the learning process.
> Creating communities and partnerships that extend thinking while practicing mutual
> care is a means to sustain effort and persistence. Interdependence — recognizing
> our interconnectedness and mutual support — is valued by the disability community."

The last sentence is a specific attribution, not a general claim about all learners
or all communities: CAST names interdependence as a value held by the disability
community, and this page reports that as CAST's framing rather than expanding it
into a universal claim the source doesn't make.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 8.3:

- Create community agreements that emphasize learners' ideas for fostering
  collaboration, interdependence, and collective learning.
- Create teams with clear goals, roles, expectations, and responsibilities.
- Use prompts that guide learners in when and how to ask for help.
- Use prompts or protocols that guide learners to surface and share differing
  perspectives.
- Encourage and support opportunities for peer interactions and supports (e.g., peer
  tutors).
- Construct communities of learners engaged in common interests or activities or who
  identify in similar ways.
- Construct communities of learners engaged in differing interests or activities or
  who identify in differing ways.
- Encourage questions to more fully understand concepts, ideas, and perspectives.

Two of those options point in opposite directions: group learners by what they share,
*and* group learners by what differs between them. CAST does not resolve which to use
when: that choice is left as a live design decision, and this page reports it as
unresolved in the source rather than picking a side for CAST.

**A worked pass.** Redesigning a group project around this consideration starts with
the second option, not the first: clear goals, defined roles, and explicit
responsibilities per team member, because unstructured group work is where the
free-rider problem and unequal participation actually come from. Layer in a taught
script for "when and how to ask for help" (third option) and a light protocol for
surfacing disagreement (fourth option) before the project starts, not after it stalls.
Whether teams are grouped by shared interest or by deliberately differing
perspectives can then vary by project phase: common ground for early idea
generation, differing perspectives for a later critique round.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The 8.3/8.4 conflation is the most common misread in this guideline.** If a page
  in this set is being used to justify "we did group work" as evidence of belonging
  work, it is being misapplied: see [[belonging-and-community]].
- **The grouping-strategy tension (shared identity vs. differing identity) is
  unresolved in the source itself**, not smoothed over on this page.
- **Collaboration structured badly creates its own barriers.** Free-riding and
  unequal participation are real failure modes of group work; CAST's own emphasis on
  clear goals, roles, and agreements functions as the guardrail against them, not
  collaboration as a bare instruction.
- **Research not retrieved.** The source page references a CAST research section
  without detailing findings. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for the
  evidence picture at the framework level.
