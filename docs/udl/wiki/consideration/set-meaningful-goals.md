# 6.1 Set Meaningful Goals

## Summary

Consideration 6.1 sits under [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) in the Action &
Expression principle, [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. It addresses the
first move in executive function as CAST frames it in 3.0: a learner cannot plan,
organize, or monitor toward a goal that has not been formed clearly enough to act on.
6.1 is about the *skill* of formulating a workable goal, not about whether the
learner cares about it, which is a different consideration entirely (see below).

## When to reach for it

Reach for 6.1 when a learner cannot articulate what they are working toward in terms
specific enough to plan or measure against: the goal exists somewhere, but not in a
form that can drive action.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| They have a clear goal but don't see why it matters to them | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |
| The goal is set, but they can't break it into a plan or foresee obstacles | [[anticipate-and-plan-for-challenges]] (6.2) |
| The goal itself, not the plan to reach it, is up for negotiation | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| They have a goal and a plan but can't tell if they're getting there | [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4) |
| The problem is holding onto the information the goal requires, not the goal statement itself | [[organize-information-and-resources]] (6.3) |

The diagnostic question: does this learner have a goal that is well-formed enough to
plan and measure against, or is the trouble somewhere else in the chain: motivation,
execution, or memory load?

## How it works

CAST's definition, quoted in full because both halves of it matter:

> "Setting meaningful goals that are both challenging and motivating is a critical
> part of the learning process. It is important to set goals that are big enough to
> capture the vision and complexity of what is being learned, but are specific enough
> to be clear, objective, and measurable. Articulating a goal helps to develop a plan
> to achieve it and to identify the tools or resources that will best support the
> learning along the way. Knowing the goal helps to monitor progress, recognize when
> to shift strategies or direction, and to give meaningful feedback."

Note the tension CAST builds into its own definition: a goal must be "big enough to
capture the vision" and simultaneously "specific enough to be clear, objective, and
measurable." Those pull in opposite directions, and the source does not say how to
resolve the trade-off; see Gotchas.

**6.1 is not 8.1, and the wiki keeps them separate on purpose.** [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]]
sits under Sustaining Effort & Persistence, in Engagement: it is about *why a goal
matters* to a learner, so they stay motivated to pursue it. 6.1 sits under Strategy
Development, in Action & Expression: it is about *whether the goal is formed well
enough to act on*, independent of whether the learner is invested in it. A learner
can have a crisp, measurable goal (6.1 satisfied) and still not care about it (8.1
unaddressed); a learner can feel deep purpose in a goal too vague to plan against
(8.1 satisfied, 6.1 unaddressed). The two nodes are easy to conflate because both
guideline briefs mention "goals." They are not the same consideration, and a design
that only touches one of them is half-finished.

**A naming note on Guideline 6 itself.** "Strategy Development" was called "Executive
Functions" in UDL 2.2; never write the older name as current. Confusingly, 3.0 also
labels the third row of its 3×3 grid "Executive Function," and Guideline 6 happens to
occupy that row. The row and the guideline share a name by coincidence of the 3.0
relabeling, not because they are the same thing. The Executive Function row also
contains Building Knowledge (Guideline 3) and Emotional Capacity (Guideline 9). See
[[access-support-executive-function]] for the full grid.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 6.1:

- Use prompts and scaffolds to estimate effort, resources, and challenge.
- Use models or examples of the process and product of goal-setting.
- Use guides and checklists for scaffolding goal-setting.
- Post goals, objectives, and schedules in an obvious place.

**A worked pass.** A project brief reads "build a working model of the system you
studied." That is not yet a 6.1-compliant goal: it captures the vision but is not
specific enough to plan or measure against. A goal-setting checklist (the third
design option) can walk a learner from the brief to something like "build a model
that demonstrates three interacting parts I can explain to a partner, using
materials I choose, by Friday," now specific enough to plan, resource, and check
progress against, while still leaving room for what the model is *of*. See
[[apply-udl-to-a-standard]] for the parallel move applied to standards language
rather than a project brief.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **CAST does not resolve its own tension.** "Big enough to capture the vision" and
  "specific enough to be measurable" are named as both required, with no guidance on
  how to balance them when they conflict. Disclosed gap, not an omission on this page.
- **The page's own research section was not retrieved.** The source notes only that a
  research link exists on CAST's site; no findings are reported here because none were
  staged. Not estimated, not inferred.
- **Do not read this as the goal-meaning consideration.** See the 6.1/8.1 split above.
  The two are the most easily confused pair of "goal" pages in the wiki because they
  share a keyword but sit in different principles.
- **Evidence caveat.** Nothing on this page should be read as a demonstrated effect for
  goal-setting scaffolds specifically; UDL's broader evidence base carries weak designs
  and no consensus operationalization. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
