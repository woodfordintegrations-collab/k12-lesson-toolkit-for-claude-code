# 8.5 Offer Action-Oriented Feedback

## Summary

Consideration 8.5 sits under [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) in
[[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. Its brief from CAST is "Guide learning by
emphasizing the role of effort and process." CAST defines the term directly:
"Action-oriented feedback provides specific comments on ways to make progress and
take action toward the learning goal. This approach emphasizes effort and practice
over innate ability, guiding learners toward sustainable engagement and long-term
success."

## When to reach for it

Reach for 8.5 for the *form* feedback takes, once you've already decided to give it:
the wording, timing, and framing choices, not whether to assess or what to assess.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The learner can't understand the goal being fed back against | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |
| The assessment format itself can't capture what the learner knows | [[design-an-accessible-assessment]] |
| The problem is the learner's own capacity to monitor progress, not what you tell them | [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4) |
| The problem is task difficulty itself, not the feedback wrapped around it | [[optimize-challenge-and-support]] (8.2) |

## How it works

CAST defines action-oriented feedback by seven characteristics. Reported here
precisely, because this is one of the few places CAST gives near-operational detail
rather than a general design stance:

- **Specificity**: specific comments (not general praise or criticism) on ways to
  make progress and take action toward the learning goal.
- **Focus on Effort & Process**: emphasizes effort, improvement, and achieving a
  goal rather than relative performance or innate ability.
- **Development & Self-Awareness**: encourages perseverance, development of
  efficacy and self-awareness, and use of specific supports and strategies in the
  face of challenge.
- **Substantive & Informative**: substantive and informative rather than
  comparative or competitive.
- **Timely & Frequent**: frequent, timely, and delivered in real time or as close
  to performance as possible.
- **Reflective & Forward-Looking**: models how to incorporate reflection
  (identifying patterns of challenges or strengths) into positive strategies for
  future success. This is the same move [[individual-and-collective-reflection]]
  (9.3) takes up as its own consideration.
- **Risk-Taking Encouragement**: encourages risk-taking and offers another (or
  differing) perspective(s).

**A caveat this page owes you.** "Emphasize effort and process over innate ability"
is CAST's stated design position here, and it is reported as CAST's position. Whether
praising effort rather than ability actually improves outcomes is contested in the
psychological literature, and that dispute is not addressed on CAST's page for this
consideration: nothing in the source resolves it, so nothing here claims it is
settled.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 8.5:

- Offer feedback that encourages perseverance, focuses on development of efficacy
  and self-awareness, and encourages the use of specific supports and strategies in
  the face of challenge.
- Offer feedback that emphasizes effort, improvement, and achieving a goal rather
  than on relative performance.
- Offer feedback that is frequent, timely, and specific.
- Offer feedback that is substantive and informative rather than comparative or
  competitive.
- Offer feedback that models how to incorporate reflection, including identifying
  patterns of challenges or strengths, into positive strategies for future success.
- Offer feedback that encourages risk taking and offers another (or differing)
  perspective(s).

These six options largely restate the seven characteristics above in imperative
form: they are the same criteria applied, not a second, independent set.

**A worked pass.** Two comments on the same essay draft: "Great job!" is not
action-oriented: it isn't specific, doesn't point toward an action, and isn't
forward-looking. "Your topic sentences state a claim but don't yet tie back to your
evidence in paragraph 2, try restating the claim right after the quote each time"
is: it names a specific pattern (specificity), gives a concrete move (actionable),
targets process rather than judging the writer (effort/process focus), and is usable
immediately if delivered during drafting rather than after the unit closes
(timely/frequent).

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The effort-over-ability research dispute is real and is not covered by CAST's
  page.** See the disclosure above: this page reports CAST's design stance, not a
  settled empirical finding.
- **CAST does not quantify "frequent" or "timely."** No interval or threshold is
  given; the source states the direction, not a measurable target.
- **Comparative grading systems are outside this page's scope.** Letter grades,
  rank-based systems, and other inherently comparative structures sit in tension
  with "substantive and informative rather than comparative or competitive," and the
  source does not address how the two coexist. That friction is reported as
  unresolved, not designed around.
- **Research not retrieved.** The source page references a CAST research section
  without detailing findings. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for the
  broader evidence picture on UDL-aligned practice; it does not contain effect-size
  evidence specific to feedback framing.
