# 9.3 Promote Individual and Collective Reflection

## Summary

Consideration 9.3 sits under [[emotional-capacity]] (Guideline 9) in the Engagement
principle, [[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. It asks you to build structured
occasions, for individuals and for groups, to look back on progress and learn from
mistakes. CAST names this explicitly as a motivation mechanism: recognizing progress
motivates, and the absence of support for recognizing it demotivates.

As with the rest of Guideline 9, this is a design question: the occasion and the
scaffold for reflection are built into the environment; they are not a trait some
learners have and others lack.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 9.3 when what's missing is not the underlying capacity to process emotion
(that's 9.2) but the actual scheduled moment (individual, collective, or both) to
look back on what happened and what it means.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The learner lacks the underlying skill to notice and manage emotion in the first place | [[awareness-of-self-and-others]] (9.2) |
| Trust has broken and needs active repair, not review | [[empathy-and-restorative-practices]] (9.4) |
| The gap is in the ongoing tracking system itself, not the occasion to process it | [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4) |
| Feedback from outside (teacher, peer) is missing entirely, not the reflection on it | [[action-oriented-feedback]] (8.5) |

**9.2 vs. 9.3, precisely.** 9.2 develops the capacity to notice emotion and have a
strategy on hand. 9.3 is the structured occasion, a real scheduled moment, to
apply that capacity backward over a stretch of learning, alone or as a group. A
classroom can have strong 9.2 scaffolds and still fail 9.3 if reflection never
actually happens on a schedule.

## How it works

CAST's own definition:

> Creating opportunities for individual and collective reflection develops emotional
> capacity. Some learners will have a heightened awareness of their progress toward
> goals and how to learn from mistakes along the way, while other learners can benefit
> from more explicit instruction and modeling.
>
> Recognizing progress motivates learners, while lack of support in recognizing
> progress diminishes motivation. Learners benefit from multiple models and scaffolds
> of different self- and group-assessment techniques.

Two things are bound together in the title and the text: **individual** reflection
and **collective** reflection are named as two separate, both-required moves, not one
practice with a group variant. A teacher who only runs a personal reflection journal
is doing half of 9.3; a teacher who only runs whole-class retrospectives is doing the
other half.

## In practice

CAST's design options for 9.3, verbatim:

- Use devices, aids, or charts to assist individuals and groups in learning to
  collect, chart, and display data from their own progress for the purpose of
  reflection and monitoring progress.
- Use activities that include a means by which learners get feedback and have access
  to alternative scaffolds (e.g., charts, templates, feedback displays) that support
  understanding progress in a manner that is understandable and timely.
- Adopt protocols that promote individual and collective reflection.

Notice how open the third option is left. Unlike 9.4, which names specific protocols
(circle practice, a buddy bench, classroom agreements), CAST does not name a specific
individual- or collective-reflection protocol here: "adopt protocols" is as far as
the fetched text goes. That is a real gap in specificity in the source, not a
compression made for this page.

**A worked pass.** At the close of a project unit, apply 9.3 on both tracks at once:
individually, a short chart each learner fills in before self-assessment ("what
worked, what I'd change, what I'm proud of"); collectively, a whole-class retro where
the group looks at aggregated data on where most people got stuck and names one
change for the next unit together. The individual chart is not a substitute for the
group retro, and vice versa. CAST asks for both.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **This is not the progress-tracking system itself.** [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4)
  is the ongoing mechanism for tracking progress toward an executive-function goal.
  9.3 is the emotionally-oriented occasion to process what that tracking shows: the
  two are meant to work together, not substitute for each other.
- **CAST leaves the actual reflection protocol undefined here.** "Different self- and
  group-assessment techniques" and "protocols that promote... reflection" are named
  without specifying which ones, in contrast to 9.4's named protocols. A page that
  implies CAST has a preferred reflection format would be overstating the source.
- **Recognizing progress as a motivator is stated as CAST's claim, not presented here
  as an independently verified effect.** See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for what the broader UDL evidence base does
  and does not support.
