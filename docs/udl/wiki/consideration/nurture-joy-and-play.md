# 7.3 Nurture Joy and Play

## Summary

Consideration 7.3 sits under [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] in the Engagement
principle. Its one-line brief from CAST is "Spark joy and playfulness in the learning
process." This is the node most likely to get waved off as soft. CAST does not present
it that way: it grounds joy and play as supporting development across all ages, and,
for marginalized learners specifically, as something that can function as resistance
to oppressive systems.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 7.3 when the rest of the design is working (the material is accessible,
relevant, and the learner has some control) and the environment is still flat: no
exploration, no delight, no room to be playful. For some learners that flatness is a
minor loss. For others, joy is doing real psychological work, and its absence is not a
cosmetic gap.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The task doesn't connect to what the learner values | [[relevance-value-and-authenticity]] (7.2) |
| The learner has no control over how they do the work | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| The environment threatens or excludes | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |
| The learner can start but can't sustain effort over time | [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) |

A caution belongs here rather than in the table: "make it more fun" is not a
substitute for fixing a task that fails on access, relevance, or safety. Joy is a
design consideration layered on top of a sound task, not a patch for one that isn't.

## How it works

CAST's own definition:

> Learning environments that spark joy and nurture opportunities for play are central
> to the learning process. Play supports development across all ages. Engaging in play
> sparks curiosity, wonder, and imagination as learners construct new understandings
> and develop relationships.

Two claims are doing the grounding work here, and neither is a claim about niceness.
First, **play supports development across all ages**: CAST states this plainly
against the common assumption that play belongs only to early childhood. Second, play
is tied directly to how learners construct understanding and relationships, not just
to how they feel while doing it.

The second half of CAST's claim carries the identity framing the rest of Guideline 7
runs on:

> For marginalized learners, joy can represent resistance to oppressive systems.
> However, learners experience joy differently, requiring flexible learning
> environments.

CAST names a specific instance rather than speaking generally: it states that
marginalized communities (**specifically referencing Black Joy**) "find meaning
through freedom, imagination, community, and love within learning contexts." This is
reported here exactly as CAST frames it: a named claim about what joy can mean for
specific communities, not a general theory of fun, and not evidence of a measured
outcome. The staged source does not go further than this: it does not attach an
empirical finding or effect size to the claim, and this page does not manufacture one.

The line immediately after that claim is a check on overreach in the other direction:
**learners experience joy differently.** The same calibration principle that governs
[[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] and [[relevance-value-and-authenticity]] governs this
consideration too: there is no single design that produces joy for everyone.

## In practice

Design options, verbatim from the source:

- Nurture playfulness in varied forms including outdoor play, sensory play,
  imaginative play, and arts-based play
- Incorporate opportunities for exploration, experimentation, and discovery
- Create space for learners to find joy through connections to their identities, sense
  of self, and communities
- Create space for learners to take pride in their accomplishments
- Incorporate storytelling

**A worked pass.** An ecosystems unit runs as lecture plus worksheet, and engagement is
low even though the content is accessible and the goal is clear. Applying 7.3 does not
mean bolting a game onto the worksheet. It means asking which of the five listed moves
is actually missing. Here: no exploration (add a sensory station: specimens,
textures, sounds, something to touch and ask questions about), no imaginative framing
(let learners narrate a food web as characters instead of labeling a diagram), and no
storytelling (a short narrative structure, "a day in the life of a keystone species,"
rather than a list of facts). Pride-taking matters as its own listed move, distinct
from exploration: give the resulting narratives or dioramas an actual audience, even a
small one, rather than only a grade. None of this touches the construct being
assessed, understanding of ecosystem interdependence, which is exactly the point:
[[firm-goals-flexible-means]] still governs what stays fixed.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **Do not flatten this to "make it fun."** CAST's own framing is developmental (play
  supports development across all ages) and, for marginalized learners, tied
  specifically to identity and resistance, not to enjoyment as an end in itself.
  Reading 7.3 as "add a game" misses both halves of what CAST actually claims.
- **Do not overclaim beyond the source.** CAST names Black Joy as a specific example
  of joy functioning as resistance for a marginalized community. That is the claim as
  stated. This page does not extend it into a general empirical finding about
  outcomes, because the staged source does not make that finding.
- **Learners experience joy differently.** The source says this outright; a single
  playful redesign is not evidence the consideration has been met for everyone in the
  room.
- **Joy is not license to drop rigor.** A playful redesign still has to keep the
  construct being taught or assessed firm. Where "flexible" quietly becomes "lower
  expectations," see [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]].
- **Evidence caveat.** Engagement effects for UDL as a whole rest on a literature with
  weak designs and no consensus operationalization; nothing on this page should be read
  as a demonstrated effect size for this consideration specifically. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
