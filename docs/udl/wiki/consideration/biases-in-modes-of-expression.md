# 5.4 Address Biases Related to Modes of Expression and Communication

## Summary

Consideration 5.4 sits under [[expression-and-communication]] in
[[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]]. CAST's overview page condenses it to honor a
wide variety of forms of communication. It is about which modes of expression get treated
as legitimate or rigorous, not just which are technically offered, including modes CAST
describes as historically deprioritized.

**A note on this page's source.** The staged extract for this consideration came back
marked *paraphrase*, not *verbatim*: the summarizing tool condensed CAST's page rather
than preserving its exact wording, and the guiding question for this consideration
specifically could not be recovered at all. Nothing below is presented as a CAST
quotation. Where CAST's position is described, it is described in this wiki's own
words, checked against two independent extracts of the same page for consistency. For
the exact text, go to the URL in the references.

## When to reach for it

Reach for 5.4 when the problem is not "which formats are technically available" (that
is 5.1) but "which formats are treated as legitimate once offered." A classroom can
nominally allow multiple modes of expression and still grade or regard one of them as
the serious option.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| A format simply isn't available yet | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) |
| The tools to execute in an available format are missing | [[tools-for-construction-and-composition]] (5.2) |
| The learner needs scaffolded practice, not a values question | [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] (5.3) |
| The bias concerns which languages or dialects are honored generally, not modes of expression specifically | [[understanding-across-languages-and-dialects]] (2.3), [[biases-in-language-and-symbols]] (2.4) |
| The bias is about identity representation in material given *to* learners | [[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3) |

5.4 overlaps with 2.3 and 2.4 but is scoped differently: those concern the language and
symbol systems a learner receives and works with generally. 5.4 is specifically about
the mode a learner uses to communicate *out*.

## How it works

As best this wiki can reconstruct from a paraphrased source, CAST's position is that
individual and systemic bias in schools and institutions routinely ranks some forms of
expression above others regardless of what is nominally permitted. The extracts name
two illustrations: text-based communication is often treated as more rigorous than
other forms in many schools and institutions of higher education, and oral traditions
(central to how Indigenous communities pass on knowledge) have historically been
silenced or ignored in those same settings. These are described as examples of the
pattern, not an exhaustive list of it.

Both staged extracts converge on three actions CAST associates with this consideration,
though the two documents label them inconsistently (one calls them design strategies,
the other guiding questions). Treat the three items as reliably CAST's position and
the label attached to them as uncertain:

- Anticipate and examine how bias shapes which modes of expression get offered in the
  first place.
- Anticipate and examine how bias shapes which modes get selected or encouraged once
  offered.
- Communicate, in more than one way, that modes of expression aligned with the goal are
  valued equally.

Unlike 5.1–5.3, the source does not carry a bulleted list of concrete design options for
this consideration. Both extracts describe it as pointing back to 5.1–5.3 for specific
strategies and forward to a research link neither extract could resolve to citations.
That absence is reported here rather than filled in.

## In practice

**A worked pass.** A class allows submissions as typed essays, oral recordings, or
storyboards under 5.1, but the rubric is written in terms specific to text: paragraph
count, MLA citation form, transitional phrases. A learner who submits an oral recording
is now being graded against criteria that only make sense for the format they didn't
use, and the format that was nominally "allowed" is silently penalized in the grading
mechanics. Fixing this means writing the rubric to the construct: the reasoning, the
evidence, the structure of the argument, so it applies the same way regardless of
medium. That is the same separation of construct from means that
[[firm-goals-flexible-means]] names; 5.4 is the case where the separation has to reach
into the grading criteria, not just the assignment options.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The source-fidelity gap is real and disclosed, not smoothed over.** This page's
  grounding is a paraphrase. CAST's exact language, including its stated guiding
  question, was not recovered in either staged extract. Treat the substance here as
  reliable and the specific phrasing as this wiki's own.
- **This does not license ignoring the construct when the mode *is* the construct.**
  5.4 is about not silently devaluing a mode when it isn't what's being assessed. Where
  a standard specifically assesses a mode (a writing standard assessing writing) that
  is [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] territory and varying it is
  [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]], not bias correction.
- **The examples given (oral tradition, sign language relative to captioning) are
  illustrations, not the boundary of the pattern.** Treating them as the complete list
  would understate what CAST is describing.
- **No empirical claim lives on this page.** 5.4 is a values-and-design-culture
  position in the source, not a measured effect. Nothing here should be read as an
  evidence claim about outcomes.
