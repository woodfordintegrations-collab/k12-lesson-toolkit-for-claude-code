# Traps

Five ways this goes wrong. Each is a reasonable-seeming inference from something true,
which is exactly why they are common and why treating them as careless mistakes
teaches nobody anything.

| Trap | The tell |
|---|---|
| [[confusing-udl-with-differentiated-instruction]] | What changed happened *after* you met the students, in response to one learner. If the design itself did not change, that is DI. |
| [[choice-as-a-substitute-for-design]] | Every option requires the same underlying capability. Three ways to present a report that all demand fluent writing is one option wearing three hats. |
| [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]] | After the redesign, a struggling learner's result and a peer's result no longer measure the same thing. |
| [[assuming-udl-replaces-accommodations]] | You are reasoning about an individual student's legal entitlement using a design framework. |
| [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]] | You have offered several formats and concluded the material is accessible. |

## The two with legal consequences

[[assuming-udl-replaces-accommodations]] and
[[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]] are different from the other three.
Acting on the first three produces weaker teaching. Acting on these two can deprive a
student of something they are legally entitled to.

The distinction both turn on is the same: **UDL is a design framework with no success
criteria and no conformance test.** WCAG, Section 504, ADA Title II, and IDEA are
enforceable obligations with tests attached. Good universal design plausibly reduces
how often an accommodation is needed. It never discharges the duty to provide one, and
it never establishes that a material is accessible.

## What they share

Four of the five are the same error in different clothing: **treating a proxy for the
work as the work.** A choice board is a proxy for having thought about control. Three
formats is a proxy for having thought about access. A differentiated worksheet is a
proxy for having redesigned the lesson.

The framework asks a question that cannot be answered by producing an artifact: what
is the goal here, and what in this design is blocking someone from reaching it who
otherwise could? See [[firm-goals-flexible-means]].
