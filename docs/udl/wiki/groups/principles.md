# Principles

The three top-level divisions of the UDL framework. Each answers a different question
about a learning experience, and each contains three guidelines.

CAST presents Engagement first in version 3.0, even though its guidelines are numbered
7 to 9. The numbering is historical; the ordering reflects that engagement is where
design attention is meant to start.

| Principle | The question it answers | Guidelines |
|---|---|---|
| [[multiple-means-of-engagement]] | **Why** would a learner invest in this? | 7, 8, 9 |
| [[multiple-means-of-representation]] | **What** is being presented, and can it be taken in? | 1, 2, 3 |
| [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]] | **How** does a learner act and show what they know? | 4, 5, 6 |

## Reading the framework as a grid

The nine guidelines are not three independent lists. They form a 3 x 3 grid: the three
principles above are the columns, and three rows cut across them. See
[[access-support-executive-function]] for what the row axis buys you, which is a
question the column view alone cannot answer.

## One caution that belongs at this level

Each principle names something you can vary. None of them licenses varying the
**goal**. Changing what is being learned or measured is construct-shift, not UDL. The
distinction lives in [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] and
[[construct-irrelevant-variance]], and its most common failure is
[[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]].
