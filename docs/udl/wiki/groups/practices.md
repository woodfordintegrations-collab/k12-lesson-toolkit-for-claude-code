## The order that matters

[[design-a-lesson-with-udl]] establishes the sequence the other pages assume: **goal
first, then barriers, then flexible options, then aligned assessment, then iterate.**

Designing options before the goal is fixed is the most common way this work goes
wrong, and it goes wrong invisibly. Until you know what is being learned, you cannot
tell a barrier from the task itself. A student struggling to write is blocked if the
construct is argumentation and being assessed correctly if the construct is writing.
The same observation, two opposite conclusions, decided entirely by a goal you may not
have written down.

## What these pages will not give you

Real gaps in the field, reported rather than papered over:

- **No definitive method exists for separating a construct from a means embedded in a
  standard's verb.** [[apply-udl-to-a-standard]] gives you the available reasoning and
  says plainly that the literature offers only a handful of worked cases.
- **No instrument fully certifies "these materials embody UDL."**
  [[evaluate-curriculum-materials-for-udl]] names what each real instrument actually
  measures, which is in every case something narrower.
- **Implementation at scale is documented mostly by its failure modes.** Workload,
  time, professional-development burden, and cost are well-evidenced obstacles, and
  [[implement-udl-at-school-scale]] treats them as findings rather than as caveats to
  wave past.
