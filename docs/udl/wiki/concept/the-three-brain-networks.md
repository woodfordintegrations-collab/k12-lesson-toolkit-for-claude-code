# The Three Brain Networks

## Summary

CAST's claim that the three UDL principles correspond to three networks in the
learning brain: **affective → Engagement**, **recognition → Representation**,
**strategic → Action & Expression**. It is current doctrine in Guidelines 3.0, and it
is seriously disputed in the peer-reviewed literature. This page states the claim, its
exact wording, and its status. Whether it holds is adjudicated on
[[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]].

## When to reach for it

Reach for this page when you need to know **what CAST actually claims** about the brain
basis of the framework: the precise wording, whether 3.0 kept it, and the shape of the
dispute. The two situations that bring people here: you are about to cite UDL's
neuroscience basis in front of someone who knows neuroscience, or you have met the
three-networks diagram in a slide deck and want to know how much weight it bears.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| You need to know whether the neuroscience claim survives scrutiny | [[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]]: that page adjudicates, this one states |
| You want the framework's premise about differences between learners | [[learner-variability]] |
| You suspect "multiple means" is learning styles in disguise | [[udl-and-the-learning-styles-problem]] |
| You want what the three principles ask you to *do* | [[multiple-means-of-engagement]], [[multiple-means-of-representation]], [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]] |
| You want the framework's *other* axis, the rows | [[access-support-executive-function]] |
| You want to know what federal law's "scientifically valid" actually asserts | [[scientifically-valid-the-statutory-claim]] |

The distinction this page turns on: **a claim and its warrant are different objects.**
You can describe CAST's position accurately, and cite it accurately, without having
decided whether it is true. Most UDL material collapses the two.

## How it works

CAST's current statement, verbatim:

> "The three basic principles are built upon the knowledge that our learning brains are
> composed of three different networks, recognition, strategic, and affective."

Read the verb. *Built upon the knowledge that* is not "loosely analogous to": it
asserts the network structure as a known fact and derives the principles from it. That
is the whole reason the claim is contested rather than merely decorative.

The three networks as CAST defines them:

| Network | Where CAST locates it | What CAST says it does | Principle | Framing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Affective** | centre of the brain | "evaluate environments and drive motivation and engagement" | Engagement | the WHY of learning |
| **Recognition** | posterior / back regions | "sense and perceive information, transforming it into usable knowledge" | Representation | the WHAT of learning |
| **Strategic** | anterior / front regions | "plan, organize, and initiate purposeful actions" | Action & Expression | the HOW of learning |

**Status in Guidelines 3.0.** CAST continues to lead with the three networks as
foundational. What 3.0 changed is the surrounding research base. Per CAST, 3.0

> "builds upon, updates, and expands the previous research base to include a greater
> diversity of perspectives and research from the areas of sociocultural perspectives on
> learning, disability studies, and asset-based pedagogies."

The staged source is explicit about how to read this: it is **a softening of the
neuroscience-first framing, not its removal.** Earlier UDL materials (2011 and before)
led more exclusively with brain networks; current versions set neurobiology alongside
variability, culture, and systems of exclusion. Two misreadings to avoid in both
directions:

- **"3.0 dropped the brain networks."** False. They remain CAST's stated foundation.
- **"3.0 reasserted the neuroscience."** Also false. The research base was broadened
  precisely so that neuroscience is no longer carrying the framework alone.

## In practice

The practical question is how much weight to put on the mapping when you use it.

**What the mapping legitimately buys you.** It explains why there are three principles
rather than four or seven: the number is not arbitrary, it is inherited from the model.
It gives the WHY / WHAT / HOW gloss, which is the fastest way to orient someone new to
the framework. And it tells you that Engagement is treated as a first-class design
surface rather than a motivational garnish on the other two.

**What it does not buy you.** It licenses no inference from brain organization to a
specific instructional decision. Nothing in the design guidance changes if the networks
turn out to be a poor model of cortical organization: 7.1 still asks you to embed
authentic choices aligned to the goal, 1.2 still asks you to make key information
perceptible by more than one route. The design moves stand or fall on their own
evidence, which is a separate question taken up in
[[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]]. This is the single most useful thing to
understand about this page: **the framework's architecture depends on the model; the
framework's advice does not.**

**A worked pass.** A district PD deck opens with a brain diagram and the line "UDL is
brain-based." A teacher with a psychology background asks what the evidence is. The
weak response is to defend the diagram, which puts you in an argument you will lose on
the merits. The strong response is three sentences: this is CAST's stated foundation and
here is their exact wording; the neuroscience is contested in the published literature
and CAST has not formally answered the critique; the guidelines themselves are worth
evaluating on their own evidence rather than on the brain claim. Then route the
conversation to [[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]] and keep the PD on the design work.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The claim is disputed in peer-reviewed venues, not just on blogs.** The strongest
  published statement is Murphy (2021): *"[CAST's] claims about brain function remain an
  untested hypothesis rather than an established fact. The framers of UDL claim that its
  principles relate to brain function, but this is a hypothesis, not an established
  fact."* Published in *Policy Futures in Education*, 19(1).
- **The oversimplification charge is specific, not vague.** Boysen (2024), as recorded in
  the staged critique source: *"CAST's brain model is overgeneralized and implausible
  given current understanding of neuroscience. A major flaw in the neuroscience
  justification of UDL is its oversimplified summary of brain function, using this broad
  generality to justify one third of their educational framework by pointing to three
  fourths of the cerebral cortex."* The staged source flags that the exact Boysen
  attribution would need direct paper access to confirm.
- **The thin-citation charge.** The staged source records this analysis of CAST's own
  materials verbatim: *"Only 1% of the 1,442 unique sources cited by CAST represented
  neuroscience, and 75% of UDL guidelines had no neuroscientific sources. The
  neuroscientific sources CAST cited mostly demonstrated activity across multiple brain
  regions, which contradicts their proposed alignment of guidelines with three distinct
  brain areas."* **Disclosed gap:** the staged source states these figures appear across
  multiple critiques but that the original source document was not verified via primary
  access. Treat the numbers as reported, not as independently confirmed here.
- **CAST has not published a peer-reviewed response.** The staged critique source records
  that no published peer-reviewed CAST reply to Murphy or Boysen on the neuroscience
  point was located. Absence of a located reply is not proof of absence, but it means
  there is no counter-paper to cite.
- **The defense on record is a reframing rather than a rebuttal.** Proponents restate the
  model as "aligned with" neuroscience rather than proven by it. The staged source
  assesses this as a weak defense: it concedes the rhetorical ground without engaging the
  substantive objection that the three-network division misdescribes brain function.
- **Do not adjudicate this on this page, and do not let anyone adjudicate it from a
  slide.** Weighing the claim against its critics, including what follows for practice
  and for the word "scientifically valid" in federal statute, belongs to
  [[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]]. This page's job is to make sure that when you argue
  about it, you are arguing about what CAST actually said.
