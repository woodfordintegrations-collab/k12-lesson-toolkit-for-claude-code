# Learner Agency

## Summary

Learner agency is the stated goal of UDL in Guidelines 3.0. It replaced "expert
learners", the goal statement carried by version 2.2 and every version before it. The
phrase "expert learners" was not softened or supplemented. It was removed, for stated
reasons that go to what UDL thinks learning is for. Everything in the framework points
at this outcome, which is why getting the current wording right matters more than it
looks.

## When to reach for it

Reach for this page when you need the framework's **goal**, not one of its design moves:
when you are writing the sentence that says what UDL is trying to produce, or when you
have met "expert learners" in a book, a course, or a district slide deck and need to know
whether it is current. It is not current. It has not been current since 30 July 2024.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| You want the design move that develops agency in a task | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| You think agency means offering more choices | [[choice-as-a-substitute-for-design]]: it does not, and this is the common failure |
| You want the premise about learner differences, not the goal | [[learner-variability]] |
| You want the goal-vs-means distinction inside a single lesson | [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] |
| You want to know whether UDL achieves this goal | [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] |
| You want to know whether "agency was developed" is measurable | [[measuring-udl-implementation-fidelity]] |

The diagnostic question: **am I naming what UDL is for, or naming something UDL does?**
Agency is the former. Choice, flexibility, and multiple means are the latter, and they
are in service of it.

## How it works

CAST's goal statement, verbatim:

> "The goal of UDL is **learner agency** that is purposeful & reflective, resourceful &
> authentic, strategic & action-oriented."

And CAST's definition of the term itself, from the UDL Goal page:

> "learner agency, the capacity to actively participate in making choices in service of
> learning goals"

Two things in that definition do work. **Actively participate** rules out agency as
something an educator hands over; it is a capacity the learner exercises.
**In service of learning goals** rules out agency as free choice; the choices are bound
to the goal, which is the same constraint that governs
[[firm-goals-flexible-means]] and [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]].

### What it replaced

Version 2.2's goal was to develop "expert learners" who are

> "resourceful and knowledgeable, strategic and goal-directed, purposeful and motivated."

Set the two triads side by side and the shape of the revision is visible: one word in
each pair survived, one was replaced.

| 2.2: expert learners | 3.0: learner agency |
|---|---|
| purposeful and **motivated** | purposeful & **reflective** |
| resourceful and **knowledgeable** | resourceful & **authentic** |
| strategic and **goal-directed** | strategic & **action-oriented** |

That comparison is this page's reading of the two verbatim statements, not CAST
commentary: CAST does not publish a gloss on the word-level swaps. What it shows is
that the revision kept the skeleton and changed what each attribute is aimed at:
knowledge became authenticity, motivation became reflection, being directed at a goal
became acting.

### Why the change was made

This is the part that is usually skipped, and it is the part that makes the change
substantive. CAST records that stakeholders in the update process raised the following
about "expert learners", verbatim:

> "The term 'expert' implies ideas of exclusivity and a finite ending to the learning
> process and devalues the importance of collectively generating knowledge."

and

> "The conceptualization of 'expert learning' devalues the importance of collectively
> generating knowledge and fails to recognize the brilliance inherent in every learner."

Three distinct objections are packed in there, and each one lands somewhere different:

1. **Exclusivity.** "Expert" is a status not everyone attains. A goal phrased as a status
   sorts learners into those who reached it and those who did not.
2. **A finite ending.** Expertise terminates. Agency does not: you can exercise more or
   less of it, always, which makes it a description of how someone learns rather than a
   destination they arrive at.
3. **Deficit framing.** If the goal is to become expert, the learner who is not yet
   expert is defined by what they lack. CAST's own statement of the alternative:
   *"UDL aims to change the design of the environment rather than to situate the problem
   as a perceived deficit within the learner."* The goal change and the barriers doctrine
   are the same move: see [[barriers-and-learner-environment-mismatch]].

The staged version history also records that the new goal emphasises learners learning
**together** and sharing experiences, and recognising **the brilliance inherent in every
learner** rather than implying assimilation toward a single model of the good learner.

**This is a reframing, not a rebrand.** A rebrand would swap the label and keep the
target. This swapped the target: from a state a learner reaches to a capacity a learner
exercises, and from an individual achievement to something generated collectively.

## In practice

**Citing it.** Use the full statement, and date the version. CAST's own recommended
citation is:

> "CAST (2024). CAST Universal Design for Learning Guidelines version 3.0. Retrieved
> from https://udlguidelines.cast.org"

Prior versions remain downloadable from CAST's Downloads page, and 2.2 PDFs are still
live at direct URLs, so citing "the UDL Guidelines" without a version number is now
genuinely ambiguous rather than merely imprecise.

**Reading the goal as a design test.** The three attribute pairs are usable as a check on
a unit you have already designed. Did the design give learners anything to be *reflective*
about: any point at which they look back at their own work? Is the work *authentic*, or
is it authentic-shaped? Are they *action-oriented*, meaning they get to do something with
the strategy, or does the strategy stop at being taught? A design can offer a great deal
of choice and still fail all three.

**A worked pass.** A department writes its UDL statement: "our goal is to develop expert
learners who are resourceful and knowledgeable." Everything about that sentence is
2.2. Correcting it is not a find-and-replace, because the sentence commits the department
to an endpoint. The 3.0 version ("our goal is learner agency: the capacity to actively
participate in making choices in service of learning goals") commits them to something
they have to build conditions for in every unit, and which no learner ever finishes. The
second sentence is harder to satisfy, which is the point of the revision.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Expert learners" is still everywhere in third-party material, and will be for
  years.** Books, courses, university teaching-centre pages, and district PD decks
  written before mid-2024 carry it, and much material written after 2024 still copies it
  from earlier sources. Encountering the phrase tells you the material is describing 2.2,
  which is a useful dating signal for the *rest* of that material too: expect
  "checkpoints", "provide options", and the old guideline names alongside it.
- **Agency is not choice.** CAST's definition binds choices to learning goals. A choice
  board with no goal behind it produces selection, not agency: that failure has its own
  page, [[choice-as-a-substitute-for-design]], and the design move done properly is
  [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]].
- **Agency is not a synonym for independence.** The stated rationale explicitly values
  learners learning together and collectively generating knowledge. A design that
  maximises solo work can reduce agency in CAST's sense while looking like it increases
  it. [[collaboration-and-collective-learning]] (8.3) is the counterweight.
- **The goal has no success criteria.** CAST states the goal but publishes no threshold,
  instrument, or test for when a learner has it. This is not an omission in this page.
  It is the framework-wide operationalization problem that
  [[measuring-udl-implementation-fidelity]] takes up, and it is the reason "we developed
  learner agency" is not a claim anyone can currently check.
- **Stating a goal is not evidence of reaching it.** Nothing on this page should be read
  as showing that UDL-designed instruction produces agency. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
- **Disclosed gap.** CAST's FAQ references a side-by-side comparison of 2.2 and 3.0
  available "upon request", but the document is not publicly posted and was not
  retrieved. The rationale quoted above is what CAST has published; a fuller account of
  the deliberation behind the goal change is not available from public sources.
