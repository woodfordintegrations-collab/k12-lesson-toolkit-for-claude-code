# The Access / Support / Executive Function Rows

## Summary

The nine UDL guidelines are not three lists of three. They are a **3 × 3 grid**: three
principles running down the columns, and three rows (**Access**, **Support**,
**Executive Function**) running across them. Almost everyone learns the columns and
never learns the rows, which means they can only ever ask "is this a Representation
problem?" and never "is this an access problem?" This page is about the second question.

## When to reach for it

Reach for the row axis when the thing that is wrong with a lesson is **a kind of
difficulty rather than a kind of content**. The columns sort by what the design touches
(the why, the what, the how). The rows sort by how far into the learning a learner is
getting stuck: at the door, mid-task, or at the level of running their own learning.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| A learner cannot get into the material at all | read **across the Access row**: guidelines 1, 4, and 7 |
| A learner is in, but cannot make the work go | read **across the Support row**: guidelines 2, 5, and 8 |
| A learner can do the task but cannot run their own learning | read **across the Executive Function row**: guidelines 3, 6, and 9 |
| You want a single guideline in depth | the guideline's own page, e.g. [[perception]] or [[interaction]] |
| You want the framework's stated goal | [[learner-agency]] |
| You want the principle-level (column) view | [[multiple-means-of-engagement]] and its two siblings |

The row question is the faster diagnostic in a real classroom, because a learner who is
locked out is locked out in more than one column at once. A student who cannot decode the
text, cannot operate the interface, and does not see themselves anywhere in the material
has three Access-row problems, and no amount of good work in the Support row will reach
them.

## How it works

### The grid

| Row | Representation (the WHAT) | Action & Expression (the HOW) | Engagement (the WHY) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Access** | **1.** [[perception]] | **4.** [[interaction]] | **7.** [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] |
| **Support** | **2.** [[language-and-symbols]] | **5.** [[expression-and-communication]] | **8.** [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] |
| **Executive Function** | **3.** [[building-knowledge]] | **6.** [[strategy-development]] | **9.** [[emotional-capacity]] |

The arithmetic is the easiest way to hold it: **Access = 1, 4, 7. Support = 2, 5, 8.
Executive Function = 3, 6, 9.**

Columns are ordered here by guideline number so that pattern is visible. CAST's own site
presents **Engagement first**, so the printed graphic organiser puts the 7–8–9 column on
the left. Engagement has led since version 2.1 (2014), when it was shifted to the first
principle. Same grid, different reading order.

### What reading across a row buys you

Take the Access row. Its three cells ask the same question about three different
surfaces:

- **1. Perception**: can the learner take the information in at all? CAST's brief for
  Guideline 1 is that key information must be "equally perceptible to all learners".
- **4. Interaction**: can the learner physically act on and navigate the environment?
  CAST: "Design materials and physical environments that ensure access and participation
  for every learner."
- **7. Welcoming Interests & Identities**: can the learner enter *affectively*? CAST:
  learners' interests and identities "including intersecting dimensions such as
  race/ethnicity, culture, gender, language, and disability, must be recognized,
  sustained, and valued."

That third cell is the one people miss, and it is the row's best argument. Guideline 7 is
not a motivational nicety bolted onto the engagement column: **positionally it is the
same kind of thing as being able to see the screen.** A learner who is not recognised in
the room has not been let in, in exactly the sense that a learner who cannot read the
slide has not been let in. You only see that if you read across.

The rows work the same way in the other two bands. The Support row (2, 5, 8) is
mid-process: the learner is in and the work has to be made to go, by clarifying the
symbols, opening up the modes of expression, and sustaining the effort. The Executive
Function row (3, 6, 9)
is about the learner running their own learning: building usable knowledge, developing
strategies, managing emotional capacity. That row is where the framework points at
[[learner-agency]] most directly.

### The rename from 2.2

| Version 2.2 rows | Version 3.0 rows |
|---|---|
| Access | **Access** |
| Build | **Support** |
| Internalize | **Executive Function** |

Only the top row kept its name. The row axis also has its own version history worth
knowing: 2.1 (2014) reorganised the horizontal rows, and 2.2 (2018) reordered them based
on practitioner feedback, moving the **Access row to the top**. The current top-to-bottom
reading order is therefore itself a practitioner-driven decision, not an accident of
layout.

## In practice

**Use the rows as the first cut, the columns as the second.** Given a stuck learner, ask
the row question first (where are they stuck: at the door, in the work, or in running
the work?), then use the column to find which of the three cells in that row applies.
This is faster and less error-prone than the usual route, which is to guess a principle
and then hunt down its guidelines.

**A worked pass.** A student is failing a science unit built around a lab report. The
column-first reading produces a shrug: it could be Representation (the reading), it could
be Action & Expression (the writing), it could be Engagement (they seem checked out). The
row-first reading asks what is actually happening. If they never start the reading, that
is Access: check Guideline 1 for perceptibility, Guideline 4 for whether they can
operate the materials at all, and Guideline 7 for whether anything in the unit
acknowledges them. If they read it and then stall halfway through writing, that is
Support: Guideline 5 on modes of expression, Guideline 2 on the notation, Guideline 8 on
sustaining the effort. If they can produce a report when told exactly what to do but
cannot plan one themselves, that is Executive Function: Guideline 6 on strategy,
Guideline 3 on building usable knowledge. Three different diagnoses, three different
sets of moves, and the columns alone would not have separated them.

**Use the grid to audit coverage.** A unit that is strong down one column and empty
across a row has a predictable failure profile: it serves learners who can already get
in. Reading a design against the rows finds gaps that reading against the principles
hides, because it is entirely possible to hit all three principles and still have done
nothing in the Access band.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The naming collision, which is the single biggest source of confusion here.**
  Guideline 6 is called **Strategy Development**, but the row it sits in is called
  **Executive Function**. Worse: in version 2.2, Guideline 6 itself was named **Executive
  Functions**. So a practitioner holding a 2.2 mental model hears "executive function" and
  reaches for one guideline in one column, when in 3.0 it names a row spanning guidelines
  3, 6, and 9. If someone says "that's an executive function issue", establish which they
  mean before answering.
- **The collision is a symptom of a real restructuring, not just a renaming.** The staged
  version history records that executive function was *"integrated across multiple
  guidelines instead of standing alone, better reflecting neuroscience research."* Note
  the attribution: that wording comes from a **secondary analysis (Novak Education), not
  from CAST directly**, and the phrase "better reflecting neuroscience research" is an
  editorial characterisation: the underlying neuroscience framing is itself contested,
  see [[the-three-brain-networks]].
- **Disclosed gap: CAST's row labels are explicit in the sources for one column only.**
  The staged version history spells out the row assignment inside the Engagement column
  (Access = Welcoming Interests & Identities, Support = Sustaining Effort & Persistence,
  Executive Function = Emotional Capacity) and states the rows as a framework-wide
  organisational axis. For Representation and Action & Expression, the row assignment
  follows from each guideline's position in the same ordering rather than from a labelled
  quotation in the staged extracts. The grid above is consistent with everything in the
  sources; the per-cell labelling for columns 1 and 2 was not separately quoted.
- **Disclosed gap: no CAST definition of the row labels was retrieved.** The sources give
  the row *names* and their membership, but not a CAST definition of what "Support" means
  as distinct from "Executive Function". The readings offered above are this page's gloss
  on the guideline descriptions, not CAST doctrine, and you should not quote them as CAST's.
- **The rows are not a sequence you complete.** Nothing in the sources says Access is
  finished before Support begins. A learner can be fully in the Executive Function band
  on one task and locked out at Access on the next: that is
  [[learner-variability]] operating within a single person.
- **Old graphic organisers still circulate.** 2.2 PDFs and graphic organisers remain
  available at direct URLs on CAST's Downloads page, so a grid labelled
  Access / Build / Internalize is not a forgery, it is an old version. Date the artefact
  before correcting whoever handed it to you.
