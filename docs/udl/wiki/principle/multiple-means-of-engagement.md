# Design Multiple Means of Engagement

## Summary

Engagement is the "why" of learning: affect, motivation, and the identities learners
bring into the room. It is the first of UDL's three principles and contains
[[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] (Guideline 7), [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]]
(Guideline 8), and [[emotional-capacity]] (Guideline 9). CAST's own framing: "Affect
represents a crucial element to the learning process, and learners differ markedly in
what sparks their motivation for learning."

## When to reach for it

Reach for this principle when the barrier is not that a learner *can't* do the work but
that they are not investing in it: the block sits in motivation, identity, effort, or
emotional bandwidth rather than in perceiving information or acting on it.

This page's job is to route you to the right guideline, not to repeat any of them:

| The actual problem | Where to go |
|---|---|
| Capable but not investing: the task is being done to them, or doesn't feel like theirs | [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] (7) |
| Can start but the effort collapses over time, or the goal doesn't feel worth sustaining | [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (8) |
| Anxiety, low expectations, ruptured trust, or an inability to regulate is what's in the way | [[emotional-capacity]] (9) |
| They cannot perceive or decode the material at all | [[multiple-means-of-representation]] |
| They can access it but cannot navigate, act on it, or show what they know | [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]] |

Each guideline page carries its own routing table down to its considerations. This page
stops at "which of the three."

## How it works

CAST's description of the principle, in full:

> "Affect represents a crucial element to the learning process, and learners differ
> markedly in what sparks their motivation for learning." ... "There is not one means
> of engagement that will be optimal for all learners in all contexts; multiple options
> for engagement are essential."

Version 3.0 adds an explicit claim that 2.2 did not centre: learners bring **multiple
and intersecting identities** into the learning environment, and those identities are
part of what "engagement" has to account for, not a separate concern.

**A numbering note, because it trips people up.** Engagement's guidelines are numbered
7, 8, 9 (the highest numbers in the framework), but CAST presents Engagement *first*
among the three principles, ahead of Representation and Action & Expression. This is
not new to 3.0: the version history records that the 2.1 release in 2014 "shifted
Engagement to first principle" while the underlying numbering (inherited from the
original 1.0/2.0 structure, where Representation led) never changed. Read the guideline
numbers as a legacy index, not a statement about which principle CAST considers
primary.

**The grid.** This principle is one column of UDL's 3×3 structure; its three guidelines
are the rows: Guideline 7 is Access, Guideline 8 is Support, Guideline 9 is Executive
Function. See [[access-support-executive-function]] for what those rows mean read
across all three principles, not just down this one.

CAST also ties this principle to the **affective networks**, in the center of the
brain, which it describes as networks that "evaluate environments and drive motivation
and engagement." That is one leg of CAST's three-network model: affective networks to
Engagement, recognition networks to Representation, strategic networks to Action &
Expression. See [[the-three-brain-networks]] for the current status of that claim and
the dispute over it, and [[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]] for whether the neuroscience
grounding itself holds up; this page states the mapping, not its evidentiary weight.

## In practice

Use the routing table above, then open the relevant guideline page, which routes again
into its considerations. A single stuck learner can look like any of the three
guidelines depending on how you read the case: the diagnostic work is in telling them
apart, not in picking whichever one occurs to you first.

**A worked pass.** A student stops turning in homework. Three genuinely different
diagnoses live under this one principle:

- They see the assignment as something done to them, disconnected from anything they
  chose or find relevant: that's [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]], likely
  [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) or [[relevance-value-and-authenticity]] (7.2).
- They start the work and abandon it partway, or the goal has never been made concrete
  enough to sustain effort toward: that's [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]],
  likely [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) or [[optimize-challenge-and-support]]
  (8.2).
- They've had a bad experience with this subject or this teacher and are avoiding the
  anxiety of trying: that's [[emotional-capacity]], likely
  [[expectations-beliefs-and-motivations]] (9.1).

Same symptom, three different guidelines, three different design responses. Guessing
wrong wastes a redesign cycle on a problem that was never there.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The renames under this principle are substantive, not cosmetic.** Guideline 7 was
  "Recruiting Interest" in 2.2, a name about sparking initial curiosity. Guideline 9
  was "Self-Regulation", a name that put the regulatory burden on the learner. Both
  3.0 names reflect real conceptual shifts, covered on their own pages, and both feed
  [[learner-agency]], the framework's 3.0 goal that replaced "expert learners."
- **Evidence for this principle is not established through outcome measures.** The
  UDL evidence base overall leans on self-report and process measures rather than
  measured learning outcomes: the earliest comprehensive meta-analysis found
  improvements in learning process but could not demonstrate effects on measurable
  educational outcomes. That is a disclosed, field-wide gap, not a flaw specific to
  this page, and the staged sources do not break that gap down by principle: this
  wiki cannot say from them whether Engagement's evidence is weaker than
  Representation's or Action & Expression's specifically, only that the field-wide
  problem applies here too. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for what the
  evidence does and does not support. It does not mean the principle is wrong. It
  means claims of a demonstrated effect on outcomes should be held to the same
  "verbatim or absent" numbers standard as anything else in this wiki, and right now
  the numbers mostly aren't there.
- **"Engagement" in casual use often means "make it fun."** CAST's Engagement
  principle is broader and more structural than that: it includes identity,
  belonging, goal clarity, and emotional capacity, not just novelty or gamification.
  [[nurture-joy-and-play]] (7.3) is one consideration among thirteen, not the
  principle's summary.
