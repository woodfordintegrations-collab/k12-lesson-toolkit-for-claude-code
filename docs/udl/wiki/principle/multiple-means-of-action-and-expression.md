# Design Multiple Means of Action & Expression

## Summary

Action & Expression is the "how" of learning: navigating the learning environment and
demonstrating what you know. It is the third of UDL's three principles and contains
[[interaction]] (Guideline 4), [[expression-and-communication]] (Guideline 5), and
[[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6). CAST's own framing: "Learners differ in the
ways they navigate a learning environment, approach the learning process, and express
what they know." No single means of action and expression works optimally for every
learner, so multiple options are essential.

## When to reach for it

Reach for this principle when a learner has the material and wants to work with it, but
the barrier is in **doing**: moving through the environment, operating a tool,
planning the approach, or producing an answer, rather than in perceiving the material
or wanting to engage with it in the first place.

This page's job is to route you to the right guideline, not to repeat any of them:

| The actual problem | Where to go |
|---|---|
| They cannot perceive or make sense of the material itself | [[multiple-means-of-representation]] |
| They understand the task but have no motivation to start or sustain it | [[multiple-means-of-engagement]] |
| The physical or motor act of navigating, clicking, writing, or responding is the barrier | [[interaction]] (4) |
| The medium or tool for producing and communicating their answer is the barrier | [[expression-and-communication]] (5) |
| They can act and express but cannot plan, organize, or monitor the work | [[strategy-development]] (6) |

The diagnostic question is "**can this learner act on and express what they know, once
they've perceived it and want to engage with it?**" If the honest answer is that they
can't get access to the material at all, or don't care to, this principle is not where
the problem lives: Representation and Engagement sit earlier in the causal chain, and
redesigning Action & Expression first will not repair a problem that starts upstream of
it. Each guideline page carries its own routing table down to its considerations; this
page stops at "which of the three."

## How it works

CAST ties this principle to the **strategic networks**, in the anterior/front regions
of the brain, which it describes as networks that "plan, organize, and initiate
purposeful actions." That is one leg of CAST's three-network model: affective
networks to Engagement, recognition networks to Representation, strategic networks to
Action & Expression. See [[the-three-brain-networks]] for the claim itself and
[[the-neuroscience-basis-claim]] for its current status and the dispute over it; this
page states the mapping, not its evidentiary weight.

**The grid.** This principle is one column of UDL's 3×3 structure; its three
guidelines are the rows: [[interaction]] is Access, [[expression-and-communication]]
is Support, [[strategy-development]] is Executive Function. See
[[access-support-executive-function]] for what those rows mean read across all three
principles, not just down this one.

Version 3.0 kept this principle's subject matter but reframed its language: "Design
Multiple Means of Action & Expression" replaces the older, flatter "Action &
Expression," part of a language shift across all three principles away from
"provide" and toward "design." Underneath it, two of the three guidelines were
renamed outright. Guideline 4 was "Physical Action" in 2.2 and is now
[[interaction]]. Guideline 6 was "Executive Functions" and is now
[[strategy-development]]. Guideline 5, [[expression-and-communication]], kept its name
across the revision; none of its neighbors did.

**This is the principle carrying the framework's most consequential misreading.** The
premise of Action & Expression is that *how* a learner demonstrates knowledge can be
varied without changing *what* is being measured: [[firm-goals-flexible-means]]
applied at the scale of an entire principle. That premise holds only when the mode of
expression is genuinely a means and not the construct itself. When the mode of
expression **is** the thing being assessed (a writing standard assessing writing
ability, a public-speaking rubric assessing oral delivery), varying it is not
flexibility. It is **construct-shift**: what gets measured has changed, not merely how
it gets measured, and the resulting score no longer means what the goal called for.
This is the flip side of [[construct-irrelevant-variance]]: instead of an irrelevant
barrier contaminating a measurement, a relevant one has been designed away. CAST does
not spell out this bound at the principle level in the staged material; it is a
working consequence of "firm goals, flexible means" that this wiki states explicitly,
because "vary the means" is the reflex practitioners reach for under this principle,
and the reflex is not always safe. See [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]]
for the failure mode this produces.

## In practice

There is no design-options list at the principle level; those live inside each
consideration, layers down. What belongs here is the routing move and the
construct-shift check, worked once.

**A worked pass.** A unit culminates in a research report, and a student is struggling
to produce it. Before touching Guidelines 4–6, ask what the goal actually is.

- If the goal is *evaluate sources and synthesize a claim*, the report's medium is
  means. A recorded presentation, an annotated slide deck, or a written report can all
  carry the same synthesis: [[expression-and-communication]] is fully open, and which
  specific consideration applies (medium, tool, or the fluency to execute in either)
  depends on which part of production is actually stuck.
- If the goal is *write a research report to disciplinary convention*, under a
  standard that is itself assessing written composition, the medium is the construct.
  Opening it to video or oral presentation is construct-shift, however
  well-intentioned. What remains genuinely open is [[interaction]] (can they physically
  produce the writing by whatever input method works for them) and
  [[strategy-development]] (can they plan, organize, and monitor the writing process).
  Neither of those touches the construct.
- If the student can pick a medium and plan the work but still can't get past a
  keyboard-only draft interface, that is a Guideline 4 problem and does not require
  reopening the construct question at all.

The goal decides what is open before you decide what to redesign. See
[[apply-udl-to-a-standard]] for reading standards language to find the construct.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Vary the means" is not a blanket license.** See "How it works" above; this is the
  single most important caution on this page, and it governs every consideration under
  Guidelines 4, 5, and 6.
- **This principle carries no success criteria of its own**, and neither do its
  guidelines. Guideline 4 in particular overlaps with enforceable accessibility law
  (Section 504, ADA Title II, WCAG 2.2) without being identical to it: a well-designed
  Action & Expression lesson is not proof of legal compliance, and a legally compliant
  interface is not automatically a good design under this principle. See
  [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]].
- **Evidence caveat.** Nothing on this page should be read as a demonstrated outcome
  effect for Action & Expression specifically. UDL's broader outcomes evidence rests on
  designs with weak controls and no consensus way to measure whether UDL was actually
  implemented. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
