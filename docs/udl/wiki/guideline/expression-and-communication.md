# Guideline 5: Expression & Communication

## Summary

Guideline 5 sits in [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]], the **Action &
Expression** column of CAST's 3×3 grid, in the **Support** row, the same row position
as Language & Symbols (Representation) and Sustaining Effort & Persistence
(Engagement). See [[access-support-executive-function]] for the full grid. CAST's
guiding question: "How might we design learning environments that value multiple and
novel modes of expression and communication, including those that have been
historically deprioritized?" It contains four considerations:
[[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1), [[tools-for-construction-and-composition]]
(5.2), [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] (5.3), and
[[biases-in-modes-of-expression]] (5.4).

**This guideline's name did not change in 3.0**: it was "Expression & Communication"
in 2.2 and stayed that name, the one guideline in this principle whose neighbors (4 and
6) were both renamed.

## When to reach for it

Reach for Guideline 5 when a learner can physically operate whatever interface they're
given, but the **medium, tool, or fluency required to produce and communicate an
answer** is the barrier.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The physical or motor act of navigating or responding is the barrier, not what they'd produce | [[interaction]] (Guideline 4) |
| The required output medium itself (writing vs. speech vs. film vs. sculpture) is the obstacle | [[multiple-media-for-communication]] (5.1) |
| They've picked a medium but lack the specific tool to execute in it (word processor, calculator, notation software) | [[tools-for-construction-and-composition]] (5.2) |
| They can produce output but need scaffolded, gradually-released support to get fluent at it | [[build-fluencies-with-graduated-support]] (5.3) |
| The medium is available and usable but institutionally treated as less rigorous | [[biases-in-modes-of-expression]] (5.4) |
| The barrier is in planning, organizing, or monitoring the work, not in how it gets expressed | [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) |
| The standard being assessed is itself a standard about the medium (a writing standard assessing writing) | [[firm-goals-flexible-means]], [[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]] |

The diagnostic question: "**is the barrier in which medium or tool the learner uses to
produce and communicate what they know**, separate from whether they can physically
operate the interface (Guideline 4) or plan and monitor the work (Guideline 6)?" Check
that last row of the table first: if the medium is the construct, this guideline does
not apply at all, no matter how real the struggle looks.

## How it works

CAST's official description: "There is no medium of expression that is equally suited
for all learners or for all kinds of communication. The objective is to provide
multiple modalities for expression to reduce communication barriers and support
learners to express knowledge, ideas, and concepts in the learning environment."

The four considerations divide the problem cleanly:

- **5.1** varies the *medium itself*: text, speech, film, sculpture, dance.
- **5.2** varies the *tools within a medium*: a word processor with a spell-checker is
  still writing; a graphing calculator is still math.
- **5.3** is not about choice at all. It's about **building the fluency** to perform
  well once a medium and tools are chosen, through scaffolds that get gradually
  released as skill develops.
- **5.4** turns the lens on the institution rather than the learner: which media get
  treated as rigorous and which get dismissed.

**A note on this page's own grounding.** The staged extract for the Guideline 5
overview and for 5.4 is marked *paraphrase*, not verbatim: the capture condensed
rather than preserved exact CAST wording on those two pages. The quotation above is
drawn from the framework structure map instead, captured independently and not flagged
paraphrase. The design options for 5.1, 5.2, and 5.3 quoted below are verbatim; the 5.4
content in this section is described rather than quoted, and the exact CAST wording
should be checked at the source URL if it matters for your purpose.

5.4 is worth naming directly rather than only routing to it, with that caveat in mind:
CAST's own examples include text-based communication being treated as more rigorous
than storytelling traditions, and closed captioning being prioritized over sign
language. That is a claim about institutional bias, not learner skill: a different
kind of problem from 5.1–5.3.

## In practice

**Worked pass.** A research project ends in a report.

- **5.1 (medium):** let the learner choose a video essay, a written report, or an oral
  presentation: three media that can all carry the same synthesis of sources. CAST's
  own list of media runs far wider: "text, speech, drawing, illustration, comics,
  storyboards, design, film, music, storytelling, dance/movement, visual art,
  sculpture, or video."
- **5.2 (tools):** within whichever medium is chosen, offer the tools that make it
  achievable. CAST names spell-checkers, word-prediction software, speech-to-text,
  outlining and concept-mapping tools, and, for a math-heavy report, calculators and
  graphing tools.
- **5.3 (fluency):** the learner has picked video but has never edited one. Scaffold it
  with differentiated models (finished examples that reach the same outcome by
  different routes) plus mentors and feedback that is gradually withdrawn as
  competence builds, not a one-shot rubric handed over on day one.
- **5.4 (bias):** the teacher does not quietly grade the oral presentation as less
  rigorous than the written report, or treat video as "not really" demonstrating the
  synthesis.

If the assignment sits under a standard that specifically assesses written
composition, none of the above is available for the medium itself. See
[[lowering-rigor-in-the-name-of-flexibility]] and [[apply-udl-to-a-standard]].

## Gotchas & constraints

- **5.1 is not 2.5, and they run in opposite directions.** 2.5 (Representation) varies
  what the learner *receives*; 5.1 (this guideline) varies what the learner
  *produces*. The two get confused because CAST uses nearly identical "multiple media"
  language for both.
- **Where the medium is the construct, opening it is construct-shift, not UDL.** See
  [[firm-goals-flexible-means]] and the principle-level warning on
  [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]].
- **This page's own source discipline:** the overview and 5.4 content above is
  paraphrase, flagged as such where it appears: treat any close-quotation reading of
  those two sections with that caveat in mind, and check the URL for exact wording if
  it matters.
- **Disclosed gap.** No effect sizes or specific research findings were retrieved for
  any of the four considerations; CAST's pages reference a research link rather than
  reporting findings directly. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
