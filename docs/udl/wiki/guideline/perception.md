# Guideline 1: Perception

## Summary

Guideline 1 sits in [[multiple-means-of-representation]], the **Representation**
column of CAST's 3×3 grid, in the **Access** row, the same row position as Welcoming
Interests & Identities (Engagement) and Interaction (Action & Expression). See
[[access-support-executive-function]] for the full grid. CAST's guiding question:
"How might we increase accessibility, flexibility, and diversity in the sharing of
information?" It contains three considerations: [[customize-the-display-of-information]]
(1.1), [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2), and
[[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3).

## When to reach for it

Reach for Guideline 1 when the problem is whether information **reaches** the learner
at all: not whether they understand its vocabulary once it arrives, and not whether
they can turn it into knowledge. It's the front door of Representation.

| The actual problem | Go to |
|---|---|
| Content reaches the learner through a workable modality, but its display is fixed: too small, too fast, wrong contrast, wrong volume | [[customize-the-display-of-information]] (1.1) |
| The learner can't perceive the content through the modality it currently arrives in at all | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |
| The content reaches the learner fine perceptually, but it excludes or flattens whose voices and experiences are represented | [[diversity-of-perspectives-and-identities]] (1.3) |
| The content is perceptible, but the vocabulary, symbols, or sentence structure carrying it is the barrier | [[language-and-symbols]] (Guideline 2) |
| The content is perceptible and understandable, but the learner isn't connecting or organizing it into knowledge | [[building-knowledge]] (Guideline 3) |

The distinction between 1.1 and 1.2 is the one people blur most: 1.2 asks "does an
alternative channel for this content exist at all," 1.1 asks "can the channel that
already exists be tuned." A video with no captions is a 1.2 gap. A video with captions
that can't be resized or recolored is a 1.1 gap.

## How it works

CAST's own definition, verbatim, names three distinct mechanisms in one guideline:

> Ensure "key information is equally perceptible to all learners by: 1) offering the
> same information through different modalities (e.g., through vision, hearing, or
> touch); 2) offering information in a format that will allow for adjustability by the
> user (e.g., text that can be enlarged, sounds that can be amplified); and 3) offering
> a variety of perspectives."

Those three mechanisms map directly onto the three considerations: modality choice is
1.2, adjustability is 1.1, and perspective variety is 1.3. The guideline's framing text
adds that multiple representations "support learners with sensory/perceptual
disabilities while benefiting others," the standard UDL move of designing for the
edge case in a way that widens access for everyone, not a special-education patch
layered on top of an otherwise-fixed lesson.

1.3 is the odd one out structurally, and worth naming as such: 1.1 and 1.2 are about
the mechanics of a channel (can you perceive it, can you adjust it), while 1.3 is about
the content traveling through that channel (whose perspectives and identities are in
it). It sits in this guideline because unrepresentative content is treated as its own
kind of perceptual barrier: a learner who never sees themselves or an authentic
version of others reflected in the material is being shut out in a way CAST groups
alongside sensory access, not because the mechanism is the same.

## In practice

The three considerations, in CAST's own one-line framing:

- **1.1**: Use accessible materials with settings that can be adjusted based on needs
  and preferences.
- **1.2**: Share information in more ways than images and text alone.
- **1.3**: Learn from multiple and varying perspectives.

Each has its own design-options list and worked example on its own page; this page
doesn't repeat them.

**A worked pass.** A biology unit on cell structure runs entirely on labeled diagrams
with a short paragraph of text underneath each one. A student flags that they're lost.
Before reaching for any design option, find out which failure this is:

- If the student is blind or low-vision and the diagrams have no text or audio
  description, there is no non-visual channel for the diagram's content at all: that's
  1.2, and the fix is a description, a tactile equivalent, or both.
- If the student can see the diagrams fine but they're rendered too small on a shared
  classroom screen, or the labels are low-contrast, that's 1.1, a customization gap,
  not an access gap.
- If every example diagram and every scientist named in the unit looks the same, and a
  student has quietly concluded this field isn't for people like them, that's 1.3, a
  representation gap that neither resizing nor an audio description touches.

Three different students in the same room can hit three different considerations off
the same diagram.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **1.1 and 1.2 are not the same failure and don't share a fix.** Treating "add a
  settings panel" as an answer to "there's only one modality" (or vice versa) is the
  most common way this guideline gets misapplied. See each consideration's own page
  for the differential.
- **This guideline is not the legal accessibility standard.** WCAG 2.2, Section 504,
  and ADA obligations overlap heavily with 1.1 and 1.2 in particular, but they are
  separate, testable requirements with their own success criteria. A page that "feels"
  perceptually flexible can still fail a technical accessibility audit. See
  [[mistaking-udl-for-accessibility-compliance]].
- **1.3 is easy to treat as a content checklist rather than a design consideration.**
  CAST frames it as authentic representation woven into the material, not a quota of
  represented identities added after the fact. The staged source doesn't elaborate
  further than the design options themselves on how to tell the difference in
  practice.
- **The source doesn't rank the three considerations.** Nothing in the staged extract
  says 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3 takes priority when a lesson has gaps in more than one at once;
  this page's worked pass sequences them for clarity, not because CAST specifies an
  order.
