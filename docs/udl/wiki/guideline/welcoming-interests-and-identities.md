# Guideline 7: Welcoming Interests & Identities

## Summary

Guideline 7 sits at **Engagement × Access** on UDL's 3×3 grid: the first row under
[[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. See [[access-support-executive-function]] for what
the Access row means across all three principles. CAST's description: "Learners'
unique interests as well as their unique identities, including intersecting dimensions
such as race/ethnicity, culture, gender, language, and disability, must be recognized,
sustained, and valued." It contains four considerations, 7.1 through 7.4.

This guideline was renamed in 3.0. It was **Recruiting Interest** in version 2.2, a
narrower name about sparking initial curiosity. The rename is substantive: 3.0 centres
learners' intersecting identities as a design input in their own right, not just a
route to attention.

## When to reach for it

| The actual problem | Where to go |
|---|---|
| The task is done to the learner rather than by them: no control over means | [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] (7.1) |
| The learner doesn't see why this connects to their life, goals, or community | [[relevance-value-and-authenticity]] (7.2) |
| Learning feels mechanical, joyless, or too risk-averse to enjoy | [[nurture-joy-and-play]] (7.3) |
| The environment itself carries bias, threat, or distraction: the learner is guarded, not just unmotivated | [[biases-threats-and-distractions]] (7.4) |

And where Guideline 7 is the wrong tool entirely:

| The actual problem | Go here instead |
|---|---|
| The learner starts but the effort doesn't hold up over time | [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) |
| The learner is emotionally flooded or can't regulate a response | [[emotional-capacity]] (Guideline 9) |
| The learner cannot perceive or decode the material in the first place | [[multiple-ways-to-perceive-information]] (1.2) |

## How it works

CAST's identity language is explicit and worth quoting rather than paraphrasing: the
guideline names "intersecting dimensions such as race/ethnicity, culture, gender,
language, and disability" as things that "must be recognized, sustained, and valued."
The version history frames this as one of three core shifts in 3.0, "identity as
variability," moving the framework beyond ability-based variability alone and putting
UDL "in conversation with other asset-based approaches," including pedagogies that
centre and sustain learners' cultural practices.

The guideline is not a silo. Guideline 8's belonging consideration explicitly borrows
from it: one of 8.4's design options is to "welcome interests and identities,"
pointing straight back here. See [[belonging-and-community]].

## In practice

The four considerations, each with its own deep page:

- **7.1 Optimize Choice and Autonomy**: variable dimensions (content, tools, rewards,
  practice/assessment opportunities, layout, sequence/timing) plus co-design with
  learners, bounded by the requirement that choice track a fixed learning goal. See
  [[optimize-choice-and-autonomy]] for the full treatment; it is this wiki's golden
  page.
- **7.2 Relevance, Value, and Authenticity**: authenticity does not require real-life
  equivalence (fiction can engage as much as non-fiction); the source stresses content
  that is culturally relevant and sustaining, not just generically "interesting."
- **7.3 Nurture Joy and Play**: framed as more than decoration: for marginalized
  learners specifically, the source names joy (citing Black Joy) as a form of
  resistance, not just enjoyment.
- **7.4 Address Biases, Threats, and Distractions**: covers both overt bias and
  subtle, chronic threat, and treats predictability (routines, previews, timers) and
  sensory load as design variables alongside bias itself.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Recruiting Interest" muscle memory will undersell this guideline.** Anyone
  trained on 2.2-era material will expect this guideline to be about grabbing initial
  attention. 3.0's version is broader: it is about whether the learner's whole
  identity is welcome in the room, not just whether the opening hook lands.
- **7.1 is the guideline most often reduced to "add a choice board."** CAST's own text
  in 7.1 says plainly that providing choice is not sufficient on its own. See
  [[choice-as-a-substitute-for-design]] for the full failure mode.
- **Evidence caveat.** As with the rest of [[multiple-means-of-engagement]], effects
  reported for this guideline's considerations lean on self-report and process
  measures rather than learning outcomes. See [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
