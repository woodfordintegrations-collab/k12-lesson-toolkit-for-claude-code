# Guideline 9: Emotional Capacity

## Summary

Guideline 9 sits at **Engagement × Executive Function** on UDL's 3×3 grid: the third
row under [[multiple-means-of-engagement]]. See [[access-support-executive-function]]
for the Executive Function row read across all three principles. It contains four
considerations, 9.1 through 9.4.

This guideline was renamed in 3.0. It was **Self-Regulation** in version 2.2, and the
rename is not cosmetic: 3.0 reframes the whole guideline from a learner trait to be
built or corrected into a question about environmental design. Its own guiding
question makes the shift explicit: "How might we design learning environments that
support and extend emotional capacity while honoring variability among learners?" The
object of the question is the environment, not the learner.

## When to reach for it

| The actual problem | Where to go |
|---|---|
| The learner doesn't believe they can succeed, or has given up before starting | [[expectations-beliefs-and-motivations]] (9.1) |
| The learner can't read their own emotional state or others', or has no strategy for frustration | [[awareness-of-self-and-others]] (9.2) |
| The learner has no way to see whether they're making progress, or no space to step back | [[individual-and-collective-reflection]] (9.3) |
| Trust has broken in the room and it needs repair, not punishment | [[empathy-and-restorative-practices]] (9.4) |

And where Guideline 9 is the wrong tool:

| The actual problem | Go here instead |
|---|---|
| The learner just needs more control over the task, not emotional support | [[welcoming-interests-and-identities]] (Guideline 7) |
| The goal itself is unclear or the challenge is miscalibrated | [[sustaining-effort-and-persistence]] (Guideline 8) |
| The gap is purely organizational (planning, goal-setting, monitoring) with no emotional component | [[strategy-development]] (Guideline 6) |

## How it works

CAST's full definition: "A critical aspect of human development is attending to one's
abilities to recognize emotions, manage thoughts and behaviors, and empathize with
others. An emotional response does not preclude rational thinking; emotions are part
of the human experience." And directly: "The design of the learning environment should
not require learners to cope with inequitable conditions."

**The rename's substance.** "Self-Regulation" named a skill located in the learner:
the implicit design question was "how do we get learners to regulate themselves better."
"Emotional Capacity," paired with its guiding question, relocates the design target to
the environment. This tracks a broader 3.0 shift the version history states directly:
"UDL aims to change the design of the environment rather than to situate the problem as
a perceived deficit within the learner." See [[learner-agency]] and
[[barriers-and-learner-environment-mismatch]] for the same move made elsewhere in the
framework.

**This guideline does not name SEL or CASEL.** The staged source is explicit on this
point: CAST does not reference the Collaborative for Academic, Social, and Emotional
Learning or CASEL terminology anywhere on the Guideline 9 pages. The competencies
covered (self-awareness, managing thoughts and behaviors, social awareness, empathy)
overlap substantially with standard SEL frameworks, but CAST keeps its own language and
its own environmental emphasis rather than adopting SEL's. If you're coming to this
guideline expecting it to be UDL's version of an SEL curriculum, or expecting a CASEL
citation, neither is here: treat the overlap as convergence, not equivalence.

## In practice

The four considerations, briefly:

- **9.1 Expectations, Beliefs, and Motivations**: prompts and rubrics for setting
  realistic personal goals and managing anxiety around them; coaches or mentors who
  model appropriate goal-setting; explicit examination of whether the environment's
  own expectations (e.g., expecting every learner to show engagement the same way)
  are themselves a source of bias.
- **9.2 Awareness of Self and Others**: differentiated scaffolds for managing
  frustration and seeking support; reframing self-talk about ability ("How can I
  improve on the areas I am struggling in?" rather than "I am not good at math.");
  space to appreciate one's own and others' personal, cultural, and linguistic assets.
- **9.3 Individual and Collective Reflection**: tools for tracking and charting one's
  own progress; protocols for both individual and group reflection, since some
  learners already have strong awareness of their own progress and others need
  explicit modeling.
- **9.4 Empathy and Restorative Practices**: CAST defines restorative practices
  precisely: "Rather than punishing learners for mistakes, restorative practices allow
  for learners to consider the impact of their actions and design an appropriate
  response to repair trust in the community." Design options include circle practice,
  accountability partners, and co-created classroom agreements that name what learners
  need to feel safe.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **"Self-Regulation" muscle memory imports the wrong frame.** Anyone trained on
  2.2-era material will read this guideline as being about fixing a learner's
  self-control. 3.0's version asks what the environment is doing to make regulation
  harder than it needs to be: a different diagnostic question with a different set of
  fixes.
- **Don't assume SEL or CASEL equivalence.** The overlap is real but CAST never states
  it; importing a CASEL competency framework wholesale onto this guideline is your
  addition, not CAST's.
- **CAST's "restorative practices" is a specific, defined term here**, not a generic
  pointer to whatever restorative-justice program a school already runs. Check that an
  existing program actually matches the non-punitive, repair-focused definition above
  before assuming 9.4 is already satisfied.
- **Evidence caveat.** As with the rest of [[multiple-means-of-engagement]], claims
  about this guideline's effects rest on a literature weighted toward self-report and
  process measures rather than learning outcomes. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]].
