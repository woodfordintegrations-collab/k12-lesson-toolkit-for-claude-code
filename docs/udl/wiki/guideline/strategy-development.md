# Guideline 6: Strategy Development

## Summary

Guideline 6 sits in [[multiple-means-of-action-and-expression]], the **Action &
Expression** column of CAST's 3×3 grid, in the **Executive Function** row, the same
row position as Building Knowledge (Representation) and Emotional Capacity
(Engagement). See [[access-support-executive-function]] for the full grid.

**Read this carefully: the guideline and the row share a name, and they are not the
same thing.** "Executive Function" is the *row*: a position that also contains
Guideline 3 (Building Knowledge) and Guideline 9 (Emotional Capacity). Guideline 6 is a
single *node* that happens to sit in that row. Making this worse, Guideline 6 itself
was literally named **"Executive Functions"** (plural) in 2.2, before 3.0 renamed it
Strategy Development. A reader coming from 2.2 vocabulary, or simply reading the row
label, has two separate reasons to assume "Executive Function" and "Guideline 6" are
interchangeable. They are not: the row spans three guidelines across three principles,
and Guideline 6 is only one of them.

It contains five considerations: [[set-meaningful-goals]] (6.1),
[[anticipate-and-plan-for-challenges]] (6.2), [[organize-information-and-resources]]
(6.3), [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4), and [[challenge-exclusionary-practices]] (6.5).

## When to reach for it

Reach for Guideline 6 when a learner can act on material and express what they know,
but struggles with **planning, organizing, or monitoring** the work itself.

| The actual problem | Where to go instead |
|---|---|
| The physical or motor act of navigating or responding is the barrier | [[interaction]] (Guideline 4) |
| The medium or tool for producing the output is the barrier | [[expression-and-communication]] (Guideline 5) |
| The learner doesn't know what the goal is | [[set-meaningful-goals]] (6.1) |
| They have a goal, but it isn't compelling enough to commit to | [[meaning-and-purpose-of-goals]] (8.1) |
| They have a goal but no plan for the obstacles ahead | [[anticipate-and-plan-for-challenges]] (6.2) |
| They're drowning in information or materials and can't keep them organized | [[organize-information-and-resources]] (6.3) |
| They can't tell whether they're making progress or when to change approach | [[monitoring-progress]] (6.4) |
| The barrier is a biased or exclusionary practice in the classroom or system, not an individual planning skill | [[challenge-exclusionary-practices]] (6.5) |
| You're looking for the Executive Function *row* generally, not this one guideline | [[access-support-executive-function]] |

The diagnostic question: "**is the barrier in planning, organizing, monitoring, or
goal-setting the work**, separate from perceiving material, physically interacting with
it, or choosing how to express it?"

## How it works

CAST frames Guideline 6 around two scaffolding moves: "(1) by scaffolding lower level
skills so they require less executive processing; and (2) by scaffolding higher level
executive skills and strategies so they are more effective and developed." The aim is
to help learners become "intentional in their short-term reactions" and able to "set
long-term goals and plan effective strategies," including "monitoring progress and
modifying strategies as needed."

Considerations 6.1 through 6.4 map onto that two-part model directly: set a goal
(6.1), plan for what will go wrong (6.2), keep the information organized (6.3), and
monitor progress against the goal (6.4): the classic executive-function skill chain,
each scaffolded so the skill itself, not the underlying content, is what's being
supported.

**6.5 is a different kind of move, not a fifth skill in the same chain.** CAST grounds
it in "deep-seated biases and systems of discrimination" that exclude marginalized
learners, and its design options run toward community sessions, naming and addressing
exclusionary practice collectively, and restorative-justice-based healing when one
surfaces. This extends executive function into the socio-emotional and systemic
domain: it treats exclusion itself as something a learning environment must develop
the capacity to recognize and repair, not a gap in one learner's planning skill. Do not
route a systemic-exclusion problem to 6.1–6.4's toolkit of checklists and organizers;
it will not touch the actual barrier.

## In practice

**Worked pass.** A long-term research project.

- **6.1:** help the learner turn "do good research" into a goal specific enough to
  plan against: CAST suggests prompts and scaffolds to estimate effort, resources,
  and challenge, plus models of what good goal-setting looks like.
- **6.2:** before they start, use a planning template or checklist that surfaces
  likely snags (source access, a tight deadline, a skill they haven't practiced yet)
  rather than letting them discover each one at the point of failure.
- **6.3:** give them a graphic organizer or note-taking guide so source material stays
  categorized as it accumulates, instead of becoming an unsorted pile by week three.
- **6.4:** build in visible progress markers: a process portfolio, a before-and-after
  comparison, or a checklist reviewed at set review points, so the learner, not just the
  teacher, can see whether they're on track and when to shift strategy.
- **6.5:** if the actual barrier is that this learner's way of researching or knowing
  gets dismissed in the classroom (their community's oral-history sources treated as
  less legitimate than print, say), no organizer fixes that. It needs to be named and
  addressed as a practice, not scaffolded as a skill gap.

## Gotchas & constraints

- **The name collision is real and worth restating.** "Executive Function" the row
  (Guidelines 3, 6, 9) is not "Strategy Development" the guideline (6 alone). Confusing
  them will make you search this one page for content that actually belongs to
  Guideline 3 or Guideline 9.
- **2.2 called this guideline "Executive Functions."** That name is retired; do not
  use it as current, though 2.2-trained readers will search for it here.
- **6.5 is not a scaling-up of 6.1–6.4.** It is a distinct kind of intervention aimed
  at the environment and the community, not an individual skill deficit. Treating it as
  "just another executive-function checklist" undersells what CAST is asking for.
- **Disclosed gap.** Each consideration's staged extract references a research link
  rather than reporting specific findings, designs, or effect sizes. See
  [[does-udl-improve-student-outcomes]] for what the broader UDL evidence literature
  does and does not support.
