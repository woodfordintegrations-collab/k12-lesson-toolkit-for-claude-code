# UDL and Academic Standards Alignment: Practice & Curriculum

This document synthesizes authoritative guidance on how Universal Design for Learning (UDL) interacts with fixed academic standards and standards-aligned curriculum design. The core insight: UDL does not change WHAT students must learn (the standard); it expands HOW they can demonstrate learning.

---

## 1. Goal Separation: Standard vs. Method

### The Core Move

The fundamental distinction UDL brings to standards implementation is separating the **learning construct** (what must be learned) from the **means of demonstrating that learning** (how it is shown). [CAST Guidelines FAQ](https://udlguidelines.cast.org/more/frequently-asked-questions/), [Novak Education Standards-Based Design](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/udl-is-a-standards-based-curriculum-design)

**CAST's explicit guidance:**
> "Focusing on the learning goal supports educators to identify and reduce construct-irrelevant barriers so learners can access and engage with the construct-relevant learning goals."

This is operationalized through two standard categories:

1. **Content Standards** — demonstrate *knowledge* (use verbs: explain, demonstrate, describe, analyze)
2. **Method Standards** — demonstrate *skill* in doing something (use verbs: write, use, measure, solve)

### How to Read a Standard

Ask yourself: **"Is this standard requiring that students all do the exact same thing in the same way?"** [Novak, "UDL is a Standards-Based Curriculum Design"](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/udl-is-a-standards-based-curriculum-design)

If yes, the means (the method) is embedded in the goal. If no, flexibility is already available.

**CAST on the standard-goal relationship:**
> "Goals are often described as learning expectations and represent the knowledge, concepts, and skills all students should master, and are generally aligned to standards."

The UDL Guidelines define this operationally: "The first step in applying the UDL framework to practice is to define a specific, challenging learning goal." Once the goal is identified, educators then select which UDL guidelines and considerations will reduce barriers to reaching that goal.

### Worked Example: "Write Arguments"

A standard stating "Write arguments" (content goal) is fundamentally different from a standard requiring "Give a persuasive speech" (method-embedded goal).

- **"Write arguments"** → Goal is argumentation. Method (written) is embedded.
- **Better:** "Construct and defend a claim with evidence" → Goal is clear. Students could demonstrate via essay, video explanation, oral argument, multimedia presentation, etc.

## 2. The Verb-Embeds-Means Problem

### Definition and Examples

Many academic standards embed the *method* directly into the standard statement, restricting how students can demonstrate learning. This is the central problem for applying UDL to fixed standards. [Novak Education](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/udl-is-a-standards-based-curriculum-design)

**Problematic Example (with embedded means):**
> "Print with appropriate spacing between words and sentences"

**Why it's problematic:** 
This goal has only one rigid expected outcome because the means for demonstrating achievement is restricted to one medium (handwriting/printing). A student who demonstrates understanding through speech, video, or digital text may be marked as not meeting the standard, even though the underlying construct (understanding spacing conventions) has been mastered.

**Other examples of embedded methods:**
- "Use a semicolon (and perhaps a conjunctive adverb) to link two or more closely related independent clauses" — Restricts to punctuation as the sole expression mode
- "Measure and estimate liquid volumes and masses of objects using standard units" — Method (measurement) is embedded; the goal of understanding volume/mass is harder to separate
- "Represent proportional relationships through equations" — Specifies representation form (equations) as required

### The Construct-Irrelevant Barrier

**CAST/IRIS Center definition:** A construct-irrelevant barrier is any aspect of how we assess learning that prevents accurate measurement of what students actually know. [IRIS Center](https://iris.peabody.vanderbilt.edu/module/udl/cresource/q1/p02/)

When a standard embeds a method, students who cannot access that method may appear not to meet the standard even when they've mastered the construct:
- A student who cannot write by hand but can type or dictate may fail a "Print with spacing" standard
- A student with speech differences may fail an oral presentation standard despite understanding the content
- A student with processing difficulties may fail a timed written standard despite knowledge of the material

### Guidance for Educators

**From [Novak Education](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/udl-is-a-standards-based-curriculum-design):**

When you encounter a method-embedded standard, the UDL move is:
1. Identify the true learning construct (separate from the stated method)
2. Design flexible methods and materials that allow multiple pathways to demonstrate that construct
3. Ensure assessment captures the construct regardless of method used

**Example reconstruction:**
- **Standard as written:** "Write a paragraph with topic sentence, supporting details, and conclusion"
- **Construct identified:** Understanding paragraph structure and composition
- **Flexible demonstration methods:** Written essay, video explanation of structure, oral presentation with visual organizer, digital interactive diagram, etc.

**Note:** This does NOT eliminate standards that require specific skills. A student who must learn handwriting, specific writing formats, or particular communication styles still learns them—but these are recognized as the *skills being taught*, not as incidental methods for demonstrating unrelated knowledge.

## 3. Curriculum Materials Review: Real Instruments

### EdReports Review Tools (Version 2.0)

EdReports maintains the most widely-used curriculum review tools in K-12. Updated in 2024-2025 for comprehensive materials evaluation. [EdReports Review Tools](https://edreports.org/process/review-tools)

**Five primary focus areas:**
1. **English Language Arts (ELA)** — K–12 core + K–2 foundational skills
2. **Mathematics** — K–12 core comprehensive materials  
3. **Science** — K–12 core comprehensive materials
4. **Multilingual Learner (MLL) Supports** — K–12 ELA, math, and science materials (new in 2.0)
5. **Pre-Kindergarten (Pre-K)** — Pre-K instructional materials

**Tool structure:** Each review consists of two documents:
- Review Criteria document with specific evaluation indicators
- Evidence Guide with purposes, evidence collection methods, guiding questions, and scoring criteria

**EdReports 2.0 Enhancement Focus Areas** [EdReports Updated Review Criteria](https://edreports.org/resources/article/edreports-updated-2-0-review-criteria-for-k-12-comprehensive-materials):
- **ELA:** Significant enhancements for science of reading and structured literacy alignment
- **Mathematics:** Deepened emphasis on Standards for Mathematical Practice
- **Science:** Phenomena-driven three-dimensional instruction
- **Multilingual Learners:** New MLL-specific review criteria for each content area to evaluate language supports, accessibility, and asset-based approaches

### CAST UDL Progression Rubric

Published through CAST Professional Publishing by Katie Novak and Kristan Rodriguez. [CAST Professional Publishing](https://publishing.cast.org/stories-resources/book-product-resources/udl-progression-rubric-novak-rodriguez)

**Structure:** 8-page downloadable PDF
**Purpose:** Measures educator progress in implementing UDL Guidelines
**Scoring levels:** Emerging, Proficient, Progressing toward Expert Practice
**Use cases:** Self-evaluation by educators; team-level progress assessment
**Note:** This is an implementation rubric (how well is UDL being applied?), not a curriculum materials rubric (does this textbook support UDL?)

### CAST UDL Curriculum Toolkit

An open-source web application designed to support creation of interactive, multimedia curricula following UDL principles. [CAST UDL Curriculum Toolkit](https://www.cast.org/resources/digital-tools/udl-curriculum-toolkit-building-flexible-customizable-learning-environments/)

**Embedded features:**
- Multiple means for comprehension (highlighting, accessible text, vocabulary support with embedded glossaries)
- Multiple means for action/expression (notetaking, writing prompts, drawing, audio recording)
- Multiple means for engagement (video, teacher feedback, collaborative whiteboard)

### Student Achievement Partners Evaluation Tools

Achieve and Student Achievement Partners jointly developed alignment evaluation tools for Common Core standards. [Achieve/SAP IMET](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED588503.pdf)

**Instructional Materials Evaluation Toolkit (IMET):** Used to assess comprehensiveness of curriculum materials (ELA and math, K–12) for alignment to CCSS and standards-aligned pedagogy.

## 4. UDL and Explicit Instruction: The Science of Learning

### The Compatibility Question

Early misconception: "UDL means discovery learning and rejects explicit instruction."

**Research reality:** Explicit Instruction and UDL are **compatible and synergistic** when properly integrated. [Foxworth et al., 2022; Virginia Commonwealth University TTAC](https://blogs.vcu.edu/ttacinfo/2026/03/03/working-together-how-explicit-instruction-can-fit-within-a-universal-design-for-learning-framework/)

### What Research Shows

**From peer-reviewed literature (Foxworth et al., 2022, *Teaching Exceptional Children*):** 

"There are many compatible aspects of explicit instruction practices and the principles of universal design for learning. Explicit instruction and UDL can be used in tandem to render instruction in special education accessible to the broadest range of learners, and in the most impactful manner possible."

**Shared principles:**
- Both recognize individual differences in how learners process information
- Both emphasize the importance of providing appropriately matched, flexible instruction
- UDL's representation principle explicitly states: "No single teaching methodology will be satisfactory for every learner" — which explicit instruction also recognizes

### How to Integrate Explicit Instruction within UDL

**Novak Education, "Explicit Instruction & UDL: 4 Ways to Provide Explicit Instruction"** [Novak](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/explicit-instruction-udl-4-ways-to-provide-explicit-instruction):

1. **Combined approaches:** Foundational knowledge through direct teaching + inquiry-based learning for real-world application
2. **Accessible delivery:** Captions, recordings, varied note-taking organizers, visual supports during explicit teaching
3. **Lesson integration:** Direct instruction connects to prior knowledge, links to learning objectives, includes multiple practice and demonstration options
4. **Cognitive management:** Break content into smaller chunks; limit direct instruction to 5–15 minutes to reduce cognitive load

**The key tension resolved:** Explicit instruction becomes a *component* within a broader UDL-designed learning experience, not the sole teaching method.

### Five Essential Components of Explicit Instruction

When delivered within UDL:
1. **Segmenting:** Breaking complex skills into smaller units
2. **Highlighting:** Showing (via demonstration) or telling (via think-aloud) important features
3. **Modeling:** Demonstrating the skill or thinking process
4. **Guided practice:** Prompts/support that gradually withdraw
5. **Frequent querying:** Immediate feedback and understanding monitoring

All of these can be provided through **multiple means** (visual models, video, audio narration, peer modeling, student self-explanation) without losing explicitness.

### Disputed Territory: Direct Instruction vs. UDL

**Distinction that matters:** Explicit instruction (clear teaching of skills + strategies) is different from Direct Instruction (scripted, standardized lesson sequences).

Direct Instruction as typically scripted can conflict with UDL because scripting assumes one correct delivery path. However, the *principles* of Direct Instruction (systematic progression, frequent practice, data-driven) are compatible with UDL if the delivery remains flexible.

**No authoritative resolution:** The research literature does not settle whether all elements of Direct Instruction (as a branded curriculum model) can coexist with UDL principles. Most sources resolve this pragmatically: use explicit teaching strategies within a UDL-designed overall structure.

## 5. Content-Area Specific UDL Guidance

### Mathematics (K–5 and beyond)

**CAST Professional Publishing:** "Universal Design for Learning in Mathematics Instruction, K-5" (Novak & Marlow)

**Content-specific applications:**
- **Representation:** Provide options for organizing and manipulating mathematical operations (tables, algorithms, visual number lines, concrete manipulatives, digital tools)
- **Action/Expression:** Multiple ways to show mathematical thinking (written computation, drawing/diagrams, verbal explanation, gesture/modeling with objects, digital entry)
- **Engagement:** Problems using varied, relevant contexts; choice in problem-solving approaches; collaborative problem-solving

**Example:** Instead of "Solve these 10 addition problems," UDL mathematics might offer: "Show how you can make 15 using addition (you choose: objects, drawings, numbers, words, or a combination)"

### Literacy (ELA)

**Science of reading alignment:** CAST's updated literacy guidance emphasizes phonemic awareness, phonics, fluency, vocabulary, and comprehension—all deliverable through multiple means of representation and expression. [EdReports 2.0 ELA Enhancements](https://edreports.org/resources/article/edreports-updated-2-0-review-criteria-for-k-12-comprehensive-materials)

**Content-specific applications:**
- **Text access:** Audio narration, digital text with adjustable fonts, supported vocabulary, simplified/complex text variations
- **Comprehension support:** Graphic organizers, discussion prompts, annotation tools, multimedia supplements
- **Writing/expression:** Options for drafting (voice-to-text, handwriting, typing), peer collaboration tools, varied genres and formats

**Caution:** Providing text in audio format does NOT replace teaching phonics/decoding. The construct being learned may require decoding practice, even if comprehension of content can be accessed through audio.

### Science

**Phenomena-driven, three-dimensional instruction:** CAST materials emphasize science as constructing explanations and engaging in scientific practices—not memorizing facts.

**IQWST (Improving Quaility of Science Teaching):** A 12-unit middle-school science curriculum integrating literacy practices with science content under UDL principles.

**Content-specific applications:**
- **Representation:** Multiple models of phenomena (physical models, digital simulations, diagrams, video, real-world observation)
- **Investigation methods:** Options for data collection (direct measurement, sensor technology, simulation, analysis of existing data)
- **Communication:** Express findings through lab reports, posters, digital presentations, peer discussion, multimedia explanations

## 6. UDL and Multilingual Learners / English Language Development

### WIDA English Language Development Standards Framework

WIDA (World-class Instructional Design and Assessment) provides the most widely-used ELD standards framework in K–12. Designed to work *with* content standards, not replace them. [WIDA ELD Standards Framework](https://wida.wisc.edu/teach/standards/eld)

**Key concept:** WIDA frames language development through three dimensions:
- **Word/Phrase level** (individual vocabulary, morphology)
- **Sentence level** (syntax, discourse patterns)
- **Discourse level** (larger text organization, academic register)

### Compatibility with UDL

**Research finding (ResearchGate case study on UDL + WIDA):** Both frameworks prioritize flexible, multimodal scaffolding and recognize learner variability as the norm. They are explicitly designed to be complementary.

**WIDA resources for UDL integration:**
- WIDA Proficiency Level Descriptors (PLDs) — detailed articulation of growth across interpretive and expressive modes
- WIDA Can Do Descriptors — frame language goals as "what students can do now" while scaffolding toward next proficiency level
- Language Expectations — specify what language students need to function in academic contexts
- Reflection Tool: "UDL and Language Considerations for Instructional Planning" [WIDA Reflection Tool](https://wida.wisc.edu/resources/reflection-tool-udl-and-language-considerations-instructional-planning)

### Practical Integration

**From research on multilingual learner support with UDL:** [ResearchGate case study on ELD + UDL implementation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386426286_Empowering_Multilingual_Learners_A_Case_Study_on_the_Implementation_of_Universal_Design_for_Learning_and_WIDA_Frameworks_to_Enhance_English_Language_Development_and_Content_Learning)

"Sentence frames, graphic organizers, collaborative work, exemplars, and the use of WIDA ELD Standards provide necessary supports that allow all students to express what they know in inclusive, diverse classrooms."

**Specific strategies:**
- Sentence frames (scaffolds for oral and written expression, faded as proficiency increases)
- Graphic organizers (organize thinking; support access to content independent of reading level)
- Collaborative grouping (peer language modeling; authentic communication practice)
- Exemplars (models of target language/genres; anchor meaning)
- Visual supports (images, videos, realia tied to vocabulary)

### Important distinction for standards-aligned instruction:

WIDA standards address *language development*, not *content learning*. Content standards (math, science, literacy) must still be taught. **UDL + WIDA together** means:
- Content goals remain fixed (aligned to state/national standards)
- Language demands are made transparent and scaffolded (WIDA)
- Multiple means of accessing content and expressing understanding (UDL)

**Multilingual learners with disabilities:** WIDA maintains specific resources for this population, recognizing that language development and special education needs require coordinated, not sequential, support. [WIDA Supporting MLs with Disabilities](https://wida.wisc.edu/teach/disabilities)

## 7. UDL and Gifted / Advanced Learners

### The Challenge Problem

**Central issue:** Gifted and advanced learners are among those "in the margins" who struggle with curricula designed for the fictional "average." [CAST; Novak Education](https://www.novakeducation.com/blog/how-to-support-challenge-and-engage-gifted-students-in-your-classroom)

Inflexible curricula can actually *hold back* advanced learners by:
- Requiring them to work at the pace of grade-level average
- Offering no depth or complexity options
- Providing only one pathway to demonstrate mastery (penalizing non-traditional approaches)

### UDL's Answer: Providing Appropriate Challenge

UDL is **not** about making everything easy. It is about **removing barriers while maintaining rigor and appropriate challenge at every level**.

**From CAST:**
> "The UDL framework emphasizes that the average learner does not exist, and instead offers an approach to designing learning opportunities that honor learner variability through purposeful attention to the design of goals, assessments, methods, and materials. UDL allows educators and learners to create meaningful options and fully embrace human variability."

**For advanced learners, this means:**
- Offering depth and complexity options (not just acceleration)
- Providing choice in problem-solving approaches and resources
- Allowing demonstration of mastery through varied formats (essay, video, data analysis, creative product, peer teaching, etc.)
- Creating learning centers, tiered assignments, and independent study that enable self-directed challenge-seeking

### Content-Specific Differentiation Strategies

**From multiple sources (CAST, Novak, ASCD):**

1. **Learning centers** — Different complexity levels or topics available simultaneously; learners choose based on readiness and interest
2. **Tiered assignments** — Same goal; varied complexity/support levels (e.g., "Prove this theorem using basic properties" vs. "Prove and then generalize this pattern to a larger class of problems")
3. **Independent study projects** — Student-identified questions investigated with structured guidance; depth and complexity self-regulated
4. **Problem-based learning** — Open-ended investigations where advanced learners can pursue depth naturally through the problem's complexity

### Important nuance on the upper end:

UDL does NOT treat "gifted" as a single category. Advanced learners vary in:
- What they're advanced in (math vs. reading vs. creative thinking vs. social reasoning)
- What kinds of challenge motivate them (complexity, creativity, relevance, speed, novelty, mastery)
- How they need support (social-emotional, executive function, asynchronous development)

Effective UDL for gifted learners requires the same principle-centered assessment of variability used for all students: What are *this* learner's strengths, interests, and barriers?

## Summary of Key Takeaways

### On Goal Separation
**Authoritative source:** CAST Guidelines FAQ and Novak Education
- Standards are fixed; methods for demonstrating mastery are flexible
- Identify construct-relevant features (what must be learned) vs. construct-irrelevant barriers (incidental methods)
- This separation happens at curriculum design time, not assessment time

### On Verb-Embeds-Means Problem
**Authoritative source:** Novak Education; IRIS Center; CAST
- Many standards embed method directly (write, print, measure, give a speech)
- When method is embedded, students who cannot access that method may appear not to meet standards even when construct is mastered
- Educator move: Separate construct from method; design flexible demonstration pathways that maintain construct rigor

### On Curriculum Review Instruments
**Real tools available:**
1. **EdReports 2.0 Review Tools** (ELA, Math, Science, MLL-specific) — most widely used; updated 2024–2025
2. **CAST UDL Progression Rubric** (Novak & Rodriguez) — for evaluating educator UDL implementation
3. **CAST UDL Curriculum Toolkit** — for designing flexible multimedia curricula
4. **Achieve/Student Achievement Partners IMET** — for CCSS alignment evaluation
5. **WIDA Proficiency Level Descriptors & Can Do Descriptors** — for language development alignment

### On UDL and Explicit Instruction
**Research consensus:** Compatible when integrated properly
- Explicit instruction (clear, systematic teaching) ≠ Direct Instruction (scripted programs)
- Explicit teaching can be delivered through multiple means (video, live modeling, peer explanation, digital interaction)
- The tension is real but resolved pragmatically: explicit strategies within UDL-designed overall structure

### On Content Areas
**Evidence available for:**
- Mathematics (multiple representations, multiple solution paths)
- Literacy (decoding instruction + multiple text formats; comprehension through varied modalities)
- Science (phenomena-based inquiry with varied investigation methods)

**Limitation:** Generic UDL advice often applies across content; subject-specific deep guidance is less developed outside K–5 math and ELA.

### On Multilingual Learners
**Research consensus:** UDL + WIDA frameworks are complementary
- WIDA frames language development; UDL frames access to content
- Tools exist (proficiency descriptors, reflection tools, sentence frame scaffolds)
- Key distinction: Language development is taught (not just scaffolded); content must still be taught
- Multilingual learners with disabilities require coordinated, not sequential, support

### On Gifted/Advanced Learners
**Limitation:** Less developed in authoritative sources than other populations
- UDL frameworks recognize advanced learners as part of "normal variability"
- Strategies focus on providing appropriate depth/complexity, not acceleration alone
- Individual assessment of what constitutes "challenge" for each learner is key

---

## Gaps and Limitations in Available Guidance

1. **Verb-embeds-means problem:** No definitive how-to guide exists. Most guidance is principle-based; practical examples are limited to a few recurring standards (writing, printing, measurement).

2. **UDL + Direct Instruction:** Research shows compatibility with explicit instruction; compatibility with branded Direct Instruction programs is unresolved in literature.

3. **Content-specific guidance:** Deep subject-matter guidance exists primarily for K–5 math and K–2 literacy. Secondary math/science/ELA applications are emerging but less systematized.

4. **Gifted learners:** UDL frameworks acknowledge the upper end of variability but provide less specific, research-backed guidance than for lower-end barriers.

5. **State-level implementation:** Most state education departments (e.g., Kentucky, Colorado) reference CAST and national standards; few have developed their own standards-specific UDL guidance.
