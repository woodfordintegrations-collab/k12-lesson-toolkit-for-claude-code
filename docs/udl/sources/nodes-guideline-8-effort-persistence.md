# Guideline 8: Sustaining Effort & Persistence

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/

## Official Title & Description

**Guideline 8: Sustaining Effort & Persistence**

"Learning is an ongoing process that oftentimes requires sustained effort and determination. Learners are highly variable in terms of the ways they are inspired to persist through challenging aspects of the learning process, and this variability can shift depending on the learning context. To sustain effort and persistence, effective learning designs consider options for creating goals that are meaningful and purposeful, offering scaffolds and supports in service of challenging goals, fostering collaboration and belonging, and offering on-going, action-oriented feedback."

## Structure Overview

The guideline comprises five considerations (Considerations 8.1–8.5), each with associated design options:

- **Consideration 8.1:** Clarify the meaning and purpose of goals — Emphasize the goal and why it matters.
- **Consideration 8.2:** Optimize challenge and support — Set high expectations using flexible tools and supports.
- **Consideration 8.3:** Foster collaboration, interdependence, and collective learning — Learn from and with one another.
- **Consideration 8.4:** Foster belonging and community — Cultivate spaces where learners are wanted and where learners want to be.
- **Consideration 8.5:** Offer action-oriented feedback — Guide learning by emphasizing the role of effort and process.

# Consideration 8.1: Clarify the Meaning and Purpose of Goals

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/meaning-purpose-goals/

## Official Title & Description

**Title:** Clarify the meaning and purpose of goals

**Description:**
"Over the course of any sustained project or practice, there are many sources of interest and engagement that compete for attention and effort. To support sustained effort and persistence, it is essential for learners to be clear on the goal and to have space to explore how the goal is meaningful to their own lives and communities. Further, it is important the meaning and purpose of the goal is clearly and consistently reinforced and apparent to learners throughout the learning process."

## Complete Design Options

- Explicitly formulate or restate the goal.
- Display the goal in multiple ways.
- Encourage organization of long-term goals into short-term objectives.
- Use prompts or scaffolds for imagining desired outcomes.
- Co-construct ideals of excellence and generate relevant examples that connect to learners' cultural backgrounds, identities, and interests.

# Consideration 8.2: Optimize Challenge and Support

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/challenge-support/

## Official Title & Description

**Title:** Optimize challenge and support

**Description with Challenge Principle (Full Context):**
"All learners need to be challenged, but not always in the same way. It is important for the learning environment to embed a variety of tools, resources, and supports to promote successful engagement with the learning goal and to ensure learners find challenges that are optimally motivating. Balancing the resources available to meet the challenge is vital, as productive struggle is a key component of an engaging and rigorous learning experience."

## Design Principles

The consideration emphasizes four key directives:

- Presume competence and nurture belief in every learner's capabilities
- Offer options with varying modes of complexity or difficulty
- Offer options for tools and scaffolds aligned with learning goals that promote agency
- Emphasize process, effort, and progress toward standards rather than external evaluation and competition

# Consideration 8.3: Foster Collaboration, Interdependence, and Collective Learning

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/collaboration/

## Official Title & Description

**Title:** Foster collaboration, interdependence, and collective learning

**Description:**
"Collectively generating knowledge and learning from one another's diversity of ideas, perspectives, and lived experiences is central to the learning process. Creating communities and partnerships that extend thinking while practicing mutual care is a means to sustain effort and persistence. Interdependence—recognizing our interconnectedness and mutual support—is valued by the disability community."

## Complete Design Options

- Create community agreements that emphasize learners' ideas for fostering collaboration, interdependence, and collective learning.
- Create teams with clear goals, roles, expectations, and responsibilities.
- Use prompts that guide learners in when and how to ask for help.
- Use prompts or protocols that guide learners to surface and share differing perspectives.
- Encourage and support opportunities for peer interactions and supports (e.g., peer tutors).
- Construct communities of learners engaged in common interests or activities or who identify in similar ways.
- Construct communities of learners engaged in differing interests or activities or who identify in differing ways.
- Encourage questions to more fully understand concepts, ideas, and perspectives.

# Consideration 8.4: Foster Belonging and Community

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/belonging-community/

## Official Title & Description

**Title:** Foster belonging and community

**Description:**
"To support learners to sustain effort and persistence — and to thrive as learners more broadly — it is critical to design learning environments where learners feel a legitimate sense of belonging and community. This is particularly vital for historically excluded and marginalized learners. Sources and meanings of belonging and community will vary across learners and contexts."

## Complete Design Options

- Create opportunities for learners to share their perspectives on what belonging and community can feel like and look like.
- Create opportunities for learners to share their ideas for different ways to foster belonging and community.
- Welcome interests and identities (referencing Guideline 7).
- Examine when or how bias may be creating barriers to learners' sense of belonging.

# Consideration 8.5: Offer Action-Oriented Feedback

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/feedback/

## Official Title & Description

**Title:** Offer action-oriented feedback

**Definition:**
"Action-oriented feedback provides specific comments on ways to make progress and take action toward the learning goal. This approach emphasizes effort and practice over innate ability, guiding learners toward sustainable engagement and long-term success."

## What Makes Feedback "Action-Oriented"

Action-oriented feedback is characterized by:

- **Specificity:** Specific comments (not general praise or criticism) on ways to make progress and take action toward the learning goal
- **Focus on Effort & Process:** Emphasizes effort, improvement, and achieving a goal rather than relative performance or innate ability
- **Development & Self-Awareness:** Encourages perseverance, development of efficacy and self-awareness, and use of specific supports and strategies in the face of challenge
- **Substantive & Informative:** Substantive and informative rather than comparative or competitive
- **Timely & Frequent:** Frequent, timely, and delivered in real time or as close to performance as possible
- **Reflective & Forward-Looking:** Models how to incorporate reflection (identifying patterns of challenges or strengths) into positive strategies for future success
- **Risk-Taking Encouragement:** Encourages risk-taking and offers another (or differing) perspective(s)

Contrasted with comparative/competitive framing or feedback that emphasizes innate ability or external performance ranking.

## Complete Design Options

- Offer feedback that encourages perseverance, focuses on development of efficacy and self-awareness, and encourages the use of specific supports and strategies in the face of challenge.
- Offer feedback that emphasizes effort, improvement, and achieving a goal rather than on relative performance.
- Offer feedback that is frequent, timely, and specific.
- Offer feedback that is substantive and informative rather than comparative or competitive.
- Offer feedback that models how to incorporate reflection, including identifying patterns of challenges or strengths, into positive strategies for future success.
- Offer feedback that encourages risk taking and offers another (or differing) perspective(s).
