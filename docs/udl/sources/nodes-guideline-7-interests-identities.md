# Guideline 7: Welcoming Interests & Identities

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/

## Official Title and Definition

**Guideline 7: Welcoming Interests & Identities**

To spark engagement in the learning process, learners' unique interests as well as their unique identities, including intersecting dimensions such as race/ethnicity, culture, gender, language, and disability, must be recognized, sustained, and valued.

The framework emphasizes that engagement varies significantly across learners and even within individual learners over time. Creating a learning environment that welcomes learners' whole selves is a critical step to ensuring learners are able to access and engage with the learning process.

## Four Considerations Overview

**Consideration 7.1:** "Optimize choice and autonomy" — "Develop agency in the learning process."

**Consideration 7.2:** "Optimize relevance, value, and authenticity" — "Connect learning to experiences that are meaningful and valuable."

**Consideration 7.3:** "Nurture joy and play" — "Spark joy and playfulness in the learning process."

**Consideration 7.4:** "Address biases, threats, and distractions" — "Foster spaces to learn and take risks."

## Identity Framework in Guideline 7

CAST explicitly addresses **intersecting identities** including "race/ethnicity, culture, gender, language, and disability" as dimensions requiring recognition and valuing within learning environments. The guideline centers on welcoming learners' whole identities and recognizing the validity and importance of the full range of who learners are.

# Consideration 7.1: Optimize Choice and Autonomy

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/choice-autonomy/

## Official Title and Definition

**Consideration 7.1: Optimize choice and autonomy**

Embedding authentic choices that align with learning goals can be a meaningful way to enhance learner engagement. CAST notes that offering choices can foster agency and accomplishment, but emphasizes that individuals differ in how much and what kind of choices they prefer to have. It is therefore not enough to simply provide choice.

## CAST's Bounded Claim About Choice

The framework emphasizes that "options for choice and autonomy must be optimized to ensure engagement," meaning choice alone is insufficient without thoughtful design matching learner preferences. Choice must be:
- Authentic and aligned with learning goals
- Varied in type and degree to match individual learner preferences (not all learners want or benefit from the same amount of choice)
- Designed with intentionality, not offered for its own sake

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

Embed choices aligned with learning goals, including:

- The content to explore
- The tools used for exploration or production
- The type of rewards or recognition available
- The opportunities for practicing and assessing learning
- The design or graphics of layouts
- The sequence or timing for completion of tasks

Use a collaborative approach between learners and educators to co-design learning goals, activities, and tasks.

# Consideration 7.2: Optimize Relevance, Value, and Authenticity

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/relevance-value-authenticity/

## Official Title and Definition

**Consideration 7.2: Optimize relevance, value, and authenticity**

Individuals are engaged by content and activities that are relevant and valuable to their interests, goals, and communities. Authenticity does not require real-life equivalence—fiction can be just as engaging to learners as non-fiction—but content must connect to learners' individual and/or community goals and the instructional goals.

Educators should highlight the utility and relevance of learning through authentic, meaningful activities while recognizing that different learners find different activities equally relevant.

## Identity and Culturally Sustaining Practice

CAST emphasizes that activities and sources of information should be:
- Personalized and contextualized to learners' lives
- **Culturally relevant and sustaining**
- Socially relevant
- Age and ability appropriate
- Appropriate for different racial, cultural, ethnic, and gender groups

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Vary activities and sources of information so they can be:
  - Personalized and contextualized to learners' lives
  - Culturally relevant and sustaining
  - Socially relevant
  - Age and ability appropriate
  - Appropriate for different racial, cultural, ethnic, and gender groups

- Design activities so learning outcomes are authentic, communicate to real audiences, and reflect a purpose that is clear to the participants

- Provide tasks that allow for active participation, exploration, and experimentation

- Invite personal response, evaluation, and self-reflection to content and activities

- Include activities that foster imagination to solve novel problems or make sense of complex ideas creatively

# Consideration 7.3: Nurture Joy and Play

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/joy-play/

## Official Title and Definition

**Consideration 7.3: Nurture joy and play**

Learning environments that spark joy and nurture opportunities for play are central to the learning process. Play supports development across all ages. Engaging in play sparks curiosity, wonder, and imagination as learners construct new understandings and develop relationships.

For marginalized learners, joy can represent resistance to oppressive systems. However, learners experience joy differently, requiring flexible learning environments.

## Identity Approach: Joy and Self in Community

CAST emphasizes that joy connects to "identities, sense of self, and communities," recognizing that marginalized communities—specifically referencing Black Joy—find meaning through freedom, imagination, community, and love within learning contexts.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Nurture playfulness in varied forms including outdoor play, sensory play, imaginative play, and arts-based play

- Incorporate opportunities for exploration, experimentation, and discovery

- Create space for learners to find joy through connections to their identities, sense of self, and communities

- Create space for learners to take pride in their accomplishments

- Incorporate storytelling

# Consideration 7.4: Address Biases, Threats, and Distractions

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/interests-identities/biases-threats-distractions/

## Official Title and Definition

**Consideration 7.4: Address biases, threats, and distractions**

Educators must establish spaces where learners feel valued, supported, agentic, and encouraged to take risks. When learners have to focus their attention on having basic needs met or avoiding a negative or oppressive experience, they cannot engage with the learning process. Optimal environments acknowledge both overt and subtle threats and offer options to reduce threats and enable learning.

## Identity Approach: Asset-Based, Threat-Responsive

CAST addresses the reality that learning environments contain both overt biases and subtle threats that disproportionately affect certain learners. The framework positions educators as needing to actively explore and acknowledge educator and learner biases and their impact. This is paired with creating space for learners to name the threats and distractions they experience, positioning learner voice as essential to creating truly welcoming environments.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

**Create an accepting and supportive learning culture by:**

- Exploring how educator and learner biases can impact the learning environment
- Creating space and ways for learners to share the biases, threats, and distractions they may be experiencing
- Nurturing a culture where mistakes are a part of the learning process

**Vary the level of novelty or risk through:**

- Charts, calendars, schedules, visible timers, cues, etc., that can increase the predictability of daily activities and transitions
- Creation of class routines
- Alerts and previews that can help learners anticipate and prepare for changes in activities, schedules, and novel events
- Options that can, in contrast to the above, maximize the unexpected, surprising, or novel in highly routinized activities

**Vary the level of sensory stimulation through:**

- Variation in the presence of background noise or visual stimulation, noise buffers, number of features or items presented at a time
- Variation in pace of work, length of work sessions, availability of breaks or time-outs, or timing or sequence of activities

**Vary the social demands required for learning or performance, the perceived level of support and protection, and the requirements for public display and evaluation.**

**Acknowledge negative experiences within learning environments and take steps such as shared agreements or self regulation options to make learners comfortable.**
