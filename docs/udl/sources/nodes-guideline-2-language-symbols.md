# GUIDELINE 2: Language & Symbols

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/

## Official Title
Language & Symbols

## Definition
The guideline addresses how learners vary in their facility with different forms of representation—both linguistic and non-linguistic. The document emphasizes that "inequalities arise when information is presented to all learners through a single form of representation." A key instructional strategy involves ensuring multiple representations are available for accessibility, clarity, comprehensibility, and shared understanding.

## Guiding Question
"How might we offer, honor, and value multiple representations of language and symbols?"

## Overview of Considerations
The guideline encompasses five considerations:

- **Consideration 2.1:** Clarify vocabulary, symbols, and language structures
- **Consideration 2.2:** Support decoding of text, mathematical notation, and symbols
- **Consideration 2.3:** Cultivate understanding and respect across languages and dialects
- **Consideration 2.4:** Address biases in the use of language and symbols
- **Consideration 2.5:** Illustrate through multiple media

## Multilingual & Dialect Support
The guideline explicitly addresses learners across different linguistic backgrounds, emphasizing translations and support for unfamiliar languages and dialects as core design strategies.

# CONSIDERATION 2.1: Clarify vocabulary, symbols, and language structures

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/vocabulary-symbols-structure/

## Official Title
Clarify vocabulary, symbols, and language structures

## Definition
The semantic elements through which information is presented—words, symbols, numbers, icons, and language structures—are differentially accessible to learners with varying backgrounds, languages, and lexical knowledge. To ensure accessibility, key vocabulary, labels, icons, and symbols should be linked to alternate representations of their meaning (e.g., embedded glossaries, graphic equivalents, charts, or maps). Idioms, archaic expressions, culturally exclusive phrases, and slang should be translated. When sentence syntax or structural relationships in equations or graphics aren't obvious or familiar to learners, comprehension suffers. Providing alternative representations that clarify syntactic or structural relationships ensures equal access to information.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Pre-teach vocabulary and symbols, especially in ways that promote connection to learners' experience and prior knowledge
- Offer graphic symbols with alternative text descriptions
- Highlight how complex terms, expressions, or equations are composed of simpler words or symbols
- Embed support for vocabulary and symbols within the text (e.g., hyperlinks or footnotes to definitions, explanations, illustrations, previous coverage, or translations)
- Embed support for unfamiliar references within the text (e.g., domain-specific notation, lesser known properties and theorems, idioms, academic language, figurative language, mathematical language, jargon, archaic language, colloquialism, and dialect)
- Clarify unfamiliar syntax (in language or in math formulas) or underlying structure (in diagrams, graphs, illustrations, extended expositions, or narratives) through alternatives that highlight structural relations, make connections to previously learned structures, and make relationships between elements explicit

## Multilingual & Translation Notes
The consideration explicitly addresses translation needs for "idioms, archaic expressions, culturally exclusive phrases, and slang" and mentions supporting "unfamiliar references" including translations within embedded text support.

# CONSIDERATION 2.2: Support decoding of text, mathematical notation, and symbols

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/text-notation-symbols/

## Official Title
Support decoding of text, mathematical notation, and symbols

## Definition
The consideration explains that decoding encoded formats (visual symbols for text, haptic symbols for Braille, algebraic expressions) requires practice, with varying rates of automaticity among learners. Consistent exposure to symbols enables comprehension and effective use. When decoding fluency is lacking, cognitive load increases substantially, diminishing processing capacity. The consideration emphasizes providing alternatives when decoding ability is not the instructional focus, thereby removing barriers for unfamiliar symbols.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Allow the use of text-to-speech
- Use automatic voicing with digital mathematical notation (e.g., MathML)
- Use digital text with an accompanying human voice recording (e.g., DAISY Talking Books)
- Allow for flexibility and easy access to multiple representations of notation where appropriate (e.g., formulas, word problems, graphs)
- Offer clarification of notation through lists of key terms

# CONSIDERATION 2.3: Cultivate understanding and respect across languages and dialects

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/languages-dialects/

## Official Title
Cultivate understanding and respect across languages and dialects

## Definition
While curriculum materials typically use one language, classrooms contain multilingual learners. The framework emphasizes that "offering multiple representations, especially for key information or vocabulary, is an important aspect of accessibility." Critically, it notes that recognizing "other languages and linguistic representations are valid and valued" supports both new language learners and those developing academic language proficiency. Language hierarchy emerges in diverse environments, particularly when academic English is positioned as the standard. This consideration promotes countering such hierarchies through deliberate inclusion of multiple languages and dialects.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Offer a diverse curriculum with literary works, historical perspectives, and cultural practices from various linguistic backgrounds
- Promote opportunities to share cultures and backgrounds
- Celebrate linguistic diversity to showcase languages and cultures, fostering pride and respect among peers
- Make all key information in the dominant language also available in home or heritage languages
- Link key vocabulary words to definitions and pronunciations in both dominant and heritage languages
- Define domain-specific vocabulary using both domain-specific and common terms
- Incorporate electronic translation tools or links to multilingual glossaries on the web
- Embed visual, non-linguistic supports for vocabulary clarification (pictures, videos, etc.)
- Support translanguaging, enabling learners to move fluidly between languages and use their full linguistic capital
- Recognize and incorporate non-oral languages, such as sign languages

## Multilingual & Dialect Focus
This consideration is explicitly focused on serving multilingual learners, with specific strategies for heritage languages, translation tools, and multilingual glossaries. It emphasizes that "other languages and linguistic representations are valid and valued."

# CONSIDERATION 2.4: Address biases in the use of language and symbols

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/biases/

## Official Title
Address biases in the use of language and symbols

## Definition
The consideration addresses how language hierarchy emerges in diverse environments, particularly when academic English is positioned as the standard. The framework notes that "a hierarchy of language emerges with standard English at the top, and those who do not speak or understand this dominant language are often perceived as having a deficit or disadvantage." The guidance emphasizes disrupting messages of inferiority for speakers of other languages or dialects (Spanish, Arabic, AAVE, ASL). Since "language is intimately interwoven with a person's culture, values, and sense of identity," using multiple languages and symbols creates entry points, increases accessibility, and fosters belonging among linguistically diverse learners.

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Facilitate listening and understanding of diverse languages
- Use closed captioning and sign languages when possible
- Use closed captioning in more than one language
- Label classroom items in multiple languages
- Welcome the use of multiple languages and dialects for communication, including speaking and writing
- Recognize that the goal of learning standard languages is not assimilation or monolingualism
- Avoid using ableist language (expressions that use terms associated with disabilities as pejoratives)
- Avoid using language and symbols associated with oppression, discrimination, or dehumanization

## Multilingual & Dialect Notes
The consideration explicitly names specific language communities (Spanish, Arabic, AAVE—African American Vernacular English, ASL—American Sign Language) and addresses hierarchies that position standard English as dominant. It emphasizes that multiple languages and dialects should be welcomed and valued.

# CONSIDERATION 2.5: Illustrate through multiple media

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/language-symbols/multiple-media/

## Official Title
Illustrate through multiple media

## Definition
This consideration addresses a common barrier: classroom materials often rely too heavily on text representation. The guideline notes: "relying on text as a single mode of representation can impose barriers when presenting concepts and processes, particularly for learners who have text- or language-related disabilities or learners who communicate in languages other than the dominant language." The core benefit is that "offering multiple representations—especially illustrations, simulations, images, videos, or interactive graphics—can make the information in text more relatable and comprehensible for any learner and accessible for some who would find it completely inaccessible in text."

## Design Options/Practical Strategies

- Present key concepts beyond text (e.g., expository text or math equation) with another or multiple formats including: illustration, dance/movement, diagram, table, model, video, comic strip, storyboard, photograph, animation, or physical/virtual manipulative
- Clarify links between information provided in texts and any accompanying representation in illustrations, equations, charts, or diagrams

## Multilingual & Non-Text Learner Focus
The consideration explicitly acknowledges "learners who communicate in languages other than the dominant language" as a key audience benefiting from multi-modal representation beyond text. This supports both linguistically diverse learners and those with text-based disabilities.
