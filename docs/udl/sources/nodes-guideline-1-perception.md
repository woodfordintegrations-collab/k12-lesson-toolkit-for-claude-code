# Guideline 1: Perception

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/perception/

## Official Title
Design Options for Perception

## Full Description

Learning becomes inaccessible when information cannot be perceived, difficult when presented in formats requiring extraordinary effort, and oppressive when reinforcing stereotypes. The guideline emphasizes ensuring "key information is equally perceptible to all learners by: 1) offering the same information through different modalities (e.g., through vision, hearing, or touch); 2) offering information in a format that will allow for adjustability by the user (e.g., text that can be enlarged, sounds that can be amplified); and 3) offering a variety of perspectives."

Multiple representations support learners with sensory/perceptual disabilities while benefiting others. Providing mirrors (self-representation) and windows (diverse perspectives) strengthens authentic connections to learning.

## Guiding Question
"How might we increase accessibility, flexibility, and diversity in the sharing of information?"

## Considerations Overview

1. **Consideration 1.1:** Support opportunities to customize the display of information – Use accessible materials with settings that can be adjusted based on needs and preferences.

2. **Consideration 1.2:** Support multiple ways to perceive information – Share information in more ways than images and text alone.

3. **Consideration 1.3:** Represent a diversity of perspectives and identities in authentic ways – Learn from multiple and varying perspectives.

# Consideration 1.1: Support opportunities to customize the display of information

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/perception/customize-display/

## Official Title
Support opportunities to customize the display of information

## Full Description

While print materials have fixed displays, digital materials enable flexible customization. This adaptability helps diverse learners by adjusting perceptual clarity and accommodating individual preferences. However, an important caveat applies: digital does not mean the content is accessible, as many digital materials are equally inaccessible if accessibility features weren't integrated during development.

Educators and learners should work together to attain the best match of features to learning needs.

## Guiding Question
(Referenced in parent guideline: "How might we increase accessibility, flexibility, and diversity in the sharing of information?")

## Design Options - Complete List

Offer information in accessible formats allowing customization of:

- Font, text size, character and line spacing, character width, background color, and text colors
- Size of images, graphs, tables, and visual content
- Contrast between background and images
- Color used for information or emphasis
- Volume or rate of speech or sound
- Speed or timing of video, animation, sound, simulations
- Layout of visual or other elements

# Consideration 1.2: Support multiple ways to perceive information

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/perception/ways-perceive-information/

## Official Title
Support multiple ways to perceive information

## Full Description

Visual representations (images, graphics, animations, videos, text) are not equally accessible to all learners, particularly those with visual disabilities or unfamiliarity with graphic types. Visual information can be quite dense, particularly with visual art. Non-visual alternatives are essential for equitable access.

## Guiding Question
(Referenced in parent guideline: "How might we increase accessibility, flexibility, and diversity in the sharing of information?")

## Design Options - Complete List

### Images, Graphics, Videos & Animations

- Provide descriptions (text or spoken) for all images, graphics, videos, or animations
- Use touch equivalents (tactile graphics or objects of reference) for key visuals
- Provide physical objects and spatial models to convey perspective or interaction
- Provide auditory cues for key concepts and transitions in visual information

### Text-Specific Strategies

- Follow accessibility standards (NIMAS, DAISY) when creating digital text
- Allow competent aides, partners, or "interveners" to read text aloud
- Provide access to text-to-speech software

### Sound/Audio Alternatives

- Use text equivalents via captions or automated speech-to-text
- Provide visual diagrams, charts, or musical notations
- Provide written transcripts for videos or auditory clips
- Provide sign languages for spoken communication
- Use visual analogues representing emphasis and prosody
- Provide visual or tactile equivalents for sound effects/alerts
- Provide visual and/or emotional description for musical interpretation

## Accessibility Standards Referenced

- NIMAS (National Instructional Materials Accessibility Standard)
- DAISY (Digital Accessible Information System)

## Assistive Technology & Accessibility Notes

Multiple accessibility standards and assistive technologies are referenced throughout this consideration, emphasizing the integration of NIMAS and DAISY standards for digital text creation and the use of text-to-speech software for auditory access.

# Consideration 1.3: Represent a diversity of perspectives and identities in authentic ways

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/perception/perspectives-identities/

## Official Title
Represent a diversity of perspectives and identities in authentic ways

## Full Description

Learners benefit from seeing themselves reflected in curriculum materials. When students encounter perspectives and cultures matching their own identities, it creates a sense of validation, affirmation, and belonging. Conversely, exclusion from materials can communicate that their perspectives are less valid and appreciated.

Additionally, exposure to diverse perspectives prevents learners from assuming their experiences are universal. Without such exposure, students may develop incorrect assumptions about whose viewpoints matter.

## Guiding Question
(Referenced in parent guideline: "How might we increase accessibility, flexibility, and diversity in the sharing of information?")

## Design Options - Complete List (Practical Strategies)

- Incorporate authors representing various identities (gender, race, ability, nationality, socioeconomic background)
- Recognize the range of people, cultures, and histories contributing to current understanding
- Attend to how people and cultures are portrayed in materials
- Challenge stereotypical or harmful portrayals
- Seek authentic, complex portrayals of people, cultures, histories, and world views
- Facilitate listening to diverse perspectives

## Accessibility & Inclusion Notes

This consideration emphasizes representation and inclusive design rather than assistive technology, addressing systemic barriers through curriculum content design and authentic representation of diverse identities and perspectives.
