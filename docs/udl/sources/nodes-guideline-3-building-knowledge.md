# Guideline 3: Building Knowledge

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/

## Official Title
Design Options for Building Knowledge

## Definition and Description

Building usable knowledge requires active engagement: "making connections, synthesizing information, asking questions, selective attending, integrating new information with prior knowledge, strategic categorization, and active memorization."

The process emphasizes that knowledge construction is not passive reception but collaborative meaning-making. "Collaborative environments where students engage in shared inquiry and problem-solving foster deeper understanding and collective knowledge advancement."

Decades of cognitive science research have demonstrated that capability to create meaning depends upon "active skills" and integration with existing knowledge structures, not passive information reception.

## Guiding Question
"How might we incorporate multiple ways for building individual and collective knowledge?"

## Design Considerations (Four Total)

1. **Consideration 3.1:** Connect prior knowledge to new learning — Build connections to prior understandings and experiences.

2. **Consideration 3.2:** Highlight and explore patterns, critical features, big ideas, and relationships — Accentuate important information and how it relates to the learning goal.

3. **Consideration 3.3:** Cultivate multiple ways of knowing and making meaning — Honor multiple and diverse ways of developing understandings of the world.

4. **Consideration 3.4:** Maximize transfer and generalization — Apply learning to new contexts.

# Consideration 3.1: Connect Prior Knowledge to New Learning

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/prior-knowledge/

## Official Title
Connect prior knowledge to new learning

## Definition and Description

The guideline emphasizes that learners understand information more readily when it activates or provides prerequisite knowledge. Barriers emerge when background knowledge unfamiliar to some learners is essential for integrating new information. Conversely, learners possessing relevant background knowledge may not recognize its applicability. Reducing these barriers requires options supporting learners in linking prior knowledge or accessing prerequisite information.

## Design Options (Strategies)

- Anchor instruction by linking to and activating relevant prior knowledge (e.g., using visual imagery, concept anchoring, or concept mastery routines)
- Use advanced organizers (e.g., KWL methods, concept maps)
- Pre-teach critical prerequisite concepts through demonstration or models
- Bridge concepts with relevant analogies and metaphors
- Make explicit cross-curricular connections (e.g., teaching literacy strategies in the social studies classroom)

## Cognitive Science Concepts

This consideration references **schema theory** implicitly through activating prior knowledge and **memory** concepts through prerequisite knowledge activation and bridging strategies.

# Consideration 3.2: Highlight and Explore Patterns, Critical Features, Big Ideas, and Relationships

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/patterns-features/

## Official Title
Highlight and explore patterns, critical features, big ideas, and relationships

## Definition and Description

One of the most distinguishing differences between expert learners and novices is that experts readily identify what matters most versus what is peripheral. As understanding deepens, learners develop stronger capacity to recognize critical features and their connections to learning objectives, then allocate cognitive resources efficiently by anchoring new information to existing knowledge.

Key principle: "One of the most effective ways to make information more accessible is to use explicit cues or prompts that assist learners in attending to those features that matter most."

## Design Options (Strategies)

- Highlight or emphasize key elements in text, graphics, diagrams, or formulas
- Use outlines, graphic organizers, unit organizer routines, concept organizer routines, and concept mastery routines to emphasize key ideas and relationships
- Use multiple examples and non-examples to emphasize critical features
- Use cues and prompts to draw attention to critical features
- Highlight previously learned skills that can be used to solve unfamiliar problems

## Cognitive Science Concepts

This guidance invokes **schema theory** through references to "integrating valuable information into existing knowledge" and **transfer** through "previously learned skills" applied to novel problems.

# Consideration 3.3: Cultivate Multiple Ways of Knowing and Making Meaning

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/making-meaning/

## Official Title
Cultivate multiple ways of knowing and making meaning

## Definition and Description

"Equitable learning environments guide learners in the process of constructing knowledge by valuing and cultivating multiple ways of knowing and making meaning." The guidance emphasizes that diverse cultural perspectives—including Indigenous knowledge systems emphasizing holistic, interconnected understanding—can enrich Western methodologies. Well-designed materials incorporate multiple knowledge-building approaches: problem solving, storytelling, algorithms, and holistic/linear thinking. Combining these approaches with models, scaffolds, and feedback helps learners apply different strategies effectively.

## Design Options (Strategies)

- Incorporate multiple ways of knowing, including storytelling, kinesthetics, problem solving, and relational learning through interpersonal experiences
- Use explicit prompts for each step in sequential processes to help learners develop logical flow and structure complex tasks
- Use options for organizational methods and approaches (e.g., tables and algorithms for mathematical operations)
- Use interactive models that guide exploration and new understandings
- Introduce graduated scaffolds that support information processing strategies
- Provide multiple entry points to lessons and optional pathways through content
- "Chunk" information into smaller elements, preventing cognitive overload
- Progressively release information (e.g., sequential highlighting)
- Remove unnecessary distractions unless essential to instructional goals

## Cognitive Science Concepts

- Information processing strategies
- Cognitive overload prevention (cognitive load theory)
- Schema building through multiple entry points

# Consideration 3.4: Maximize Transfer and Generalization

**Source:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/representation/building-knowledge/transfer-generalization/

## Official Title
Maximize transfer and generalization

## Definition and Description

"All learners need to be able to generalize and transfer their learning to new contexts. Learners vary in the amount of scaffolding they need for memory and transfer in order to improve their ability to access their prior learning." The framework emphasizes that learning requires multiple representations; without proper support, "information might be learned, but is inaccessible in new situations."

## Design Options (Strategies)

- Use organizational tools like checklists, sticky notes, and electronic reminders
- Prompt mnemonic strategies including "visual imagery, paraphrasing strategies, method of loci"
- Incorporate explicit review and practice of concepts
- Apply templates, graphic organizers, and concept maps for note-taking
- Create scaffolds connecting new information to prior knowledge
- Embed concepts within "familiar ideas and contexts (e.g., use of analogy, metaphor, drama, music, film)"
- Provide "explicit, supported opportunities to generalize learning to new situations"
- Revisit key ideas and their interconnections over extended periods

## Cognitive Science Concepts

This consideration directly addresses **transfer** and **generalization** as core concepts. It emphasizes the role of **memory** and **schema** accessibility across contexts, noting that information can be learned but remain inaccessible without proper **mnemonic** and **generalization** support.
