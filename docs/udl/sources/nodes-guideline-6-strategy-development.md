# Guideline 6: Strategy Development

## Guideline 6 Overview
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/

**Official Title:** Design Options for Strategy Development

**Core Description:**
Strategy development addresses "executive functions" through the strategic networks (prefrontal cortex). CAST scaffolds executive skills in two ways: "(1) by scaffolding lower level skills so they require less executive processing; and (2) by scaffolding higher level executive skills and strategies so they are more effective and developed."

**Executive Function Framework:**
CAST frames executive function as enabling learners to become "intentional in their short-term reactions" and "set long-term goals and plan effective strategies," including "monitoring progress and modifying strategies as needed."

## Consideration 6.1: Set Meaningful Goals
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/goals/

**Official Title:** Set meaningful goals

**Full Definition/Description:**
"Setting meaningful goals that are both challenging and motivating is a critical part of the learning process. It is important to set goals that are big enough to capture the vision and complexity of what is being learned, but are specific enough to be clear, objective, and measurable. Articulating a goal helps to develop a plan to achieve it and to identify the tools or resources that will best support the learning along the way. Knowing the goal helps to monitor progress, recognize when to shift strategies or direction, and to give meaningful feedback."

**Design Options (Practical Strategies):**
- Use prompts and scaffolds to estimate effort, resources, and challenge.
- Use models or examples of the process and product of goal-setting.
- Use guides and checklists for scaffolding goal-setting.
- Post goals, objectives, and schedules in an obvious place.

## Consideration 6.2: Anticipate and Plan for Challenges
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/challenges/

**Official Title:** Anticipate and plan for challenges

**Full Definition/Description:**
"Once a goal is set, it is important to create time and space to plan a strategy, identify useful tools, and anticipate the challenges that may surface along the way. This proactive planning can reduce barriers and ensure more individuals have the opportunity to reach their goals."

**Design Options (Practical Strategies):**
- Use reflection prompts to anticipate challenges and encourage strategic planning.
- Use prompts to "show and explain your work" (e.g., portfolio review, art critiques).
- Use checklists and project planning templates for understanding the goal and setting up prioritization, sequences, and schedules of steps.
- Use coaches or mentors that model think-alouds of the process.
- Use guides for breaking long-term goals into reachable short-term objectives.

## Consideration 6.3: Organize Information and Resources
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/organize-information/

**Official Title:** Organize information and resources

**Full Definition/Description:**
Working memory functions like a "scratch pad" for maintaining information chunks needed for knowledge building and problem-solving. When working memory capacity isn't directly relevant to learning objectives, educators should employ various scaffolds and organizational aids to help learners maintain organized information.

**Design Options (Practical Strategies):**
- Use graphic organizers and templates for data collection and organizing information.
- Use prompts for categorizing, systematizing, and discovering themes and patterns.
- Use checklists and guides for note-taking.

## Consideration 6.4: Enhance Capacity for Monitoring Progress
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/monitoring-progress/

**Official Title:** Enhance capacity for monitoring progress

**Full Definition/Description:**
Learning requires feedback, and learners need clarity on progress toward learning goals. The guidelines emphasize generating "multiple and varied forms of feedback throughout the learning process" that are connected to goals and characterized as "explicit, timely, informative, accessible, and customizable." The approach prioritizes formative feedback enabling learners to self-monitor and guide their own effort.

**Design Options (Practical Strategies):**
- Use prompts to guide self-monitoring and reflection.
- Use representations of progress (e.g., before-and-after photos, graphs and charts showing progress over time, process portfolios).
- Explore the different types of feedback that are most useful according to specific preferences, goals, and contexts.
- Use templates that guide self-reflection on quality and completeness.
- Use differentiated models of self-assessment strategies (e.g., role-playing, video reviews, peer feedback).
- Use checklists, rubrics, models, and examples.

## Consideration 6.5: Challenge Exclusionary Practices
**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/strategy-development/exclusionary-practices/

**Official Title:** Challenge exclusionary practices

**Full Definition/Description:**
"Deep-seated biases and systems of discrimination have led to the exclusion of historically marginalized learners across societies and around the globe. It is imperative to create structures to learn from learners' lived experiences in order to surface exclusionary practices–as well as to address them."

**What CAST Defines as Exclusionary Practices:**
Exclusionary practices are rooted in "deep-seated biases and systems of discrimination" that result in the exclusion of marginalized learners.

**Design Options (Practical Strategies):**
- Create time for regular community sessions and individual reflection sessions.
- Work as individuals and as communities to name, explore, and address exclusionary practices.
- Work as individuals and as communities to develop concrete and specific actions for addressing exclusionary practices and building inclusive communities.
- When an exclusionary practice is uncovered, provide opportunities for healing using community-based practices such as restorative justice.

**What CAST Asks Educators to Do:**
Educators should actively identify exclusionary practices through community engagement by learning from learners' lived experiences, surface those practices, address them collectively through naming and exploration, develop concrete and specific actions, and implement restorative approaches such as community-based restorative justice practices to foster healing and build inclusive learning environments.

## Executive Function Framing Across Guideline 6

CAST positions executive function as central to Guideline 6. The framework emphasizes:
- Intentional short-term reactions and long-term goal-setting
- Strategic planning and adaptation
- Progress monitoring and strategy modification
- Two-part scaffolding approach: reducing executive processing load for lower-level skills while developing higher-level executive skills and strategies

Guideline 6.5 represents an expansion of executive function into the socio-emotional and systemic domain, positioning exclusionary-practice awareness and inclusive-community-building as executive capacities educators and learners must develop together.
