# Guideline 5: Expression & Communication

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/

## Official Title
Expression & Communication | CAST UDL Guidelines

## Core Definition and Description

There is no medium of expression that is equally suited for all learners or for all kinds of communication. The guideline advocates providing multiple modalities to reduce barriers and enable learners to express knowledge in diverse ways.

This guideline works toward the overarching goal of developing "learner agency that is purposeful & reflective, resourceful & authentic, strategic & action-oriented."

## Guiding Question

How might we design learning environments that value multiple and novel modes of expression and communication, including those that have been historically deprioritized?

## Four Design Considerations

1. **Consideration 5.1 - Use multiple media for communication**: Express learning flexibly
2. **Consideration 5.2 - Use multiple tools for construction, composition, and creativity**: Share thoughts using tools aligned with learning goals
3. **Consideration 5.3 - Build fluencies with graduated support**: Apply and gradually release scaffolds to support learner agency
4. **Consideration 5.4 - Address biases related to modes of expression**: Honor diverse communication forms

# Consideration 5.1: Use Multiple Media for Communication

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/multiple-media/

## Official Title
Consideration 5.1: Use multiple media for communication

## Core Definition and Description

Unless specific media are essential to learning goals, educators should welcome and encourage a variety of media for expression. This approach accomplishes three objectives:
- Reducing media-specific barriers for learners with disabilities
- Honoring historically undervalued communication forms
- Expanding expression opportunities in our media-rich world

Learners should develop "composition" skills broadly—not just writing—and learn to select "the optimal medium for any particular content of expression and audience."

## Key Principle

The guidance notes that composition encompasses multiple formats and media types beyond traditional text-based writing.

## Design Options (Complete List of Media and Strategies)

- Composing in multiple formats:
  - Text
  - Speech
  - Drawing
  - Illustration
  - Comics
  - Storyboards
  - Design
  - Film
  - Music
  - Storytelling
  - Dance/movement
  - Visual art
  - Sculpture
  - Video
- Using physical manipulatives
- Leveraging social media and interactive web tools
- Solving problems through varied strategies

# Consideration 5.2: Use Multiple Tools for Construction, Composition, and Creativity

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/construction-composition-creativity/

## Official Title
Consideration 5.2: Use multiple tools for construction, composition, and creativity

## Core Definition and Description

Educational environments should provide diverse tool options rather than relying exclusively on traditional approaches. Without being prompted to consider contemporary tools, educators and instructional designers often depend on the tools they have grown accustomed to.

Expanding tool options serves four critical purposes:
1. Prepares learners for future contexts
2. Expands content and teaching methods
3. Broadens learners' ability to express knowledge
4. Enables diverse learners to succeed

## Design Options (Complete List of Tools and Technologies)

- Spell checkers and grammar checkers
- Word-prediction software
- Speech-to-text and voice recognition tools
- Calculators and graphing technology
- Sentence starters and organizational tools
- Concept mapping and outlining applications
- CAD (Computer-Aided Design)
- Music notation software
- Mathematical software
- Virtual and concrete manipulatives
- Web-based collaborative applications
- Web-based presentation applications

## Contemporary Considerations

The page acknowledges generative AI as "a necessary consideration when designing learning experiences around composition," reflecting contemporary educational contexts.

# Consideration 5.3: Build Fluencies with Graduated Support for Practice and Performance

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/fluencies-practice-performance/

## Official Title
Consideration 5.3: Build fluencies with graduated support for practice and performance

## Core Definition and Description

Learners developing various fluencies (visual, audio, mathematical, reading, etc.) benefit from multiple scaffolds during exploration, experimentation, practice, and confidence-building. The approach prioritizes the learning process itself rather than solely the end product.

**Key Concept:** Learning environments can support the development of fluencies by embedding a variety of formative assessment opportunities as well as scaffolds that can be gradually released over time.

## Discussion of Scaffolding and Graduated Support

The guideline specifically emphasizes that performance (essays, dramatic productions, etc.) serves as a meaningful way to synthesize and share learning while building fluencies. The critical mechanism is the **gradual release of scaffolds with increasing independence and skills**, embedded into digital software where possible.

This graduated approach moves learners from dependence on supports toward autonomous fluency development.

## Design Options (Complete List)

- Use differentiated models to emulate (demonstrating identical outcomes via differing approaches, strategies, skills)
- Use differentiated mentors (teachers/tutors employing different motivational and feedback approaches)
- Use scaffolds gradually released with increasing independence and skills (embedded into digital software)
- Use differentiated feedback (customizable and accessible to individual learners)
- Use multiple examples of novel solutions to authentic problems

# Consideration 5.4: Address Biases Related to Modes of Expression and Communication

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/expression-communication/bias/

## Official Title
Consideration 5.4: Address biases related to modes of expression and communication

## Core Definition and Description

Individual and systemic biases devalue certain forms of expression and communication and prioritize others. 

**Specific examples of biases:**
- Text-based forms of communication are often considered more rigorous than alternatives
- Oral traditions have historically been silenced or ignored despite their importance to Indigenous knowledge transmission

## Discussion of Historically Deprioritized Modes

The page identifies that certain modes of expression have been systematically undervalued in educational contexts, including oral traditions and non-text-based communication forms. Honoring and valuing multiple forms of expression and communication is critical to designing inclusive and equitable learning environments.

## Guiding Questions (Three Action Items)

1. Anticipate and explore how bias influences which modes of expression are offered
2. Anticipate and explore how bias influences selection of modes
3. Communicate in multiple ways that aligned modes are equally valued

## Design Options

Rather than providing a traditional bullet-point list of design options, this consideration directs readers to research supporting the consideration and references related considerations (5.1, 5.2, 5.3) for specific strategies.

The emphasis is on critical reflection: educators must examine their own biases and systemic patterns in how they design learning environments and value different communication modes.
