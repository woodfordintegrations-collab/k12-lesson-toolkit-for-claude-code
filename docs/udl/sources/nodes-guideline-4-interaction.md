# Guideline 4: Interaction

## Official Title
**Guideline 4: Design Options for Interaction**

## Core Description

Traditional print textbooks and educational software often restrict navigation and interaction methods (page turning, mouse/keyboard use), creating barriers for learners with physical disabilities, blindness, dysgraphia, or executive functioning support needs. The framework emphasizes designing materials and physical environments that accommodate diverse interaction preferences and ensure "a seamless interface with common assistive and accessible technologies."

## Guiding Question

**"How might we design materials and physical environments that ensure access and participation for every learner?"**

## Considerations (Two Considerations under Guideline 4)

### Consideration 4.1: "Vary and honor the methods for response, navigation, and movement"
- Interact with tools and environments that make learning physically accessible

### Consideration 4.2: "Optimize access to accessible materials and assistive and accessible technologies and tools"
- Open doors to learning with accessible tools and devices

## Consideration 4.1: Vary and honor the methods for response, navigation, and movement

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/interaction/response-navigation-movement/

### Official Title
"Vary and honor the methods for response, navigation, and movement"

### Definition & Core Description

"Learners differ widely in the ways they navigate and move through the physical environment, information, and activities. To reduce barriers to learning, it is essential to embed flexibility in the means for response, navigation, and movement."

The guideline emphasizes the importance of "reflect[ing] on biases and assumptions regarding these different means; it is important to honor the multiplicity of ways to engage and embody the learning process."

### Design Options & Practical Strategies (Four Primary Approaches)

1. "Embed flexibility in the requirements for rate, timing, speed, and range of motor action required to interact with instructional materials, physical manipulatives, and technologies."

2. "Offer options for physically responding or indicating selections (e.g., alternatives to marking with pen and pencil, alternatives to mouse control)."

3. "Offer options for physically interacting with materials by hand, voice, single switch, joystick, keyboard, or adapted keyboard."

4. "Embed flexibility into the design of the physical space (e.g., flexible seating and positioning, lighting, etc.)"

### Input Methods & Technologies Referenced

- Voice control
- Single switch activation
- Joystick
- Keyboard and adapted keyboard
- Hand-based interaction

### Related Guideline Connection

Consideration 4.2 addresses "Optimize access to accessible materials and assistive and accessible technologies and tools."

## Consideration 4.2: Optimize access to accessible materials and assistive and accessible technologies and tools

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/action-expression/interaction/assistive-technologies/

### Official Title
"Optimize access to accessible materials and assistive and accessible technologies and tools"

### Category
Action & Expression > Interaction (Guideline 4)

### Definition & Core Description

Accessible materials and assistive technologies strengthen learner access, participation, and progress. A key principle: many learners with disabilities rely on assistive technologies for regular navigation, interaction, and composition. Providing tools alone is insufficient—learners require support in using them effectively. The guidance emphasizes that accessibility features are increasingly built into commonly-owned devices and that authoring tools now often include accessibility options at point-of-creation rather than requiring external acquisition.

Critical requirement: instructional technologies must remain accessible to avoid creating barriers to assistive technology use, while maintaining appropriate learning challenge levels.

### Design Options & Practical Strategies

- Ensure navigation and interaction can be performed with a variety of tools, including keyboard, mouse, switch devices, and voice commands.
- Offer the ability to leverage alternate keyboard commands for mouse action.
- Use access to alternative keyboards (e.g., on-screen keyboards for touchscreens).
- Customize overlays for touch screens and keyboards.
- Select software that works seamlessly with keyboard alternatives.

### Named Assistive Technologies & Input Methods

- Keyboard navigation
- Mouse devices
- Switch devices (including single switch)
- Voice commands
- On-screen keyboards
- Touchscreen keyboards
- Touch screen overlays
- Expanded keyboards
- Voice-activated switches

### Physical Environment Design Elements

- Flexible seating and positioning
- Variable whiteboard heights
- Accessible aisle widths
- Adjustable lighting

## Cross-Cutting Technical Accessibility Standards & Frameworks

Based on the complete Guideline 4 content, the following accessibility frameworks and standards are referenced or implicit:

- **Assistive Technology Compatibility**: All instructional materials must work with screen readers, switch devices, voice input, keyboard navigation, and on-screen keyboards
- **Keyboard Accessibility**: Full navigation and control via keyboard required; no mouse-only interactions
- **Alternative Input Methods**: Support for voice commands, joystick input, single-switch activation, adapted keyboards
- **Physical Accessibility**: Flexible space design, variable heights, accessible seating options
