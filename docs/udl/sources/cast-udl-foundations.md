# UDL Conceptual Foundations

## 1. LEARNER VARIABILITY

**Official CAST Definition:** "The average brain — and by extension, the average learner — does not exist."

CAST frames variability as a fundamental, predictable characteristic: "Individual variability is the norm in learning environments, and learners differ from one another and an individual learner differs over time depending on the task and context." This is grounded in research findings that show learners are "highly variable" in measurable ways.

**Origin:** Grounded in foundational educational research on individual differences; CAST positions variability as the most "widely replicated finding in educational research."

**"Jagged Learning Profile" Concept:** While this specific term is widely used in education and disability advocacy, **it does not appear in CAST's official materials as a formal concept.** This is **folk-UDL** — the term is used by practitioners to describe the idea that learners have peaks and valleys in ability across domains, but CAST does not use this specific framing in their published definitions.

**"Designing for the Edges" / "Curb-Cut Effect":** CAST endorses the curb-cut principle (that designing for the most disabled benefits everyone), but the specific phrase "designing for the edges" does not appear in CAST's foundational definitions. It is attributed to CAST's influence in education practice but is **folk-UDL terminology.** The architecture lineage is clear (Ron Mace's work), but CAST's own documents reference "design to the edges" as an outcome concept, not a formal principle.

## 2. THE ORIGIN IN ARCHITECTURE

**Ron Mace and Universal Design (1980s):** Ronald Lawrence Mace (1942–1998), an architect and wheelchair user, coined the term "universal design," defined as "the design of products and environments to be usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without the need for adaptation or specialized design." Mace founded the Center for Universal Design at North Carolina State University in 1989. The seven principles of universal design were developed in 1997 by Mace's working group.

**CAST's Adaptation (1984 onward):** Dr. David Rose and Dr. Anne Meyer founded CAST in 1984, initially under the name Center for Applied Special Technology. By 1990, CAST's goal shifted to address "the disabilities of schools" instead of "the disabilities of the individual," explicitly mirroring Mace's architectural philosophy. Meyer and Rose are the principal architects of the UDL framework.

**The Critical Disanalogy:** CAST's own documents do NOT explicitly state the distinction between architecture (one physical artifact designed accessibly for all) and UDL (providing multiple flexible options and means to reach the same goal). However, the difference is implicit in CAST's framework:
- Architecture: One well-designed building serves everyone.
- UDL: Multiple pathways, materials, methods, and assessments all lead to the same rigorous learning goal.

This distinction is critical but **not formally articulated as doctrine in CAST's public materials.** It is more a matter of how UDL is practiced (flexible options) versus how universal design operates in physical space (one design for all).

## 3. THE THREE BRAIN NETWORKS

**Official CAST Framing (Current — Guidelines 3.0, July 2024):**

CAST's current definition states: "The three basic principles are built upon the knowledge that our learning brains are composed of three different networks, recognition, strategic, and affective."

**Network Definitions:**
1. **Affective Networks** — Located in the center of the brain; "evaluate environments and drive motivation and engagement." Align with the principle of Engagement (the "WHY" of learning).
2. **Recognition Networks** — Located in posterior/back regions; "sense and perceive information, transforming it into usable knowledge." Align with Representation (the "WHAT" of learning).
3. **Strategic Networks** — Located in anterior/front regions; "plan, organize, and initiate purposeful actions." Align with Action & Expression (the "HOW" of learning).

**Neuroscience Framing — Current Status:**

CAST **continues to lead with the three brain networks as foundational.** However, Guidelines 3.0 (July 2024) "builds upon, updates, and expands the previous research base to include a greater diversity of perspectives and research from the areas of **sociocultural perspectives on learning, disability studies, and asset-based pedagogies.**" This broadening does not abandon neuroscience but situates it within a larger research landscape.

**Important:** This is a **softening of the neuroscience-first framing**, not its removal. Neuroscience remains central but is no longer the sole foundation. Earlier UDL materials (2011 and earlier) led more exclusively with brain networks; current versions acknowledge variability, culture, and systems of exclusion alongside neurobiology.

## 4. FIRM GOALS, FLEXIBLE MEANS

**Official CAST Principle:** The separation of learning goals from the means of achieving them is a core UDL tenet.

**Definition:** "The goal is achieved by keen focus on the goal, as distinct from the means, enabling the provision of supports and scaffolds for construct irrelevant items."

**Construct-Irrelevant Barriers:** A "construct" is the knowledge, skill, or ability being assessed. "Construct-irrelevant features in assessments can create barriers that prevent some learners from accurately demonstrating what they have learned." UDL reduces or removes these barriers by broadening means while keeping goals firm and rigorous.

**Example:** A mathematics assessment should measure mathematics problem-solving (the goal), not reading proficiency (a construct-irrelevant barrier). Multiple ways to present the problem, respond to it, or demonstrate understanding keep the goal firm while making means flexible.

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/engagement/effort-persistence/meaning-purpose-goals/

## 5. BARRIERS AND DISABILITY AS MISMATCH

**Official CAST Doctrine:** "UDL aims to change the design of the environment rather than to situate the problem as a perceived deficit within the learner."

**The Reframing:** Barriers to learning exist in curriculum design and educational environments, not in learners. CAST states explicitly: "Curricula, rather than learners, are disabled." This represents a fundamental shift from the medical model (disability as individual pathology) to an ecological/environmental model (disability as mismatch between learner variability and inflexible design).

**Guidelines 3.0 Expansion (2024):** Version 3.0 extends this to explicitly address "barriers rooted in biases and systems of exclusion for learners with and without disabilities," emphasizing that barriers can be cultural, representational, and systemic, not merely instructional.

**Official Statement:** "The environment in determining who is or who is not considered 'disabled' plays the central role."

**URL:** https://www.cast.org/resources/about-universal-design-for-learning/

## 6. THE PLUS-ONE APPROACH

**Status: Not Official CAST Doctrine**

The term "plus-one approach" **does not appear in CAST's official published materials.** This is **folk-UDL terminology** that circulates in practitioner communities but is not an officially named CAST strategy.

**What CAST Does Offer (Official Incremental Approach):**

Rather than "plus-one," CAST provides the **UDL Schoolwide Implementation Criteria (UDL-SIC)** with explicit support for incremental adoption:

- **Self-Assessment Tool:** Helps schools map current practices and identify next steps.
- **Goal-Setting and Planning Tool:** Guides incremental goal-setting and action planning.
- **Readiness Indicators:** Support phased implementation starting from current capacity.
- **Data-Driven, Iterative Process:** Implementation follows "data-driven, iterative processes" focused on continuous improvement.

CAST explicitly states: "Whether your team is just getting started or has experienced many years of successful UDL implementation," the criteria support progression. Implementation timelines typically span 2–4 years for substantial schoolwide change.

**Certification Levels:** Schools can progress through Silver, Gold, and Platinum certification levels.

## 7. RETROFITTING VS. DESIGNING UP FRONT

**Official CAST Position:** UDL strongly favors proactive design from the beginning.

**CAST's Statement:** "Universal Design for Learning refers to a process by which a curriculum (including goals, methods, materials, and assessments) is **intentionally and systematically designed from the beginning** to address individual differences. With curricula designed with UDL principles, the difficulties and expenses of subsequent 'retrofitting' and adaptation of 'disabled' curricula can be reduced or eliminated."

**Proactive Philosophy:** CAST emphasizes that "we can and should **proactively anticipate and plan for differences in learner variability.**" This involves "setting clear, rigorous goals; anticipating barriers; and proactively designing to minimize those barriers."

**Reactive vs. Proactive Tools:** CAST acknowledges both exist, but proactive design is preferred. The organization states: "By integrating learning science (UDL) with personalization tools, we gain the power to **design experiences and tools that support all learners from the beginning**, rather than spending time and money retrofitting solutions later."

**Important Caveat:** While CAST prefers proactive design, **retrofitting existing curricula using UDL principles is possible and does occur in practice.** The preference is not a prohibition; it is a matter of efficiency and effectiveness.

**URL:** https://udlguidelines.cast.org/more/frequently-asked-questions/

## 8. FEDERAL STATUTORY DEFINITION OF UDL

**Location:** 20 U.S.C. § 1003 (Additional Definitions), Higher Education Act of 1965, Section 103

**Original Statute:** Higher Education Opportunity Act (HEOA) of 2008

**Exact Statutory Text:**

> "Universal Design for Learning (UDL) means a scientifically valid framework for guiding educational practice that—(A) provides flexibility in the ways information is presented, in the ways students respond or demonstrate knowledge and skills, and in the ways students are engaged; and (B) reduces barriers in instruction, provides appropriate accommodations, supports, and challenges, and maintains high achievement expectations for all students, including students with disabilities and students who are limited English proficient."

**Federal Adoption in K-12 Law:** The Every Student Succeeds Act (ESSA), passed in December 2015, adopted this definition for K-12 education. ESSA Section 1111(b)(2)(B)(xiii) requires that assessments be developed "to the extent practicable, using the principles of universal design for learning." ESSA also requires states to describe steps taken to incorporate UDL in alternate assessments.

**Subsequent Federal References:**
- National Education Technology Plan (2010, 2016, 2024)
- U.S. Department of Education Ed Tech Developer's Guide (2015)
- Strengthening Career and Technical Education for the 21st Century Act (2018) — adopted ESSA's UDL definition
- Senate Resolution 723 (June 2024) — acknowledged UDL's transformative impact

**Citation:** 20 U.S.C. § 1003; 20 U.S.C. § 1001 et seq. (HEOA); 20 U.S.C. § 6301 et seq. (ESSA Title I)

## Summary: Official Doctrine vs. Folk-UDL

| Concept | Status | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Learner Variability | Official CAST Doctrine | Core to all UDL; highly variable and predictable |
| Jagged Learning Profile | Folk-UDL | Term used in practice; not in CAST formal definitions |
| Design for the Edges | Folk-UDL | Principle present in practice; not formal CAST terminology |
| Ron Mace / Architecture Origin | Official — with caveat | Clear historical inspiration; disanalogy between architecture and UDL not formally stated |
| Three Brain Networks | Official CAST Doctrine | Current as of 3.0 (2024); neuroscience broadened but not abandoned |
| Firm Goals, Flexible Means | Official CAST Doctrine | Core principle; separate goal from means |
| Barriers / Curriculum Disabled | Official CAST Doctrine | Central to UDL; reframes disability as mismatch |
| Plus-One Approach | Folk-UDL | CAST offers incremental implementation but not under this name |
| Retrofitting vs. Proactive | Official CAST Doctrine | Proactive preferred; retrofitting possible but less efficient |
